This lesson builds your permanent daily practice routine and maps out where to go next. What separates singers who improve from singers who stall is not talent but the quality and consistency of their practice. This lesson gives you a repeatable session structure, a weekly plan, and a clear list of next steps, so you never again sit down wondering what to do.

The session structure has four blocks in about twenty minutes. Block one is warm-up, five minutes: the body warm-up, hums, lip trills, and sirens from Lesson 4, every single session without exception. Block two is technique, five minutes: rotate daily through pitch matching, interval singing, vowel ladders, and range slides, working on whatever is weakest. Block three is songs, ten minutes: your current song, worked phrase by phrase with the breath map, never just sung through on autopilot. Block four is fun, five minutes: sing anything you enjoy, even imperfectly, and end every session on a song you love. The order is fixed: warm-up, technique, songs, fun.

The weekly plan spreads the work. Practice five or six days a week rather than cramming: twenty focused minutes beats a two-hour weekend marathon because your brain consolidates the learning between sessions, while you sleep. Assign each day a small focus - Monday: pitch and ear training; Tuesday: vowels and diction; Wednesday: range slides and sirens; Thursday: harmony with a partner or recording; Friday: song work, recorded on your phone; weekend: one light session or rest. Keep the written practice log from your materials: date, minutes, what you worked on, and one honest note about what improved. Review the log every Sunday and set one specific goal for the coming week.

Where to go next: five directions. One, a second song in a different style - a slower ballad, where the challenge is sustaining long phrases and singing quietly with control. Two, a wider range: gentle slides that expand your comfortable range by one note at a time, over months, never by forcing. Three, a new time feel: learn to sing a song in 3/4 time, where the phrases breathe in threes instead of fours. Four, a harmony song with a partner: choose a song with a written harmony part and learn it together, trading melody and harmony between verses. Five, reading music: learn the treble clef and note values, so you can learn songs from written music instead of by ear alone. Learn these one at a time, one per few weeks, with the section method from Lesson 6.

Once a month, hold a review session instead of a normal practice. Sing through everything you have learned - your warm-up, your intervals, your vowels, and your songs - and record it all. Listen back with your practice log open and ask three questions: what is clearly better than last month, what has not moved at all, and what is the single weakest skill right now. Write the answers down, then set exactly one goal for the coming month, such as "match ten notes in a row without a miss" or "sing my song through with the breath map and no extra breaths." One goal keeps your practice focused; five goals means none of them happen. Date the goal in your log, and check it off the day you achieve it.

Two common mistakes. The first is practicing only what you already sing well: it feels good and improves nothing. Spend at least half of every session on the hardest thing, not the favorite thing. The second is the autopilot run-through: singing a song start to finish five times while thinking about dinner. If your mind wanders, stop, pick the weakest phrase, and drill it ten times - focused repetitions beat mindless ones by a wide margin. And remember the safety rule that runs through this whole course: if your voice feels tired, hoarse, or sore, stop and rest. A rested voice comes back stronger; a pushed voice does not.

Practice: Write out your weekly plan using the four-block session structure and the day-by-day focus above. Do one full session today, fill in the practice log honestly, and record your song from Lesson 6. Listen back, write down the single weakest spot, and make it tomorrow's technique block.
