This lesson teaches you to sing a complete song from start to finish: learning it in sections, mapping your breaths, and putting the sections together into a performance you can be proud of. Singing a whole song is a milestone - it is the moment all the separate skills in this course become music. By the end of this lesson you will sing a simple song cleanly, in tune, with clear words and planned breathing.

Choose a simple song that fits your range. Pick one with a narrow span of notes - no big jumps, no notes at the very edge of your range - and with words you understand and like. A short folk song, a simple hymn, or a well-known children's melody all work well. Before you sing it, learn the melody by ear: play it on the keyboard or listen to it carefully, humming along until you can hum the whole tune without help. Then learn the words separately: speak them aloud slowly, as if you were reading a poem, noticing where the natural pauses fall. Melody and words learned separately are much easier to combine than melody and words learned together.

Now build the song in sections. Divide it into phrases - short musical sentences, usually two to four measures long - and learn one phrase at a time. Sing the first phrase slowly, checking each note against the melody you know, and repeat it until it is comfortable. Then learn the second phrase the same way. Then sing phrases one and two together. Add one phrase at a time until the whole song is joined. This section-by-section method works on every song you will ever learn, and it stops the most common practice trap: singing the whole song badly from the start and never fixing the weak spots.

Next, map your breaths. A breath map is simply a plan for where you breathe: mark a breath mark in the words at the end of each phrase, and practice breathing exactly there - nowhere else. Breathe in low and quickly at each mark, and sing through the whole phrase on one breath. If a phrase is too long for one breath, split it at a natural pause in the words and add a breath there. Practice the song with the breath map three times, breathing only at the marks. Planned breathing makes the singing sound calm and controlled; random gasps in the middle of a phrase make it sound panicked. Write the breath marks on your song worksheet so you can see them every time you practice.

Add phrasing and dynamics. A phrase is a musical sentence, and like a spoken sentence it has a shape: it begins gently, grows slightly toward the middle, and settles at the end. Sing each phrase with that shape - start at a comfortable medium volume, let the sound grow a little toward the most important word in the phrase, and ease back as the phrase ends. The most important word is usually the word that carries the meaning of the line - sing it with a touch more warmth and clarity, and let the other words support it. Practice the song once with exaggerated phrasing - big swells and clear phrase endings - then once with natural phrasing, and notice how the exaggerated version teaches your voice the shape that the natural version keeps.

Two common mistakes. The first is rushing the tempo: beginners speed up whenever the easy part arrives, and the song falls apart at the hard part. Choose a tempo slow enough for the hardest phrase, and keep it steady from the first note to the last - use a gentle tap of your foot to hold the beat. The second is stopping at every mistake and restarting from the beginning: this trains you to sing the opening brilliantly and the ending never. When you stumble, keep going, note the bad spot, and drill that spot alone afterward.

Time for drills. First, the phrase builder: learn the song one phrase at a time, singing each phrase three times cleanly before adding the next. Second, the breath-map run: sing the whole song breathing only at the marks, three times through. Third, the phrasing run: sing the song once with exaggerated phrasing and once naturally, recording both and listening for the shape of each phrase.

Practice: Choose a simple song inside your range and learn it phrase by phrase until you can sing it through without stopping. Write its breath map on the song worksheet, then sing the whole song four times: twice slowly with the breath map, once at a comfortable tempo with phrasing, and once recorded. Listen back to the recording and write down the one phrase that needs the most work, then drill that phrase ten times before you finish.
