This lesson gives you your first real social toolkit: greeting people politely, introducing yourself, and surviving conversations when you do not understand something. These phrases are the highest-value sentences in the language because they unlock every interaction — ordering food, asking for help, meeting people. Learn them cold before you worry about grammar, because even a flawless sentence is useless if you cannot open and close a conversation gracefully.

The universal greeting is 你好 (nǐ hǎo, pronounced ní hǎo — remember the tone change from Lesson 1). It works with everyone, everywhere. 您好 (nín hǎo) is the respectful version for elders, officials, or formal settings — 您 (nín) is the polite "you." Time-based greetings: 早上好 (zǎo shang hǎo, good morning), 晚上好 (wǎn shang hǎo, good evening). For introductions: 我叫... (wǒ jiào..., my name is...) — for example, 我叫大卫 (wǒ jiào Dà wèi, my name is David). Ask someone's name with 你叫什么名字? (nǐ jiào shén me míng zi? — what is your name?). Say where you are from: 我是美国人 (wǒ shì Měi guó rén, I am American) or 我从美国来 (wǒ cóng Měi guó lái, I come from America). 很高兴认识你 (hěn gāo xìng rèn shi nǐ) means "nice to meet you." Goodbyes: 再见 (zài jiàn, goodbye — literally "again see"), and the casual 拜拜 (bái bái, bye-bye).

Politeness words matter everywhere. 谢谢 (xiè xie) is thank you. 不客气 (bú kè qi, pronounced with the 不 tone change) is you're welcome — literally "don't be polite." 对不起 (duì bu qǐ) is I'm sorry for a real mistake. 没关系 (méi guān xi) is "it's okay / no problem" — the reply to an apology. 请 (qǐng) means please and softens any request: 请坐 (qǐng zuò, please sit), 请进 (qǐng jìn, please come in). A frequent beginner mistake is overusing 请 the way English overuses "please" — in Mandarin, tone of voice and 谢谢 do most of the politeness work, and 请 is reserved for real requests.

Survival phrases are your safety net — memorize these before anything else. 我听不懂 (wǒ tīng bù dǒng) — I don't understand (literally "I listen-not-understand"). 请说慢一点 (qǐng shuō màn yì diǎn) — please speak a bit slower. 你会说英语吗? (nǐ huì shuō Yīng yǔ ma? — can you speak English?). 洗手间在哪里? (xǐ shǒu jiān zài nǎ lǐ? — where is the restroom?). 救命 (jiù mìng) — help! (emergency). These five phrases will rescue you anywhere Mandarin is spoken, and people respect learners who ask for clarification instead of nodding blankly.

Two cultural notes that prevent real mistakes. First, small talk often starts with 你吃饭了吗? (nǐ chī fàn le ma? — have you eaten?) — it is a greeting showing care, not a literal lunch invitation; answer 吃了 (chī le, I ate) or 还没 (hái méi, not yet) and move on. Second, when someone compliments your Mandarin, the graceful reply is 哪里哪里 (nǎ lǐ nǎ lǐ) — literally "where, where," meaning "you flatter me" — rather than 谢谢, which can sound like you agree you are excellent.

Full dialogue — a first meeting. Read aloud with pinyin and translations:

Wei: 你好! 你叫什么名字? (Nǐ hǎo! Nǐ jiào shén me míng zi?) — Hello! What is your name?
Mei: 你好! 我叫小梅。你呢? (Nǐ hǎo! Wǒ jiào Xiǎo Méi. Nǐ ne?) — Hello! My name is Xiao Mei. And you?
Wei: 我叫大卫。我是美国人。你是中国人吗? (Wǒ jiào Dà wèi. Wǒ shì Měi guó rén. Nǐ shì Zhōng guó rén ma?) — My name is David. I am American. Are you Chinese?
Mei: 是的，我是中国人。很高兴认识你。 (Shì de, wǒ shì Zhōng guó rén. Hěn gāo xìng rèn shi nǐ.) — Yes, I am Chinese. Nice to meet you.
Wei: 我也是。你会说英语吗? (Wǒ yě shì. Nǐ huì shuō Yīng yǔ ma?) — Me too. Can you speak English?
Mei: 会一点。你的中文很好! (Huì yì diǎn. Nǐ de Zhōng wén hěn hǎo!) — A little. Your Chinese is very good!
Wei: 哪里哪里。再见! (Nǎ lǐ nǎ lǐ. Zài jiàn!) — You flatter me. Goodbye!

Practice: Write a self-introduction of five lines (greeting, name, where you are from, one thing about yourself, nice to meet you) and memorize it — this is your personal script for the next month. Then drill the survival phrases: cover the Chinese, read the English, and say the Chinese from memory until you can do all five without peeking. Finally, role-play the dialogue above twice, switching roles, saying every line aloud with correct tones.
