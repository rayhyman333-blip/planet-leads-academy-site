This lesson builds your everyday number vocabulary: counting, telling time, and using days, months, and dates. Numbers are pure utility — prices, phone numbers, addresses, schedules — and telling the time unlocks appointments, opening hours, and travel. Mandarin numbers are beautifully logical once you learn 1–10, so this lesson is built for repetition and out-loud drilling.

Numbers 1–10: 一 (yī, 1), 二 (èr, 2), 三 (sān, 3), 四 (sì, 4), 五 (wǔ, 5), 六 (liù, 6), 七 (qī, 7), 八 (bā, 8), 九 (jiǔ, 9), 十 (shí, 10). From here it is pure math: 十一 (shí yī, 11 — "ten-one"), 十二 (shí èr, 12), 二十 (èr shí, 20 — "two-ten"), 二十一 (èr shí yī, 21), 九十九 (jiǔ shí jiǔ, 99). Hundreds: 一百 (yì bǎi, 100), 三百 (sān bǎi, 300). Thousands: 一千 (yì qiān, 1000). Ten thousand: 一万 (yí wàn, 10000) — Mandarin counts in ten-thousands, so 100000 is 十万 (shí wàn). Phone numbers are read digit by digit, with 一 often read as yāo to avoid confusion: 138... is "yāo sān bā..." A classic beginner mistake is using 二 (èr) before measure words — always switch to 两 (liǎng): 两个人 (liǎng gè rén), 两本书 (liǎng běn shū).

Telling time: add 点 (diǎn) for the hour — 一点 (yì diǎn, 1 o'clock), 三点 (sān diǎn, 3 o'clock), 十二点 (shí èr diǎn, 12 o'clock). Minutes use 分 (fēn): 三点十分 (sān diǎn shí fēn, 3:10), 五点二十五分 (wǔ diǎn èr shí wǔ fēn, 5:25). 半 (bàn) means half: 三点半 (sān diǎn bàn, 3:30). 刻 (kè) means quarter: 三点一刻 (sān diǎn yí kè, 3:15), 差一刻四点 (chà yí kè sì diǎn, a quarter to four). 上午 (shàng wǔ) is morning/AM, 下午 (xià wǔ) is afternoon/PM, 晚上 (wǎn shang) is evening: 下午三点 (xià wǔ sān diǎn, 3 PM). Ask the time with 现在几点? (xiàn zài jǐ diǎn? — what time is it now?). Note 几 (jǐ) is used for small unknown numbers — 几个小时? (jǐ gè xiǎo shí? — how many hours?) — while 多少 (duō shao) is for larger quantities.

Days of the week: 星期一 (xīng qī yī, Monday) through 星期六 (xīng qī liù, Saturday), and 星期天 or 星期日 (xīng qī tiān / xīng qī rì, Sunday). 今天 (jīn tiān) is today, 明天 (míng tiān) is tomorrow, 昨天 (zuó tiān) is yesterday. Months: 一月 (yī yuè, January) through 十二月 (shí èr yuè, December) — simply number plus 月. Dates use 号 (hào) in speech: 五月五号 (wǔ yuè wǔ hào, May 5th); 五号 (wǔ hào) is "the 5th." So "today is Monday, May 5th" is 今天是星期一，五月五号 (jīn tiān shì xīng qī yī, wǔ yuè wǔ hào). The order is always biggest to smallest: year, month, day — 二零二五年五月五号 for May 5, 2025, with each digit read separately.

Money and shopping vocabulary, since numbers live there: 多少钱? (duō shao qián? — how much money?) or 这个多少钱? (zhè ge duō shao qián? — how much is this?). 块 (kuài) is the spoken word for yuan: 三块 (sān kuài, 3 yuan), 五十块 (wǔ shí kuài, 50 yuan). 太贵了 (tài guì le) — too expensive. 便宜一点 (pián yi yì diǎn) — a bit cheaper. 我要这个 (wǒ yào zhè ge) — I want this one. The full question 这个多少钱 is worth memorizing cold — it opens every market transaction.

Dialogue — at a market:

Customer: 你好! 这个多少钱? (Nǐ hǎo! Zhè ge duō shao qián?) — Hello! How much is this?
Vendor: 十块钱一斤。你要几个? (Shí kuài qián yì jīn. Nǐ yào jǐ gè?) — 10 yuan per jin (half kilo). How many do you want?
Customer: 我要三个。那个呢? (Wǒ yào sān gè. Nà ge ne?) — I want three. What about that one?
Vendor: 那个五块钱一个。 (Nà ge wǔ kuài qián yí gè.) — That one is 5 yuan each.
Customer: 太贵了。便宜一点吧。 (Tài guì le. Pián yi yì diǎn ba.) — Too expensive. A bit cheaper?
Vendor: 好吧，四块钱一个。 (Hǎo ba, sì kuài qián yí gè.) — Okay, 4 yuan each.
Customer: 好，我要两个。 (Hǎo, wǒ yào liǎng gè.) — Good, I want two.

Practice: Count aloud from 1 to 100 without stopping, then backwards from 50 to 1. Write today's date, your birthday, and three appointment times in Chinese. Drill the time expressions (点, 分, 半, 刻) until they are automatic. Finally, memorize the market dialogue's price lines and role-play buying three different items, asking 这个多少钱 each time.
