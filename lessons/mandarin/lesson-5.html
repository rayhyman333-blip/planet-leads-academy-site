This lesson turns you into someone who can get answers: how to use every question word, ask for directions, handle a full shopping conversation, and keep small talk going. Up to now you have mostly made statements; questions are what make you independent in a Mandarin-speaking place. The good news: Mandarin questions keep the same word order as statements — you just swap in a question word where the answer goes, or add 吗 at the end.

The core question words: 什么 (shén me, what), 哪里/哪儿 (nǎ lǐ / nǎ r, where), 谁 (shéi, who), 什么时候 (shén me shí hou, when), 怎么 (zěn me, how), 多少 (duō shao, how much/many), 几 (jǐ, how many — small numbers), 为什么 (wèi shén me, why). They sit exactly where the answer would be: 这是什么? (zhè shì shén me? — what is this?), 你去哪里? (nǐ qù nǎ lǐ? — where are you going?), 他是谁? (tā shì shéi? — who is he?), 你什么时候来? (nǐ shén me shí hou lái? — when are you coming?). For yes/no questions, add 吗: 你去吗? (nǐ qù ma? — are you going?) or use the affirmative-negative form: 你去不去? (nǐ qù bú qù? — go or not go?). A classic beginner mistake is moving words around English-style — "Where are you going?" never becomes 哪里你去; keep 你去哪里.

Directions vocabulary is survival-level. ...在哪里? (...zài nǎ lǐ? — where is...?): 火车站在哪里? (huǒ chē zhàn zài nǎ lǐ? — where is the train station?). 怎么去? (zěn me qù? — how do I get there?). 左 (zuǒ, left), 右 (yòu, right), 直走 (zhí zǒu, go straight), 左转 (zuǒ zhuǎn, turn left), 右转 (yòu zhuǎn, turn right), 红绿灯 (hóng lǜ dēng, traffic light), 路口 (lù kǒu, intersection). If you are lost: 我迷路了 (wǒ mí lù le) — I'm lost. The lifesaver follow-up: 你可以在地图上指给我看吗? (nǐ kě yǐ zài dì tú shàng zhǐ gěi wǒ kàn ma? — can you show me on the map?). When someone answers, listen for 往左 (wǎng zuǒ, to the left), 往右 (wǎng yòu, to the right), 一直走 (yì zhí zǒu, keep going straight).

Shopping questions beyond prices: 有...吗? (yǒu...ma? — do you have...?): 有大号的吗? (yǒu dà hào de ma? — do you have a large size?). 我可以试穿吗? (wǒ kě yǐ shì chuān ma? — can I try it on?). 可以刷卡吗? (kě yǐ shuā kǎ ma? — can I pay by card?). 你们几点开门? (nǐ men jǐ diǎn kāi mén? — what time do you open?). Small-talk questions that keep conversations alive: 你是哪里人? (nǐ shì nǎ lǐ rén? — where are you from?), 你做什么工作? (nǐ zuò shén me gōng zuò? — what work do you do?), 你来这里多久了? (nǐ lái zhè lǐ duō jiǔ le? — how long have you been here?), 你喜欢这个城市吗? (nǐ xǐ huan zhè ge chéng shì ma? — do you like this city?), 你有什么推荐? (nǐ yǒu shén me tuī jiàn? — what do you recommend?).

Dialogue 1 — asking directions:

Tourist: 请问，博物馆在哪里? (Qǐng wèn, bó wù guǎn zài nǎ lǐ?) — Excuse me, where is the museum?
Local: 很近。一直走，在红绿灯那儿左转。 (Hěn jìn. Yì zhí zǒu, zài hóng lǜ dēng nà r zuǒ zhuǎn.) — Very close. Go straight, turn left at the traffic light.
Tourist: 走路远吗? (Zǒu lù yuǎn ma?) — Is it far on foot?
Local: 不远，五分钟。在银行和药店中间。 (Bù yuǎn, wǔ fēn zhōng. Zài yín háng hé yào diàn zhōng jiān.) — Not far, five minutes. It's between the bank and the pharmacy.
Tourist: 谢谢! 这条街叫什么? (Xiè xie! Zhè tiáo jiē jiào shén me?) — Thank you! What is this street called?
Local: 这是中山路。 (Zhè shì Zhōng shān lù.) — This is Zhongshan Road.

Dialogue 2 — small talk at a café:

Li: 你是哪里人? (Nǐ shì nǎ lǐ rén?) — Where are you from?
Chen: 我是四川人。你呢? (Wǒ shì Sì chuān rén. Nǐ ne?) — I'm from Sichuan. And you?
Li: 我是本地人。你来这里多久了? (Wǒ shì běn dì rén. Nǐ lái zhè lǐ duō jiǔ le?) — I'm a local. How long have you been here?
Chen: 两个星期。我很喜欢这个城市。 (Liǎng gè xīng qī. Wǒ hěn xǐ huan zhè ge chéng shì.) — Two weeks. I really like this city.
Li: 你有什么推荐? 明天我想去公园。 (Nǐ yǒu shén me tuī jiàn? Míng tiān wǒ xiǎng qù gōng yuán.) — What do you recommend? Tomorrow I want to go to the park.
Chen: 好主意! 我们几点见面? (Hǎo zhǔ yi! Wǒ men jǐ diǎn jiàn miàn?) — Good idea! What time shall we meet?
Li: 上午十点。在这里见。 (Shàng wǔ shí diǎn. Zài zhè lǐ jiàn.) — 10 AM. Meet here.

Practice: Write ten questions — two each for directions, shopping, time, personal info, and opinions — and say each aloud with correct tones. Memorize the direction sequence (直走, 左转, 右转, 红绿灯) and give directions from your front door to the nearest shop, in Mandarin, out loud. Finally, role-play Dialogue 2 with changed details (your city, your time, your plan) until the question patterns feel automatic.
