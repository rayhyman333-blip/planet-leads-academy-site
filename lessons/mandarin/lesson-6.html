This lesson gives you the past tense so you can say what you did, where you went, and what happened. Without a past tense you are stuck in the eternal present; with it, you can tell stories, explain your day, and answer the most common small-talk question in any language: 你昨天做了什么? (nǐ zuó tiān zuò le shén me? — what did you do yesterday?). Mandarin verbs never change form — instead, the particle 了 (le) after a verb marks a completed action.

The core pattern: verb + 了. 我吃了 (wǒ chī le) — I ate. 我去了商店 (wǒ qù le shāng diàn) — I went to the store. 他昨天买了一本书 (tā zuó tiān mǎi le yì běn shū) — he bought a book yesterday. Notice that 了 alone does not always mean past — it marks completion or a new situation. 我累了 (wǒ lèi le) means "I'm tired now" (became tired), not "I was tired." Time words make the past explicit: 昨天 (zuó tiān, yesterday), 前天 (qián tiān, the day before yesterday), 上个星期 (shàng gè xīng qī, last week), 上个月 (shàng gè yuè, last month), 去年 (qù nián, last year), 刚才 (gāng cái, just now). Combine them freely: 我上个星期去了北京 (wǒ shàng gè xīng qī qù le Běi jīng) — I went to Beijing last week.

Negating the past uses 没, never 不. 我昨天没去 (wǒ zuó tiān méi qù) — I didn't go yesterday. 他没吃饭 (tā méi chī fàn) — he didn't eat. When 没 negates a verb, the 了 disappears: 我没买 (wǒ méi mǎi) — I didn't buy — not 我没买了. This is a hard rule and a classic test point: 没 + verb, no 了. The one exception is 没有 + noun for "don't have," which you learned in Lesson 3: 我没有时间 (wǒ méi yǒu shí jiān).

For life experiences — things you have done at least once — use 过 (guo) after the verb: 我去过中国 (wǒ qù guo Zhōng guó) — I have been to China. 你吃过北京烤鸭吗? (nǐ chī guo Běi jīng kǎo yā ma? — have you ever eaten Beijing duck?). The negative is 没...过: 我没去过 (wǒ méi qù guo) — I have never been. 过 covers your whole life up to now, while 了 covers a specific completed event: 我去了中国 (I went to China — a specific trip) vs 我去过中国 (I have been to China — at least once in my life).

Two more past patterns worth knowing. 是...的 (shì...de) emphasizes details of a past event: 我是昨天去的 (wǒ shì zuó tiān qù de) — it was yesterday that I went. And verb + 好 as a result: 我吃好了 (wǒ chī hǎo le) — I've finished eating. You will hear these constantly; recognizing them matters more than producing them at this level.

Two beginner warnings. First, don't sprinkle 了 after every verb — it marks completion, so 我昨天去商店了 sounds odd without an object or context; 我昨天去了商店 is natural. Second, remember the 不/没 split: 不 is for present/future and states (我不去 — I'm not going), 没 is for past actions and 有 (我没去 — I didn't go). Mixing them is the single most common grammar error learners make.

Dialogue — talking about the weekend:

Xiao: 你周末做了什么? (Nǐ zhōu mò zuò le shén me?) — What did you do on the weekend?
Da: 我星期六去了长城。 (Wǒ xīng qī liù qù le Cháng chéng.) — I went to the Great Wall on Saturday.
Xiao: 怎么样? 好玩吗? (Zěn me yàng? Hǎo wán ma?) — How was it? Was it fun?
Da: 太好玩了! 我拍了很多照片，还吃了好吃的东西。 (Tài hǎo wán le! Wǒ pāi le hěn duō zhào piàn, hái chī le hǎo chī de dōng xi.) — So much fun! I took lots of photos and ate delicious food.
Xiao: 真好。我在家看电影了。 (Zhēn hǎo. Wǒ zài jiā kàn diàn yǐng le.) — Wonderful. I watched a movie at home.
Da: 电影好看吗? (Diàn yǐng hǎo kàn ma?) — Was the movie good?
Xiao: 很好看。星期天我去看我父母了。 (Hěn hǎo kàn. Xīng qī tiān wǒ qù kàn wǒ fù mǔ le.) — Very good. On Sunday I went to see my parents.

Practice: Write a paragraph of 8–10 sentences about what you did yesterday, using at least five different verbs with 了 and two sentences with 没. Read it aloud twice with correct tones. Then write three experience sentences with 过 about things you have done in your life. Finally, answer aloud in complete Mandarin sentences: 你昨天吃了什么? 你几点起床的? 你上个周末做了什么? 你去过中国吗?
