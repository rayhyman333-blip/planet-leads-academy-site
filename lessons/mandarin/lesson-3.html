This lesson teaches the sentence patterns behind most Mandarin conversation: 是 (shì) for identity, 有 (yǒu) for possession and existence, measure words that go between numbers and nouns, and the two negation words 不 and 没. Mandarin grammar has no verb conjugations, no tenses on verbs, and no plurals — the complexity lives in word order and these small function words. Master this lesson and you can build thousands of correct sentences.

The basic word order is subject-verb-object, like English: 我吃饭 (wǒ chī fàn) — I eat rice/meal. 我喝水 (wǒ hē shuǐ) — I drink water. 我去商店 (wǒ qù shāng diàn) — I go to the store. Time words go before the verb: 我明天去 (wǒ míng tiān qù) — I go tomorrow. 我昨天去了商店 (wǒ zuó tiān qù le shāng diàn) — I went to the store yesterday (the past marker 了 comes in Lesson 6).

是 (shì) connects two nouns — identity, category, or definition: 我是学生 (wǒ shì xué sheng) — I am a student. 他是医生 (tā shì yī shēng) — he is a doctor. 这是我的书 (zhè shì wǒ de shū) — this is my book. But 是 is NOT used with adjectives — a classic beginner error. "I am tired" is not 我是累; it is 我很累 (wǒ hěn lèi) — literally "I very tired," where 很 (hěn) is a required filler word that often just means the adjective applies, not "very." More examples: 这本书很有意思 (zhè běn shū hěn yǒu yì si) — this book is interesting. 天气很热 (tiān qì hěn rè) — the weather is hot.

有 (yǒu) means "have" and "there is/are": 我有三个孩子 (wǒ yǒu sān gè hái zi) — I have three children. 这里有银行吗? (zhè lǐ yǒu yín háng ma? — is there a bank here?). The negative of 有 is always 没, never 不: 我没有钱 (wǒ méi yǒu qián) — I have no money. This is a hard rule — 没有, never 不有.

Measure words (量词, liàng cí) go between a number and a noun, and every noun needs one. The all-purpose measure word is 个 (gè): 一个人 (yí gè rén, one person), 三个苹果 (sān gè píng guǒ, three apples). Learn these common ones: 本 (běn) for books — 一本书 (yì běn shū); 只 (zhī) for animals — 一只猫 (yì zhī māo, one cat); 张 (zhāng) for flat things — 一张票 (yì zhāng piào, one ticket); 杯 (bēi) for cups — 一杯茶 (yì bēi chá, one cup of tea); 件 (jiàn) for clothes — 一件衣服 (yí jiàn yī fu, one piece of clothing). Use 两 (liǎng) instead of 二 for "two" before a measure word: 两个人 (liǎng gè rén, two people) — saying 二个人 marks you as a beginner instantly.

Negation uses two words with a clean split. 不 (bù) negates present and future: 我不去 (wǒ bú qù) — I'm not going. 我不是学生 (wǒ bú shì xué sheng) — I am not a student. 没 (méi) negates past actions and 有: 我昨天没去 (wǒ zuó tiān méi qù) — I didn't go yesterday. 我没有时间 (wǒ méi yǒu shí jiān) — I have no time. Questions come in two forms. Add 吗 (ma) to a statement: 你去吗? (nǐ qù ma? — are you going?). Or use the affirmative-negative pattern: 你去不去? (nǐ qù bú qù? — go or not go?). Both are equally natural; the second is slightly more casual.

The particle 的 (de) shows possession and connects descriptions to nouns: 我的 (wǒ de) — mine/my. 你的名字 (nǐ de míng zi) — your name. 漂亮的衣服 (piào liang de yī fu) — pretty clothes. And 呢 (ne) makes follow-up questions: 我很好，你呢? (wǒ hěn hǎo, nǐ ne? — I'm fine, and you?). 太...了 (tài...le) means "too...": 太贵了 (tài guì le) — too expensive. 太好了 (tài hǎo le) — great!

Dialogue — coworkers at lunch:

Lin: 你是哪里人? (Nǐ shì nǎ lǐ rén?) — Where are you from?
Fang: 我是北京人。你呢? (Wǒ shì Běi jīng rén. Nǐ ne?) — I'm from Beijing. And you?
Lin: 我是上海人。你有兄弟姐妹吗? (Wǒ shì Shàng hǎi rén. Nǐ yǒu xiōng dì jiě mèi ma?) — I'm from Shanghai. Do you have siblings?
Fang: 我有一个哥哥，没有姐姐。你呢? (Wǒ yǒu yí gè gē ge, méi yǒu jiě jie. Nǐ ne?) — I have one older brother, no older sister. And you?
Lin: 我有两个妹妹。今天的菜很好吃，你觉得呢? (Wǒ yǒu liǎng gè mèi mei. Jīn tiān de cài hěn hǎo chī, nǐ jué de ne?) — I have two younger sisters. Today's food is delicious, what do you think?
Fang: 太好吃了! 我很喜欢中国菜。 (Tài hǎo chī le! Wǒ hěn xǐ huan Zhōng guó cài.) — So delicious! I really like Chinese food.

Practice: Write ten 是 sentences about yourself (identity, profession, origin). Write eight 有 sentences (four positive, four with 没有). Then write six sentences using measure words: two with 个, one with 本, one with 只, one with 张, one with 杯. Say every sentence aloud with correct tones. Finally, translate: "I am not a teacher. I have two cats. Are you going tomorrow?" Check: 我不是老师 (wǒ bú shì lǎo shī)。我有两只猫 (wǒ yǒu liǎng zhī māo)。你明天去吗? (nǐ míng tiān qù ma?)
