This lesson gives you your first real social toolkit: greeting people politely, introducing yourself, and surviving conversations when you do not understand something. These phrases are the highest-value sentences in the language because they unlock every interaction — ordering food, asking for help, meeting people. Learn them cold before you worry about grammar, because even a flawless sentence is useless if you cannot open and close a conversation gracefully.

Start with greetings. Guten Tag (GOO-tun tahk) means good day and works all day. Guten Morgen (GOO-tun MOR-gun) is good morning, Guten Abend (GOO-tun AH-bent) is good evening. Hallo (hah-LOH) is hi — friendly and universal. Tschüss (tchewss) is bye among friends; Auf Wiedersehen (owf VEE-der-zay-un) is the formal goodbye. In southern Germany and Austria you will hear Grüß Gott (grewss got) and Servus (ZEHR-voos) as greetings — recognize them. For introductions: Ich heiße... (ikh HIGH-suh) means 'my name is'. Wie heißen Sie? (vee HIGH-sun zee) asks someone's name formally; Wie heißt du? (vee highst doo) is the informal version. Freut mich (froyt mikh) means pleased to meet you.

Now the small-talk core. Wie geht es Ihnen? (vee gayt ehs EE-nun) is 'how are you' formally; Wie geht's? (vee gayts) is the casual version everyone uses. Common answers: Sehr gut, danke (zayr goot DAHN-kuh — very well, thanks), Es geht (ehs gayt — it's going okay), Nicht so gut (not so good), Ich bin müde (ikh bin MEW-duh — I am tired). Und Ihnen? / Und dir? (oont EE-nun / oont deer) means 'and you?' — short and essential. Bitte (BIT-tuh) is please and you're welcome, danke is thank you, danke schön is thank you very much. A frequent beginner mistake is answering Wie geht's? with a long life story; a short answer plus Und dir? keeps the conversation balanced and gives you twice the practice.

Survival phrases are your safety net — memorize these before anything else. Ich verstehe nicht (ikh fehr-SHTAY-uh nikht) — I don't understand. Können Sie das bitte wiederholen? (KURN-nun zee dahs BIT-tuh vee-der-HOH-lun) — Can you repeat that, please? Sprechen Sie bitte langsamer (SHPREH-khen zee BIT-tuh LAHNG-zah-mer) — Please speak more slowly. Sprechen Sie Englisch? (SHPREH-khen zee EHNG-lish) — Do you speak English? Entschuldigung (ehnt-SHOOL-dee-goong) — excuse me / sorry. Es tut mir leid (ehs toot meer lite) — I'm sorry. Was bedeutet...? (vahs buh-DOY-tet) — What does ... mean? These phrases will rescue you in any German-speaking place, and people respect learners who ask for clarification instead of nodding blankly.

Watch for this social mistake: German runs on the du/Sie distinction. Use Sie with strangers, shopkeepers, elders, and in any formal setting; switch to du only when the other person offers it or with clear peers and friends. Using du with a stranger is not a grammar failure — it is a social one, and it can come across as rude. When in doubt, start with Sie. A second note: Germans value directness and punctuality in a way that surprises some visitors — a straightforward question is polite, and arriving on time matters.

Full dialogue — a first meeting. Read aloud with the translations:

Julia: Guten Tag! Wie heißen Sie? — Good day! What is your name?
Felix: Guten Tag. Ich heiße Felix. Und Sie? — Good day. My name is Felix. And you?
Julia: Ich heiße Julia. Freut mich. — My name is Julia. Pleased to meet you.
Felix: Freut mich, Julia. Woher kommen Sie? — Pleased to meet you, Julia. Where are you from?
Julia: Ich komme aus England. Und Sie? — I come from England. And you?
Felix: Ich komme aus Berlin. Sprechen Sie Deutsch? — I come from Berlin. Do you speak German?
Julia: Ein bisschen. Sprechen Sie bitte langsamer. — A little. Please speak more slowly.
Felix: Natürlich. Wie geht es Ihnen heute? — Of course. How are you today?
Julia: Sehr gut, danke. Und Ihnen? — Very well, thanks. And you?
Felix: Sehr gut. Auf Wiedersehen! — Very well. Goodbye!

Practice: Write a self-introduction of five lines (greeting, name, where you are from, one feeling word, goodbye) and memorize it — this is your personal script for the next month. Then practice the survival phrases: cover the German column, read the English, and say the German from memory until you can do all seven without peeking. Finally, role-play the dialogue above twice, switching roles, saying every line aloud with the umlauts and CH sounds from Lesson 1.
