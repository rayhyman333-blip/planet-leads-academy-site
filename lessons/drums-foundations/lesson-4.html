This lesson teaches you to vary the hi-hat and snare beyond the basic beat: eighth-note hi-hats, open and closed hi-hat sounds, rim clicks, and ghost notes. These are the colors that turn a plain groove into something that sounds like a real drummer. By the end of this lesson you will play the rock beat with eighth-note hi-hats, add open hi-hat accents, and shape the snare with soft ghost notes between the backbeats.

Start with eighth-note hi-hats. Instead of one hi-hat hit per beat, play two: "1-and-2-and-3-and-4-and," with the right hand alternating down and up strokes in a steady stream. The trick is keeping the two hits inside each beat perfectly even - the "and" sits exactly halfway between the beats, not rushed toward the next one. Practice the hi-hat alone first, counting "1-and-2-and-3-and-4-and" out loud for two minutes. Then add the snare on beats two and four and the bass drum on one and three, exactly as in the rock beat. The groove immediately feels busier and more driving, and it is the version of the beat you will use most often when playing along to songs.

Next, the open hi-hat. Your hi-hat pedal controls two cymbals pressed together: with your left foot down, the cymbals are closed and give a short, crisp tick; lift your foot slightly and they open, giving a longer, shimmering wash. An open hi-hat is usually placed on the "and" just before a snare hit, or on the last eighth note of a measure, as an accent that pushes the groove forward. Practice this: play the eighth-note hi-hat pattern, and on the "and" of beat four, lift your left foot to open the hats, then close them again on beat one. The open sound should ring briefly and then be cut off cleanly by the closing cymbals. Repeat the pattern for two minutes, keeping the rest of the hi-hat strokes closed and even.

Now the snare. The backbeat on two and four stays loud, but the spaces between backbeats can hold ghost notes - very soft snare taps, played with a low stick height, that add texture without disturbing the groove. Ghost notes are felt more than heard, like a whisper inside the beat. Practice them on the "and" of beat one and the "and" of beat three: play the full beat with eighth-note hi-hats, keep the backbeats strong, and add a tiny snare tap on those two off-beats. The stick should rise only a finger-width off the head. If the ghost notes sound as loud as the backbeat, lower the stick height until they sit underneath the groove. The other snare color is the rim click: turn your left stick sideways and lay it across the drumhead so the shaft strikes the metal rim, producing a dry wooden click. Rim clicks often replace the backbeat in quieter passages.

Time for drills. First, the eighth-note builder: hi-hat eighth notes alone for one minute, then add snare and bass drum for two minutes, counting "1-and-2-and" throughout. Second, the open-hat accent: play the full beat with an open hi-hat on the "and" of four, four measures in a row, then four measures without it, alternating for three minutes. Third, the ghost-note ladder: play the beat with no ghost notes for four measures, then one ghost note per measure for four, then two ghost notes per measure for four, listening for the texture change at each step.

Two common mistakes. The first is uneven eighth notes: the "and" strokes rush ahead, turning the groove into a gallop. Slow down and count every subdivision out loud until the halves of each beat are truly equal. The second is ghost notes that are too loud: a ghost note that competes with the backbeat is not a ghost note. If in doubt, play it softer - a ghost note you can barely hear is doing its job, while one you can clearly hear is just an extra backbeat in the wrong place.

Practice: Play the rock beat with eighth-note hi-hats, snare backbeats on two and four, and bass drum on one and three, for three minutes. Add one open hi-hat on the "and" of beat four in every second measure, and one ghost note on the "and" of beat one in every measure. Count the subdivisions aloud for the first minute, then silently for the rest. Finish by playing the same beat with rim clicks instead of snare backbeats for one minute, keeping the tempo identical.
