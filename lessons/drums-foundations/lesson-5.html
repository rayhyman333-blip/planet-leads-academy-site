This lesson teaches you bass drum control: clean single strokes, heel-down and heel-up technique, and double strokes that let the bass drum play faster patterns without losing timing. The bass drum is the foundation of the groove - when it is clean and steady, the whole band can lean on it; when it is sloppy, everything above it wobbles. By the end of this lesson you will play single and double bass drum patterns cleanly inside the rock beat.

Start with the single stroke. Sit with your right foot on the pedal and play one bass drum hit per beat, counting "1, 2, 3, 4," with your heel resting on the floor. Press with the ball of your foot, let the beater strike the head, and let it rebound - do not press the beater into the head and hold it there. Each stroke should sound full and round, with a clear gap of silence before the next one. Practice twenty single strokes in a row, listening for even volume and even spacing. If some strokes sound louder than others, check that your foot is returning to the same starting position each time - a foot that drifts higher or lower between strokes produces uneven volume.

Now the two foot positions. Heel-down, which you have been using, keeps the heel on the floor and uses the ankle to press the pedal. It is precise, quiet, and ideal for slow and medium tempos. Heel-up lifts the heel off the floor and uses the whole leg to drive the pedal down, with the ankle adding a final snap. Heel-up gives more power and speed, and it is the position most drummers use for louder playing. Practice both: play one minute heel-down, then one minute heel-up, at the same tempo, and notice the difference in sound and effort. Neither is wrong - the skill is being able to switch between them depending on how loud the music needs to be. For this course, use heel-down for slow practice and heel-up when you need power.

The double stroke is the next step: two bass drum hits inside one beat, counted "1-and." The first hit comes from the leg, and the second comes from a quick ankle snap while the foot is still near the pedal - think of it as one larger motion with a bounce at the end, not two separate stomps. Practice the double slowly: play "1-and" with the bass drum alone, four times per measure, for two minutes, keeping the two hits inside each beat perfectly even. Then place doubles inside the rock beat: keep the hi-hat and snare going, and play the bass drum on beat one as a double instead of a single, so the pattern becomes "bass-bass, snare, bass, snare." The double should feel like a small push, not a stumble - if the second hit lands late, slow the whole beat down until both hits sit inside the beat.

Build a bass drum vocabulary with three patterns. Pattern one is the basic beat: bass on one and three. Pattern two adds a double on beat one: bass-bass on one, bass on three. Pattern three adds an extra bass hit on the "and" of beat three: bass on one, bass on three, bass on the "and" of three - this is the classic push that drives a groove into the next measure. Practice each pattern for two minutes inside the full beat, then cycle through all three, four measures each, for another three minutes. Count every beat and subdivision out loud until the patterns feel automatic.

Two common mistakes. The first is burying the beater: pressing the pedal down and holding it, so the beater stays pressed against the head and the drum cannot ring. After every stroke, let the foot relax back so the beater rebounds. The second is stomping from the knee with a stiff ankle: the motion becomes slow and heavy, and fast patterns are impossible. Keep the ankle loose and let it add the snap - the leg provides the power, the ankle provides the speed.

Time for drills. First, the singles ladder: play single bass drum strokes at a slow metronome tempo for one minute, then raise the tempo one step and repeat, stopping when the strokes start to blur. Second, the double builder: bass drum doubles alone, "1-and" per beat, for two minutes, then doubles on beat one inside the full beat for two minutes. Third, the pattern cycle: patterns one, two, and three in rotation, four measures each, for three minutes.

Practice: Play the rock beat with bass drum pattern three - bass on one, on three, and on the "and" of three - for four minutes at a steady tempo, heel-up, counting subdivisions aloud. Keep a tally of clean measures versus measures where the extra bass hit landed late, and aim for sixteen clean measures in a row. Finish with one minute of heel-down single strokes, slow and quiet, to cool the foot down.
