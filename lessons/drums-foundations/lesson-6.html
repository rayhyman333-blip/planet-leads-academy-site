This lesson teaches you fills: the short bursts of drumming that mark the end of a section and lead the music into the next part. A fill is a small variation, usually played on the toms, that breaks away from the groove for a moment and then lands back on beat one of the next measure. The golden rule of fills is simple: the fill must end exactly on time. A flashy fill that lands late is worse than a simple fill that lands perfectly.

Start with the one-beat fill. Play three measures of the basic rock beat, and in the fourth measure, replace the hi-hat and snare on beat four with four sixteenth-note hits moving around the kit: snare, rack tom, floor tom, floor tom. Count the whole measure as "1, 2, 3, 4-e-and-a," and make sure beat one of the next measure lands exactly on time, with the crash cymbal if you like. Practice the four-measure cycle for three minutes: three measures of groove, one measure with the one-beat fill, and back into the groove without a pause. The fill is only four notes, so there is no excuse for it to disturb the tempo - if it does, slow the whole cycle down.

Now the two-beat fill. This time the fill takes beats three and four of the fourth measure: play the groove for beats one and two, then fill on three and four with eight notes moving down the toms: snare, snare, rack tom, rack tom, floor tom, floor tom, floor tom, crash. Count it as "1, 2, 3-e-and-a, 4-e-and-a," keeping the spacing of the fill notes perfectly even. The two-beat fill gives you room to build a small shape - two notes per drum, descending - and the listener hears a clear run down the kit. Practice the same four-measure cycle for three minutes, alternating the one-beat fill and the two-beat fill so your hands learn to switch between them.

The full-measure fill is the showpiece: the entire fourth measure is a fill, and the groove returns on beat one of the next measure. A reliable full-measure fill is sixteen notes moving around the kit in a pattern you can remember: four on the snare, four on the rack tom, four on the floor tom, and four back on the snare, ending with a crash on beat one of the next measure. Count it as four groups of "1-e-and-a," one group per drum. Play it slowly first - the danger with a full-measure fill is rushing, because sixteen notes feel like a lot to fit into one measure. Set the metronome slow, play the fill alone for two minutes, then put it into the four-measure cycle for three minutes.

Fills also need dynamics: a fill should build. Start the fill at the same volume as the groove and grow slightly louder toward the end, so the crash on beat one feels like an arrival rather than a surprise. Practice this deliberately: play a two-beat fill pianissimo at first, then at normal volume, then with a clear crescendo across the fill. Record yourself and listen back - a fill that grows sounds intentional, while a fill that starts loud and stays loud sounds like an accident. Also practice the reverse: a fill that stays quiet, used when the next section is soft. Dynamics are what separate a drummer who plays fills from a drummer who shapes music.

Two common mistakes. The first is the fill that never ends: beginners keep filling past the bar line, and the groove never comes back. Always know exactly how many beats your fill takes, and count it out loud - "three, four" for a two-beat fill - so the landing is planned, not hoped for. The second is filling every four measures: if every phrase ends with a fill, the fills stop meaning anything. Use fills to mark real section changes - the end of a verse, the start of a chorus - and let the groove breathe in between.

Time for drills. First, the one-beat cycle: four-measure cycles with the one-beat fill, three minutes, counting aloud. Second, the two-beat cycle: the same with the two-beat fill, three minutes. Third, the fill ladder: one-beat fill, two-beat fill, full-measure fill, in rotation, four measures of groove between each, for four minutes.

Practice: Play four-measure cycles for five minutes, using the one-beat fill in the first cycle, the two-beat fill in the second, and the full-measure fill in the third, then repeat the rotation. Every fill must land back on beat one on time - keep a tally, and aim for ten cycles in a row with no late landings. Finish by playing two minutes of groove with no fills at all, proving you can leave space.
