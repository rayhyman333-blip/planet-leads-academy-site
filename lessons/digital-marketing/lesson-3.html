This lesson teaches social media marketing: using social platforms to reach your audience, earn their attention, and build a following that compounds. By the end you will choose the right platforms, set up profiles that convert visitors, create a sustainable posting rhythm, and engage in ways that actually build relationships. Social platforms are where attention already gathers — your job is to show up where your audience already spends time, with material worth their seconds.

Choose platforms by audience, not by hype. Your Lesson 1 audience description tells you where they spend time: visual products skew toward image and video platforms, professional topics toward professional networks, local services toward community groups and local listings. Pick one platform to start — two at most. Beginners who open accounts on six platforms post nowhere consistently, while one platform done well for three months teaches you what works. Research each candidate honestly: search your topic there, look at who posts and who engages, and ask whether your content format (writing, photos, short videos) fits what the platform rewards. The right platform is the overlap of "my audience is here" and "I can sustain this format."

Your profile is a landing page, not a diary. Four elements matter: a clear profile photo or logo, a name people can find by searching, a one-line description stating who you help and how (your Lesson 1 message, compressed), and one link to your website or email signup. That is it — no clever vagueness. When someone discovers one good post, they visit the profile to decide whether to follow; the profile must answer "what will I get if I follow this account?" in five seconds. Pin or feature your best piece of content so new visitors immediately see your quality. Revisit the profile quarterly as your offer evolves.

Posting rhythm and content mix are the operating system. Aim for a sustainable schedule — three to five posts a week on most platforms beats daily bursts followed by silence. Mix four content types: useful (tips, how-tos, answers — the bulk, about half your posts), engaging (questions, polls, opinions that invite replies), personal (behind-the-scenes, your process, honest lessons learned — builds trust), and promotional (your offer, used sparingly — roughly one post in ten). This ratio matters because platforms show your posts to more people when followers interact, and followers interact with useful and human content, not ads. Write each post to earn one action: a save, a share, a reply, or a click — and say which one plainly when it fits ("save this for later" is a legitimate call to action).

Engagement is half the work and the half most people skip. Reply to every genuine comment in the first months — each reply doubles the conversation and signals to the platform that your post deserves wider distribution. Spend fifteen minutes a day engaging with others' posts in your topic area: thoughtful comments, not "nice post." This is not vanity; it puts your name in front of exactly the audience you defined, and relationships built this way become collaborations, shares, and customers. Track what works with simple notes: which posts got saves, which got replies, which flopped — then make more of what worked. Growth on social platforms is slow for months and then compounds; the accounts that win are the ones still posting in month nine.

Two mistakes to avoid. First, buying followers or engagement: fake numbers destroy your reach because platforms measure the ratio of interaction to audience — ten thousand silent followers means every post "underperforms" and gets shown to fewer real people. Second, treating every platform identically: copy-pasting the same post everywhere ignores that each platform has its own formats, norms, and audience expectations. Adapt each piece to the platform — it takes ten extra minutes and multiplies the result.

Practice: (1) Choose your one starting platform and write one paragraph justifying it from your audience description. (2) Draft your profile: photo concept, searchable name, one-line description, and link destination. (3) Write twelve post drafts — six useful, three engaging, two personal, one promotional — each with its intended action (save, share, reply, click). (4) Schedule them across three weeks on a simple calendar. (5) Write your fifteen-minute daily engagement plan: whose posts you will comment on and what a thoughtful comment looks like versus a throwaway one.
