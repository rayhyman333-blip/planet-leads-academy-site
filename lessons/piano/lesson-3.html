This lesson teaches you five-finger patterns and your first full scale, C major. A scale is the raw material of melody: most tunes you will ever play are built from the notes of a scale, and practicing scales trains your fingers to move in the smooth, even way that good playing requires. By the end of this lesson you will play the C major scale up and down with both hands, one octave, at a steady tempo.

First, finger numbers, because piano music often marks which finger to use: on each hand the thumb is 1, the index finger is 2, the middle finger is 3, the ring finger is 4, and the little finger is 5. A five-finger pattern is exactly what it sounds like - all five fingers resting on five neighboring white keys, played in order. Place your right-hand thumb (finger 1) on middle C and let fingers 2 through 5 fall on D, E, F, and G. Play 1-2-3-4-5-4-3-2-1 slowly, keeping every note even. Repeat with the left hand, where finger 5 (the little finger) starts on the C below middle C.

The C major scale is eight notes: C-D-E-F-G-A-B-C. It uses only white keys, which is why it is the perfect first scale. Going up with the right hand, the fingering is 1-2-3, then the thumb tucks under the hand to play F, and fingers 2-3-4-5 finish the climb: C(1)-D(2)-E(3)-F(1)-G(2)-A(3)-B(4)-C(5). Coming down, reverse it: 5-4-3-2-1-3-2-1, crossing finger 3 over the thumb. The left hand going up is 5-4-3-2-1-3-2-1, and coming down is 1-2-3-1-2-3-4-5. Learn one hand at a time, hands separately, before ever combining them. Play slowly enough that every note sounds clean and connected.

The tricky moment is the thumb tuck: as finger 3 plays E, the thumb must already be moving under the palm toward F, arriving exactly when it is needed - not before, which breaks the line, and not late, which causes a gap. Think of the hand gliding sideways smoothly rather than jumping. Practice the tuck in isolation: play E-F-E-F with fingers 3-1, thumb passing under, twenty times slowly, listening for zero gap between the notes. Then add the surrounding notes until the full octave flows.

Time for drills. First, five-finger patterns: play the C-D-E-F-G pattern up and down with each hand, four times each, at a comfortable tempo. Second, one-octave scales hands separately: right hand up and down, then left hand up and down, three times each, counting "1-2-3-4-5-6-7-8" as you climb. Third, the metronome ladder: set a metronome to a slow pulse, play one note per click, and only raise the speed after three perfect repetitions at the current one. Never raise the tempo to cover up unevenness - fix the unevenness first.

Once the scale is even hands separately, try it hands together. Start both hands on C an octave apart - right thumb on middle C, left little finger on the C below - and climb in parallel motion, both hands ascending together. Keep the same fingerings you learned for each hand; the only new challenge is coordination, so drop the tempo back to slow. Play the hands-together scale up and down four times. Then try contrary motion: both hands start together on middle C and move outward in opposite directions, mirroring each other. Contrary motion is often easier at first because the thumb tucks happen at the same moment in both hands - a satisfying milestone that proves your hands are starting to work as a team.

Two common mistakes. The first is lifting the fingers too high between notes, which wastes motion and breaks the smooth, connected sound scales need. Keep your fingers close to the keys, moving just enough to strike and release. The second is rushing the thumb crossover: the notes before and after the tuck get squeezed or stretched. If your scale sounds lumpy at the crossover, slow down and give that one spot ten extra repetitions until it evens out.

Practice: Play the C major scale one octave, hands separately, up and down, six times through with each hand. Use a metronome at a slow tempo, one note per click, and do not increase the speed until all six repetitions are even and clean. End with the E-F-E-F thumb-tuck drill, twenty slow repetitions with the right hand.
