This lesson turns you into someone who can get answers: how to form every kind of question, ask for directions, handle a full shopping conversation, and keep small talk going. Up to now you have mostly made statements; questions are what make you independent in a Hindi-speaking place. The good news: Hindi questions are simple. You already know the main pattern from the dialogues so far, and this lesson makes it automatic.

Hindi has two main ways to ask a question. 1) Rising intonation: keep normal word order and raise your voice at the end — Aap Hindi bolte hain? (Do you speak Hindi?). This is the most common in speech. 2) Question word in place: put the question word where the answer would go — Aap kahan rehte hain? (Where do you live?), Yeh kitne ka hai? (How much is this?), Aap kab jaa rahe hain? (When are you going?). The verb stays at the end in both patterns, which is the golden rule of Hindi word order. The core question words: kya (what — Yeh kya hai? = What is this?), kaun (who), kaun sa (which), kahan (where), kab (when), kyun (why), kaise (how), kitna/kitne (how much/many). Note that kya at the start of a sentence turns it into a yes/no question — Kya aap Hindi bolte hain? — while kya in the middle asks what: Aap kya karte hain? (What do you do?). Kyunki (because) answers kyun — Kyun? Kyunki main thaka hua hoon (Why? Because I am tired).

Directions vocabulary is survival-level. Kahan hai...? (Where is...?), Main ... kaise jaaoon? (How do I get to...?), Kya yeh paas hai? (Is it near?), Kya yeh door hai? (Is it far?), daayein (to the right), baayein (to the left), seedha (straight ahead), kone par (on the corner), ... ke beech mein (between... and...). When someone answers, listen for: seedha jaaiye (go straight), daayein mudiye (turn right), baayein mudiye (turn left), paar kijiye (cross). Polite opener: Maaf kijiye, station kahan hai? (Excuse me, where is the station?). And the lifesaver follow-up: Kya aap mujhe map par dikha sakte hain? (Can you show me on the map?). Hindi uses postpositions where English uses prepositions — mein (in), par (on), se (from/with), ko (to), tak (until) come after the noun: bazaar mein (in the market), ghar se (from home), Delhi tak (until Delhi).

Shopping questions beyond prices: Kya aap ke paas... hai? (Do you have...?), Kya ... hai? (Is there...? / Are there...?), Kya main pehen kar dekh sakta hoon? (Can I try it on? — masculine) or Kya main pehen kar dekh sakti hoon? (feminine), Kya aap ke paas bada size / chhota size hai? (Do you have a bigger/smaller size?), Kya main card se bhugtan kar sakta hoon? (Can I pay by card?), Aap kitne baje kholte hain? (What time do you open?). Small-talk questions that keep conversations alive: Aap kahan se hain? (Where are you from?), Aap kya karte hain? (What do you do?), Aap yahan kab se hain? (How long have you been here?), Kya aap ko sheher pasand hai? (Do you like the city?), Aap kya salaah denge? (What do you recommend?). Two mistakes to avoid: saying Aap ka naam kya hai? with tum endings by accident (keep aap with hain, tum with ho), and answering a question you only half understood — use Mujhe theek se samajh nahi aaya, kya aap dobara keh sakte hain? instead of guessing.

Dialogue 1 — asking directions:

Yatri: Maaf kijiye, museum kahan hai? — Excuse me, where is the museum?
Raahgir: Yeh paas hai. Seedha jaaiye aur baayein mudiye. — It is near. Go straight and turn left.
Yatri: Kya yeh paidal door hai? — Is it far on foot?
Raahgir: Nahi, paanch minute hai. Yeh bank aur pharmacy ke beech mein hai. — No, it is five minutes. It is between the bank and the pharmacy.
Yatri: Bahut dhanyavaad. Is sadak ka naam kya hai? — Thank you very much. What is this street called?
Raahgir: Yeh MG Road hai. — This is MG Road.

Dialogue 2 — small talk at a café:

Lata: Aap kahan se hain? — Where are you from?
Mark: Main Belgium se hoon. Aur aap? — I am from Belgium. And you?
Lata: Main yahin se hoon, Delhi se. Aap yahan kab se hain? — I am from here, from Delhi. How long have you been here?
Mark: Do hafte se. Mujhe sheher bahut pasand hai. — For two weeks. I like the city a lot.
Lata: Aap kya dekhne ki salaah denge? — What do you recommend seeing?
Mark: Main kal park jaana chahta hoon. Aap kyun nahi aate? — I want to go to the park tomorrow. Why do you not come?
Lata: Achha vichaar hai! Kitne baje? — Good idea! At what time?
Mark: Das baje. Ham yahin milte hain. — At ten. We meet here.

Practice: Write ten questions — two each for directions, shopping, time, personal info, and opinions — and say each aloud with rising intonation. Then memorize the direction-giving sequence (seedha, daayein, baayein, kone par) and give directions from your front door to the nearest shop, in Hindi, out loud. Finally, role-play Dialogue 2 with a partner or alone, then change three details (city, time, place) and run it again — this is how small talk becomes automatic.
