This lesson teaches you to talk about the future — making plans, accepting invitations, and handling two of the most useful real-world conversations: ordering in a restaurant and navigating travel. Future talk is what turns you from a tourist into someone who can actually live in Hindi: booking, inviting, promising, and planning.

Hindi has two easy ways to express the future. The everyday one is the future tense itself: stem + -unga (masculine singular, I), -ungi (feminine singular, I), -ega (masculine singular, he), -egi (feminine singular, she), -enge (plural/formal). Main jaaunga (m, I will go), Main jaaungi (f, I will go), Main khaaunga (m, I will eat), Main khaaungi (f, I will eat), Vah aayega (m, he will come), Vah aayegi (f, she will come), Ham jaayenge (we will go), Aap aayenge (you will come — formal). The second way is the present tense with a future time word, which is very common in casual speech: Kal main Delhi jaata hoon (Tomorrow I go to Delhi) is understood as a future plan. For this level, default to the future endings in conversation and recognize the present-tense-with-future-time pattern when you hear it. Promises use the future tense: Main aap ko kal phone karoonga (m, I will call you tomorrow), Main aap ko kal phone karoongi (f).

Restaurant vocabulary and phrases. Menu (the menu), shuruaat (starter), mukhya khaana (main course), meetha (dessert/sweets), peene ki cheez (drink). Ordering: Mujhe... chahiye (I would like...), Mere liye... (For me...), Aap kya salaah denge? (What do you recommend?). During the meal: Yeh bahut swaadisht hai (It is delicious), Kya aap mujhe... laa sakte hain? (Can you bring me...?), Bill dijiye, kripya (The bill, please). Common mistake: ordering with a bare Mujhe chahiye is fine, but adding kripya (please) and using aap forms with the waiter sounds much better. Tipping: a small tip is appreciated in restaurants, though service charges are sometimes included — check the bill. Indian food to know: roti (flatbread), naan (oven bread), chawal (rice), daal (lentils), sabzi (vegetables), samosa (fried pastry), thali (platter meal), chai (tea), lassi (yogurt drink), mithai (sweets). Vegetarian food is everywhere — Main shakahari hoon (I am vegetarian) is a useful sentence.

Travel phrases. Hawai adda par (at the airport): Gate kahan hai? (Where is the gate?), Meri flight ... baje hai (My flight is at...), Meri booking hai (I have a reservation). Hotel mein: Mujhe ek double kamra chahiye (I would like a double room), Nashta kitne baje hai? (What time is breakfast?), Kya aap ke paas wifi hai? (Do you have wifi?). Problems: Mera passport kho gaya hai (I lost my passport), Mujhe madad chahiye (I need help), Pharmacy kahan hai? (Where is a pharmacy?). At the station: Train kis platform se jaayegi? (From which platform does the train leave?), Delhi ke liye ek ticket dijiye, kripya (A ticket to Delhi, please). Note how much of this reuses Lessons 2–5 — the course is compounding.

Dialogue 1 — at a restaurant:

Waiter: Namaste. Kya aap ki booking hai? — Hello. Do you have a reservation?
Claire: Haan, Claire ke naam par. — Yes, under the name Claire.
Waiter: Is taraf, kripya. Kya aap menu dekhna chahti hain? — This way, please. Would you like to see the menu?
Claire: Haan, dhanyavaad. Aaj ka khaas khaana kya hai? — Yes, thanks. What is the special dish today?
Waiter: Yeh murghi aur chawal hai. Yeh bahut swaadisht hai. — It is chicken and rice. It is delicious.
Claire: Mujhe yeh chahiye, aur ek salad. Aur aap, Jeroen? — I would like that, and a salad. And you, Jeroen?
Jeroen: Mere liye machhli. Kya aap mujhe paani laa sakte hain, kripya? — For me, the fish. Can you bring me water, please?
Waiter: Abhi laata hoon. — Right away.
(baad mein) Claire: Bill dijiye, kripya. — The bill, please.
Waiter: Paintees rupay hue. — That is thirty-five rupees.

Dialogue 2 — making weekend plans:

Nikhil: Aap Shanivaar ko kya karenge? — What will you do on Saturday?
Emma: Main apni behen se milne jaaungi. Aur aap? — I am going to visit my sister. And you?
Nikhil: Main Shanivaar subah bazaar jaaunga. Kya aap aana chahti hain? — I am going to go to the market on Saturday morning. Do you want to come?
Emma: Haan, zaroor. Ham kitne baje milenge? — Yes, gladly. What time shall we meet?
Nikhil: Nau baje. Uske baad ham saath khaana khaayenge. — At nine. Afterwards we will eat lunch together.
Emma: Badhiya. Main aap ko Shukravaar ko phone karoongi. — Perfect. I will call you Friday to confirm.

Practice: Write three plans for next week using the future tense, and three promises using the future tense with a time word. Then write a full restaurant dialogue (8+ lines) where you order a starter, main, drink, and dessert, ask for the bill, and react to the food. Say it all aloud. Finally, plan a real weekend in Hindi: where you will go, who you will see, what you will eat — five sentences minimum, out loud.
