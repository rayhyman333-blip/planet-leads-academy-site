This lesson gives you the past tense so you can say what you did, where you went, and what happened. Without a past tense you are stuck in the eternal present; with it, you can tell stories, explain your day, and answer the most common small-talk question in any language: Aap ne kal kya kiya? (What did you do yesterday?). Hindi past tense has one special feature — the word ne — but once you learn its pattern, the tense is regular and easy to use.

The past tense has two patterns. For intransitive verbs (verbs with no direct object, like go, come, sleep, stay), the formula is: stem + -a (masculine) / -i (feminine) / -e (masculine plural) + tha/thi/the. Main gaya (m, I went), Main gayi (f, I went), Main soya (m, I slept), Main soyi (f, I slept), Vah aaya (m, he came), Vah aayi (f, she came), Ham gaye (we went). For transitive verbs (verbs with a direct object, like eat, drink, see, buy, do), Hindi uses the ergative marker ne after the subject, and the verb agrees with the OBJECT, not the subject: Main ne khaana khaaya (m, I ate food — khaana is masculine so khaaya), Main ne kitaab padhi (f, I read a book — kitaab is feminine so padhi), Usne chai pee (He drank tea — chai is feminine so pee), Main ne film dekhi (f, I watched a movie — film is feminine so dekhi), Ham ne khaana khaaya (We ate food). This ne pattern is the single most important grammar point in Hindi — drill it until it feels natural.

Common past forms to memorize now: kiya (did — karna), khaaya/khaayi (ate — khaana), peeya/peeyi (drank — peena), dekha/dekhi (saw — dekhna), gaya/gayi (went — jaana), aaya/aayi (came — aana), liya/li (took — lena), diya/di (gave — dena), bola/boli (said — bolna), mila/mili (met/found — milna), soya/soyi (slept — sona), khareeda/khareedi (bought — khareedna). Time words anchor the past: kal (yesterday/tomorrow), kal raat (last night), pichhle hafte (last week), pichhle saal (last year), aaj subah (this morning). Note that tha/thi/the is often dropped in casual speech when the time is clear: Main kal bazaar gaya is understood as I went to the market yesterday, though Main kal bazaar gaya tha is the full form.

Negation goes before the verb: Main ne khaana nahi khaaya (I did not eat food), Vah nahi aaya (He did not come). Questions work as in Lesson 5: Aap ne khaana khaaya? / Kya aap ne khaana khaaya? with rising intonation is the everyday form. Two beginner warnings. First, forgetting ne: saying Main khaana khaaya instead of Main ne khaana khaaya is the most common beginner error in Hindi — ne is required with transitive verbs in the past tense. Second, agreeing the verb with the subject instead of the object: Main ne kitaab padha is wrong because kitaab is feminine, so it must be Main ne kitaab padhi. Check two things every time: is there a ne after the subject, and does the verb ending match the object?

Dialogue — talking about the weekend:

Anita: Aap ne is hafte ke ant mein kya kiya? — What did you do this weekend?
Rohan: Main apne parivaar ke saath samudra tat par gaya. — I went to the beach with my family.
Anita: Kaisa tha? Mausam achha tha? — How was it? Was the weather nice?
Rohan: Haan, mausam bahut achha tha. Ham ne tairna kiya aur machhli khaayi. — Yes, the weather was very nice. We swam and ate fish.
Anita: Badhiya! Main ghar par rahi. Main ne film dekhi aur kitaab padhi. — Great! I stayed home. I watched a movie and read a book.
Rohan: Kya aap ko film pasand aayi? — Did you like the movie?
Anita: Haan, mujhe bahut pasand aayi. Aur Ravivaar ko, aap ne kya kiya? — Yes, I loved it. And Sunday, what did you do?
Rohan: Main der tak soya aur apne mata-pita se mila. — I slept in and met my parents.

Practice: Write a paragraph of 8–10 sentences about what you did yesterday, using at least five different past-tense verbs (mix ne verbs and intransitive verbs, masculine and feminine objects). Read it aloud twice. Then conjugate in writing: jaana, khaana, dekhna, karna, peena — full past tense, masculine and feminine, with correct ne and correct object agreement. Finally, answer these aloud in complete Hindi sentences: Aap ne kal kya khaaya? Aap kitne baje uthe? Aap ne pichhle hafte ke ant mein kya kiya? Kya aap ne is hafte film dekhi?
