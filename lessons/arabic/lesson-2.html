This lesson gives you your first real social toolkit: greeting people politely, introducing yourself, and surviving conversations when you do not understand something. These phrases are the highest-value sentences in the language because they unlock every interaction — ordering food, asking for help, meeting people. Learn them cold before you worry about grammar, because even a flawless sentence is useless if you cannot open and close a conversation gracefully.

The universal greeting is السلام عليكم (as-salāmu ʿalaykum) — peace be upon you. It works with everyone, everywhere. The reply is وعليكم السلام (wa ʿalaykum as-salām) — and upon you be peace. مرحبا (marḥaban) is a friendly hello; أهلا وسهلا (ahlan wa sahlan) is a warm welcome — literally "you have come to family and ease." Time-based greetings: صباح الخير (ṣabāḥ al-khayr, good morning), answered with صباح النور (ṣabāḥ an-nūr); مساء الخير (masāʾ al-khayr, good evening), answered with مساء النور (masāʾ an-nūr). For introductions: ما اسمك؟ (mā ismuk? — what is your name? — to a man; mā ismuki? to a woman). أنا اسمي... (anā ismī... — my name is...). من أين أنت؟ (min ayna anta? — where are you from? — to a man; anti to a woman). تشرفنا (tasharrafnā) — pleased to meet you. Goodbyes: مع السلامة (maʿa as-salāmah, goodbye — literally "with safety"), إلى اللقاء (ilā al-liqāʾ, see you later).

Politeness words carry enormous weight. شكرا (shukran) is thank you — add جزيلا (jazīlan) for "thank you very much." عفوا (ʿafwan) is you're welcome. من فضلك (min faḍlik — to a man; min faḍliki — to a woman) means please. آسف (āsif — said by a man; آسفة āsifah — said by a woman) is I'm sorry. A frequent beginner mistake is using the wrong gender form — Arabic addresses men and women differently, so learn both forms of من فضلك and آسف/آسفة from the start.

Survival phrases are your safety net — memorize these before anything else. لا أفهم (lā afham) — I don't understand. تكلم ببطء من فضلك (takallam bi-buṭʾ min faḍlik) — speak slowly, please. هل تتكلم الإنجليزية؟ (hal tatakallam al-inglīziyyah? — do you speak English?). أين الحمام؟ (ayna al-ḥammām? — where is the bathroom?). ساعدني من فضلك (sāʿidnī min faḍlik) — please help me. These five phrases will rescue you anywhere Arabic is spoken, and people respect learners who ask for clarification instead of nodding blankly.

Two cultural notes that prevent real mistakes. First, greetings are longer and warmer than in English — rushing past السلام عليكم to your question feels abrupt; exchange the greeting fully first. Second, the right hand is used for giving, receiving, and eating — offer things with your right hand as a sign of respect.

Full dialogue — a first meeting. Read aloud with transliteration and translations:

Layla: السلام عليكم! ما اسمك؟ (As-salāmu ʿalaykum! Mā ismuk?) — Peace be upon you! What is your name?
Omar: وعليكم السلام! أنا اسمي عمر. وأنت؟ (Wa ʿalaykum as-salām! Anā ismī ʿUmar. Wa ant?) — And upon you be peace! My name is Omar. And you?
Layla: أنا اسمي ليلى. من أين أنت؟ (Anā ismī Laylā. Min ayna ant?) — My name is Layla. Where are you from?
Omar: أنا من مصر. وأنت؟ (Anā min Miṣr. Wa ant?) — I am from Egypt. And you?
Layla: أنا من أمريكا. تشرفنا! (Anā min Amrīkā. Tasharrafnā!) — I am from America. Pleased to meet you!
Omar: تشرفنا! هل تتكلمين الإنجليزية؟ (Tasharrafnā! Hal tatakallamīn al-inglīziyyah?) — Pleased to meet you! Do you (feminine) speak English?
Layla: نعم، قليلا. تكلمي ببطء من فضلك. (Naʿam, qalīlan. Takallamī bi-buṭʾ min faḍlik.) — Yes, a little. Speak slowly, please.
Omar: لا مشكلة. مع السلامة! (Lā mushkilah. Maʿa as-salāmah!) — No problem. Goodbye!

Practice: Write a self-introduction of five lines (greeting, name, where you are from, one thing about yourself, pleased to meet you) and memorize it — this is your personal script for the next month. Then drill the survival phrases: cover the Arabic, read the English, and say the Arabic from memory until you can do all five without peeking. Finally, role-play the dialogue above twice, switching roles, saying every line aloud with the throat sounds from Lesson 1.
