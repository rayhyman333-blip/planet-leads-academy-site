This lesson teaches the sentence patterns behind most Arabic conversation: word order, the definite article ال, masculine and feminine gender, and present-tense verbs. Arabic has a famous feature beginners love: there is no "to be" in the present tense — "I am a student" is simply أنا طالب (anā ṭālib), "I student." Master this lesson and you can build thousands of correct sentences.

Arabic word order is flexible, but beginners should use subject-verb-object like English: أنا أدرس العربية (anā adrusu al-ʿarabiyyah) — I study Arabic. أخي يشرب القهوة (akhī yashrabu al-qahwah) — my brother drinks coffee. Verb-first order (يدرس الطالب — yadrusu aṭ-ṭālibu, studies the student) is also correct and very common, but SVO keeps you safe while learning.

The definite article ال (al-) means "the" and attaches to the front of the word: كتاب (kitāb, a book) → الكتاب (al-kitāb, the book). مدرسة (madrasah, a school) → المدرسة (al-madrasah, the school). Remember the sun letters from Lesson 1: with sun letters the l assimilates — الشمس is pronounced ash-shams (the sun), while moon letters keep al-: القمر al-qamar (the moon). There is no indefinite article — كتاب alone means "a book."

Gender shapes everything. Every noun is masculine or feminine; feminine nouns usually end in ة (tāʾ marbūṭah): مدرسة (school), سيارة (sayyārah, car), طالبة (ṭālibah, female student). Adjectives match the noun's gender: طالب مجتهد (ṭālib mujtahid — a hardworking male student) vs طالبة مجتهدة (ṭālibah mujtahidah — a hardworking female student). Pronouns: أنا (anā, I), أنتَ (anta, you masculine), أنتِ (anti, you feminine), هو (huwa, he), هي (hiya, she), نحن (naḥnu, we). Demonstratives: هذا (hādhā, this masculine), هذه (hādhihi, this feminine), ذلك (dhālika, that masculine), تلك (tilka, that feminine). هذا كتاب (hādhā kitāb — this is a book), هذه مدرسة (hādhihi madrasah — this is a school).

Present-tense verbs use prefixes: أ (a) for I, ت (ta) for you/he-she, ي (ya) for he, ن (na) for we. With the verb درس (darasa, to study): أنا أدرس (anā adrusu — I study), أنتَ تدرس (anta tadrusu — you study), هو يدرس (huwa yadrusu — he studies), هي تدرس (hiya tadrusu — she studies), نحن ندرس (naḥnu nadrusu — we study). Learn one more verb fully — كتب (kataba, to write): أكتب (aktubu), تكتب (taktubu), يكتب (yaktubu), نكتب (naktubu). Two verbs fully conjugated gives you the pattern for hundreds.

Negation: لا (lā) before a present verb — لا أدرس (lā adrusu) — I don't study. For "to be" sentences, use ليس (laysa): لست طالبا (lastu ṭāliban) — I am not a student; ليس كبيرا (laysa kabīran) — it is not big. Questions: add هل (hal) at the start for yes/no — هل تدرس؟ (hal tadrusu? — do you study?) — or use question words in place (Lesson 5).

Two beginner mistakes to avoid. First, inserting a "to be" verb in the present — أنا أكون طالب is wrong; أنا طالب is correct. Save كان (kāna) for the past (Lesson 6). Second, mismatching adjective gender — طالبة مجتهد is wrong; the ة on the noun demands ة on the adjective: طالبة مجتهدة.

Dialogue — coworkers at lunch:

Samir: أين تعمل؟ (Ayna taʿmal?) — Where do you work?
Nadia: أعمل في بنك. أنا موظفة. وأنت؟ (Aʿmalu fī bank. Anā muwaẓẓafah. Wa ant?) — I work in a bank. I am an employee (feminine). And you?
Samir: أنا مدرس. أعمل في مدرسة. (Anā mudarris. Aʿmalu fī madrasah.) — I am a teacher (masculine). I work in a school.
Nadia: هل أنت متعب اليوم؟ (Hal anta mutʿab al-yawm?) — Are you tired today?
Samir: لا، أنا بخير. هل تريدين أن تأكلي معي؟ (Lā, anā bi-khayr. Hal turīdīn an taʾkulī maʿī?) — No, I'm fine. Do you want to eat with me?
Nadia: نعم، أريد. هل نذهب إلى المطعم؟ (Naʿam, urīd. Hal nadhhabu ilā al-maṭʿam?) — Yes, I want to. Shall we go to the restaurant?
Samir: هيا بنا. أنا جائع. (Hayyā binā. Anā jāʾiʿ.) — Let's go. I'm hungry.

Practice: Write ten أنا sentences about yourself (identity, profession, origin) — no verb needed. Write eight present-tense verb sentences using أدرس and أكتب with different pronouns. Then write six noun-adjective pairs with correct gender matching (three masculine, three feminine). Say every sentence aloud. Finally, translate: "She is a doctor. She works in a hospital. She is tired today. She wants coffee." Check: هي طبيبة (hiya ṭabībah). تعمل في مستشفى (taʿmalu fī mustashfā). هي متعبة اليوم (hiya mutʿabah al-yawm). تريد قهوة (turīdu qahwah).
