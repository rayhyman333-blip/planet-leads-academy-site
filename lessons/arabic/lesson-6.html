This lesson gives you the past tense so you can say what you did, where you went, and what happened. Without a past tense you are stuck in the eternal present; with it, you can tell stories, explain your day, and answer the most common small-talk question in any language: ماذا فعلت أمس؟ (mādhā faʿalta amsi? — what did you do yesterday?). Arabic past tense uses suffixes on the verb stem — learn the pattern once and it works for every verb.

The past tense adds endings to the three-letter stem. With كتب (kataba, to write): كتبتُ (katabtu — I wrote), كتبتَ (katabta — you masculine wrote), كتبتِ (katabti — you feminine wrote), كتب (kataba — he wrote), كتبتْ (katabat — she wrote), كتبنا (katabnā — we wrote). The full pattern to memorize: ـتُ (tu, I), ـتَ (ta, you m.), ـتِ (ti, you f.), ـَ (a, he — no ending), ـتْ (at, she), ـنا (nā, we). Apply it to درس (darasa, to study): درستُ (darastu — I studied), درستَ (darasta), درستِ (darasti), درس (darasa), درستْ (darasat), درسنا (darasnā). Two verbs fully conjugated gives you the engine for every regular verb.

The negative past uses لم (lam) + the present-tense verb without its final vowel: لم أدرس (lam adrus — I didn't study), لم يذهب (lam yadhhab — he didn't go). This is a hard rule — لم always pairs with the shortened present form, never with the past suffixes. Saying لم درستُ is wrong; لم أدرس is correct.

For "was" in the past, use كان (kāna): كان الطقس جميلا (kāna aṭ-ṭaqsu jamīlan — the weather was beautiful), كنت متعبا (kuntu mutʿaban — I was tired, masculine; mutʿabatan feminine). كان conjugates like any past verb: كنتَ (kunta, you m. were), كانت (kānat, she was), كنا (kunnā, we were).

Time words anchor the past: أمس (amsi, yesterday), أول أمس (awwala amsi, the day before yesterday), الأسبوع الماضي (al-usbūʿ al-māḍī, last week), الشهر الماضي (ash-shahr al-māḍī, last month), السنة الماضية (as-sanah al-māḍiyah, last year), قبل قليل (qabla qalīl, a little while ago). Combine them with the past tense: شاهدت فيلما أمس (shāhadtu filman amsi — I watched a movie yesterday), ذهبت إلى دمشق الأسبوع الماضي (dhahabtu ilā Dimashq al-usbūʿ al-māḍī — I went to Damascus last week). To list several past actions, chain them with و (wa, and): شاهدت فيلما وتسوقت (shāhadtu filman wa tasawwaqtu — I watched a movie and went shopping).

Two beginner warnings. First, don't mix لم with past suffixes — لم is always followed by the present stem: لم أكتب (lam aktub — I didn't write). Second, remember gender agreement in the past: هي ذهبتْ (hiya dhahabat — she went) needs the تْ ending; هو ذهب (huwa dhahaba — he went) takes none. Dropping the feminine تْ is the most common past-tense error.

Dialogue — talking about the weekend:

Nour: ماذا فعلت في عطلة نهاية الأسبوع؟ (Mādhā faʿalta fī ʿuṭlat nihāyat al-usbūʿ?) — What did you do on the weekend?
Tariq: ذهبت إلى الشاطئ مع عائلتي يوم السبت. (Dhahabtu ilā ash-shāṭiʾ maʿa ʿāʾilatī yawm as-sabt.) — I went to the beach with my family on Saturday.
Nour: كيف كان؟ هل كان الطقس جميلا؟ (Kayfa kān? Hal kāna aṭ-ṭaqsu jamīlan?) — How was it? Was the weather beautiful?
Tariq: نعم، كان مشمسا جدا! سبحنا وأكلنا السمك. (Naʿam, kāna mushmisan jiddan! Sabaḥnā wa akalnā as-samak.) — Yes, very sunny! We swam and ate fish.
Nour: جميل! بقيت في البيت. شاهدت فيلما وقرأت كتابا. (Jamīl! Baqītu fī al-bayt. Shāhadtu filman wa qaraʾtu kitāban.) — Lovely! I stayed home. I watched a movie and read a book.
Tariq: هل أعجبك الفيلم؟ (Hal aʿjabak al-film?) — Did you like the movie?
Nour: نعم، أحببته كثيرا. وماذا فعلت يوم الأحد؟ (Naʿam, aḥbabtuhu kathīran. Wa mādhā faʿalta yawm al-aḥad?) — Yes, I loved it. And what did you do Sunday?
Tariq: نمت حتى وقت متأخر وزرت والدي. (Nimtu ḥattā waqtin mutaʾakhkhir wa zurtu wāliday.) — I slept in late and visited my parents.

Practice: Write a paragraph of 8–10 sentences about what you did yesterday, using at least five different past-tense verbs with correct pronoun endings. Read it aloud twice. Then conjugate in writing: درس، كتب، ذهب — full past tense, all persons. Finally, answer aloud in complete Arabic sentences: ماذا أكلت أمس؟ في أي ساعة استيقظت؟ ماذا فعلت في عطلة نهاية الأسبوع الماضية؟
