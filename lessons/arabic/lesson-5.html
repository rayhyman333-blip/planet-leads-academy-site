This lesson turns you into someone who can get answers: how to use every question word, ask for directions, handle a full shopping conversation, and keep small talk going. Up to now you have mostly made statements; questions are what make you independent in an Arabic-speaking place. The good news: Arabic questions keep statement word order — you just swap in a question word where the answer goes, or start with هل for yes/no questions.

The core question words: ماذا (mādhā, what), أين (ayna, where), متى (matā, when), كيف (kayfa, how), كم (kam, how much/many), من (man, who), لماذا (limādhā, why). They sit where the answer would be: ما هذا؟ (mā hādhā? — what is this?), أين تذهب؟ (ayna tadhhab? — where are you going?), من هو؟ (man huwa? — who is he?), متى تأتي؟ (matā taʾtī? — when are you coming?). For yes/no questions, start with هل (hal): هل تذهب؟ (hal tadhhab? — are you going?). A classic beginner mistake is translating English word order — "Where are you going?" never scrambles the Arabic; keep أين تذهب؟.

Directions vocabulary is survival-level. أين...؟ (ayna...? — where is...?): أين المحطة؟ (ayna al-maḥaṭṭah? — where is the station?). كيف أذهب إلى...؟ (kayfa adhhabu ilā...? — how do I get to...?). يمين (yamīn, right), يسار (yasār, left), إلى الأمام (ilā al-amām, straight ahead), قريب (qarīb, near), بعيد (baʿīd, far), عند الزاوية (ʿinda az-zāwiyah, at the corner), إشارة المرور (ishārat al-murūr, traffic light). If you are lost: أنا ضائع (anā ḍāʾiʿ — said by a man; ḍāʾiʿah for a woman) — I'm lost. The lifesaver follow-up: هل يمكنك أن تريني على الخريطة؟ (hal yumkinuka an turīyanī ʿalā al-kharīṭah? — can you show me on the map?). When someone answers, listen for انعطف يمينا (inʿaṭif yamīnan, turn right) and استمر مباشرة (istamirr mubāsharatan, continue straight).

Shopping questions beyond prices: هل عندك...؟ (hal ʿindak...? — do you have...?): هل عندك مقاس كبير؟ (hal ʿindaka miqās kabīr? — do you have a large size?). هل يمكنني أن أجرب؟ (hal yumkinunī an ujarrib? — can I try it on?). هل تقبلون البطاقة؟ (hal taqbalūn al-biṭāqah? — do you take card?). متى تفتحون؟ (matā taftaḥūn? — when do you open?). Small-talk questions that keep conversations alive: من أين أنت؟ (min ayna ant? — where are you from?), ما هي مهنتك؟ (mā hiya mihnatuk? — what is your profession?), منذ متى وأنت هنا؟ (mundhu matā wa anta hunā? — how long have you been here?), هل تحب هذه المدينة؟ (hal tuḥibbu hādhihi al-madīnah? — do you like this city?), ماذا تنصحني؟ (mādhā tanṣaḥunī? — what do you recommend?).

Dialogue 1 — asking directions:

Tourist: عفوا، أين المتحف؟ (ʿAfwan, ayna al-matḥaf?) — Excuse me, where is the museum?
Local: قريب. اذهب مباشرة ثم انعطف يسارا عند إشارة المرور. (Qarīb. Idhhab mubāsharatan thumma inʿaṭif yasāran ʿinda ishārat al-murūr.) — Near. Go straight, then turn left at the traffic light.
Tourist: هل هو بعيد مشيا؟ (Hal huwa baʿīd mashyan?) — Is it far on foot?
Local: لا، خمس دقائق. هو بين البنك والصيدلية. (Lā, khamsu daqāʾiq. Huwa bayna al-bank wa aṣ-ṣaydaliyyah.) — No, five minutes. It's between the bank and the pharmacy.
Tourist: شكرا جزيلا! ما اسم هذا الشارع؟ (Shukran jazīlan! Mā ismu hādhā ash-shāriʿ?) — Thank you very much! What is this street called?
Local: هذا شارع النيل. (Hādhā shāriʿ an-Nīl.) — This is Nile Street.

Dialogue 2 — small talk at a café:

Sara: من أين أنت؟ (Min ayna ant?) — Where are you from?
Karim: أنا من لبنان. وأنت؟ (Anā min Lubnān. Wa ant?) — I am from Lebanon. And you?
Sara: أنا من هنا، من القاهرة. منذ متى وأنت هنا؟ (Anā min hunā, min al-Qāhirah. Mundhu matā wa anta hunā?) — I am from here, from Cairo. How long have you been here?
Karim: منذ أسبوعين. أحب هذه المدينة كثيرا. (Mundhu usbūʿayn. Uḥibbu hādhihi al-madīnah kathīran.) — Two weeks. I love this city a lot.
Sara: ماذا تنصحني أن أزور؟ (Mādhā tanṣaḥunī an azūr?) — What do you recommend I visit?
Karim: أريد أن أذهب إلى الحديقة غدا. لماذا لا تأتي؟ (Urīdu an adhhabu ilā al-ḥadīqah ghadan. Limādhā lā taʾtī?) — I want to go to the park tomorrow. Why don't you come?
Sara: فكرة جيدة! في أي ساعة؟ (Fikrah jayyidah! Fī ayyi sāʿah?) — Good idea! At what time?
Karim: في العاشرة. نلتقي هنا. (Fī al-ʿāshirah. Naltaqī hunā.) — At ten. We meet here.

Practice: Write ten questions — two each for directions, shopping, time, personal info, and opinions — and say each aloud. Memorize the direction sequence (إلى الأمام, يمين, يسار, عند الزاوية) and give directions from your front door to the nearest shop, in Arabic, out loud. Finally, role-play Dialogue 2 with changed details (your city, your time, your plan) until the question patterns feel automatic.
