This lesson builds your everyday number vocabulary: counting, telling time, and using days, months, and dates. Arabic numbers have one famous quirk — the numerals ٠١٢٣٤٥٦٧٨٩ look different from Western digits — plus gender agreement rules that surprise beginners. Learn the system exactly, and numbers become pure utility: prices, phone numbers, addresses, schedules.

First, the numerals. Arabic uses Eastern Arabic numerals: ٠ (0), ١ (1), ٢ (2), ٣ (3), ٤ (4), ٥ (5), ٦ (6), ٧ (7), ٨ (8), ٩ (9). You will see these on clocks, prices, and license plates across the Middle East. Western digits (0–9) are also common, especially in the Levant and Gulf — learn to read both, and you will never be stuck.

Numbers 1–10 with transliteration: واحد (wāḥid, 1), اثنان (ithnān, 2), ثلاثة (thalāthah, 3), أربعة (arbaʿah, 4), خمسة (khamsah, 5), ستة (sittah, 6), سبعة (sabʿah, 7), ثمانية (thamāniyah, 8), تسعة (tisʿah, 9), عشرة (ʿasharah, 10). Eleven and twelve: أحد عشر (aḥada ʿashar, 11), اثنا عشر (ithnā ʿashar, 12). Tens: عشرون (ʿishrūn, 20), ثلاثون (thalāthūn, 30), أربعون (arbaʿūn, 40), خمسون (khamsūn, 50). One hundred: مائة (miʾah, 100). One thousand: ألف (alf, 1000). Compound numbers join with و (wa, and): خمسة وعشرون (khamsah wa ʿishrūn, 25).

The gender rule: numbers 3–10 take the OPPOSITE gender of the noun they count — ثلاثة كتب (thalāthatu kutub, three books — feminine number with masculine noun), خمس سيارات (khamsu sayyārāt, five cars — masculine-looking number with feminine noun). Numbers 1 and 2 match the noun's gender and usually follow it: كتاب واحد (kitāb wāḥid, one book), سيارة واحدة (sayyārah wāḥidah, one car). This feels backwards at first; drill it with real nouns until it is automatic.

Telling time: الساعة (as-sāʿah) means both "hour" and "o'clock." الساعة الواحدة (as-sāʿah al-wāḥidah, 1 o'clock), الساعة الثالثة (as-sāʿah ath-thālithah, 3 o'clock) — hours use ordinal numbers. Minutes: الساعة الثالثة وعشر دقائق (3:10), والنصف (wa an-niṣf, half past) — الساعة الثالثة والنصف (3:30), والربع (wa ar-rubʿ, quarter past) — الساعة الثالثة والربع (3:15), إلا الربع (illā ar-rubʿ, quarter to) — الساعة الرابعة إلا الربع (3:45). صباحا (ṣabāḥan) is AM, مساء (masāʾan) is PM. Ask the time with كم الساعة؟ (kam as-sāʿah? — what time is it?).

Days of the week: الأحد (al-aḥad, Sunday), الاثنين (al-ithnayn, Monday), الثلاثاء (ath-thulāthāʾ, Tuesday), الأربعاء (al-arbiʿāʾ, Wednesday), الخميس (al-khamīs, Thursday), الجمعة (al-jumuʿah, Friday), السبت (as-sabt, Saturday). اليوم (al-yawm) is today, غدا (ghadan) is tomorrow, أمس (amsi) is yesterday. Months: يناير (yanāyir, January), فبراير (fibrāyir, February), مارس (māris, March), أبريل (ibrīl, April), مايو (māyū, May), يونيو (yūnyū, June), يوليو (yūlyū, July), أغسطس (aghusṭus, August), سبتمبر (sibtambir, September), أكتوبر (uktūbir, October), نوفمبر (nūfambir, November), ديسمبر (dīsambir, December). Dates: الخامس من مايو (al-khāmis min māyū, May 5th).

Money and shopping vocabulary, since numbers live there: بكم؟ (bi-kam? — how much?), بكم هذا؟ (bi-kam hādhā? — how much is this?). غال جدا (ghālin jiddan) — very expensive. رخيص (rakhīṣ) — cheap. أريد هذا (urīdu hādhā) — I want this one.

Dialogue — at a market:

Customer: السلام عليكم! بكم هذا؟ (As-salāmu ʿalaykum! Bi-kam hādhā?) — Peace be upon you! How much is this?
Vendor: وعليكم السلام! بعشرة. تريد كم؟ (Wa ʿalaykum as-salām! Bi-ʿasharah. Turīdu kam?) — And upon you be peace! Ten. How many do you want?
Customer: أريد ثلاثة. وكم سعر هذه؟ (Urīdu thalāthah. Wa kam siʿru hādhihi?) — I want three. And what is the price of this one?
Vendor: خمسة للواحدة. (Khamsah lil-wāḥidah.) — Five for one.
Customer: غال جدا! أرخص قليلا من فضلك. (Ghālin jiddan! Arkhaṣ qalīlan min faḍlik.) — Very expensive! Cheaper, a little, please.
Vendor: حسنا، أربعة. (Ḥasanan, arbaʿah.) — Okay, four.
Customer: اتفقنا. أريد اثنتين. (Ittafaqnā. Urīdu ithnatayn.) — Agreed. I want two.

Practice: Count aloud from 1 to 20 without stopping, then backwards from 20 to 1. Write today's date, your birthday, and three appointment times in Arabic. Drill the gender rule with five masculine nouns and five feminine nouns (ثلاثة كتب، خمس سيارات pattern). Finally, memorize the market dialogue's price lines and role-play buying three different items, asking بكم هذا؟ each time.
