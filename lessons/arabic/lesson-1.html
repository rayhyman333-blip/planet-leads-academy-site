This lesson teaches you to read and write the Arabic alphabet — 28 letters written right to left, with shapes that change depending on position. Arabic script looks intimidating for exactly one week; then its logic clicks and it becomes one of the most phonetic writing systems you will ever learn. Your goal today: recognize every letter, write the basic forms, and pronounce the sounds that don't exist in English.

Arabic is written right to left. There are no capital letters. Most letters connect to their neighbors, and each letter has up to four forms: isolated, initial (start of a word), medial (middle), and final (end). Six letters never connect to the following letter: ا (alif), د (dal), ذ (dhal), ر (ra), ز (zay), و (waw). Learn the alphabet in this order, with transliteration: ا (a), ب (b), ت (t), ث (th as in "think"), ج (j), ح (ḥ — a deep breathy h from the throat), خ (kh as in Scottish "loch"), د (d), ذ (dh as in "this"), ر (r — rolled), ز (z), س (s), ش (sh), ص (ṣ — deep, emphatic s), ض (ḍ — deep, emphatic d), ط (ṭ — deep, emphatic t), ظ (ẓ — deep, emphatic "th" as in "this"), ع (ʿ — a voiced sound from deep in the throat, the hardest for beginners), غ (gh — like a gargled French r), ف (f), ق (q — a deep k from the back of the throat), ك (k), ل (l), م (m), ن (n), هـ (h), و (w/long ū), ي (y/long ī).

The sounds that don't exist in English need deliberate practice. ح (ḥ): breathe out strongly with your throat open, like fogging a mirror from deep in the throat — compare هـ (h, normal h) in هواء (hawāʾ, air) vs حار (ḥārr, hot). ع (ʿ): tighten your throat muscles and voice from deep down — practice with عين (ʿayn, eye). The emphatic letters ص ض ط ظ are pronounced with the tongue pulled back and the mouth hollowed, giving a "dark" quality: سين (sīn, the letter s) vs صين? — better pair: سار (sāra, walked) vs صار (ṣāra, became). ق (q): make a k but much further back, almost a soft pop in the throat — قلب (qalb, heart/dog? no: قلب qalb = heart). خ (kh): gargle lightly — خبز (khubz, bread).

Short vowels are marks written above or below consonants: َ (fatḥa = a), ُ (ḍamma = u), ِ (kasra = i). Long vowels use letters: ا (ā), و (ū), ي (ī). Most printed Arabic omits the short-vowel marks, which is why this course always gives you transliteration — you learn the word's sound first, and the unvowelled spelling becomes readable with practice. The ة (tāʾ marbūṭah) marks feminine words and sounds like "ah": مدرسة (madrasah, school). The ال (al-) prefix means "the": الكتاب (al-kitāb, the book).

One famous rule: sun and moon letters. The 14 sun letters (ت ث د ذ ر ز س ش ص ض ط ظ ل ن) assimilate the l of ال in pronunciation: الشمس (ash-shams, the sun — pronounced "ash-shams," not "al-shams"). The 14 moon letters keep it: القمر (al-qamar, the moon). You don't need to produce this perfectly yet — just recognize why الشمس is pronounced "ash-shams."

Common beginner mistakes to fix now. First, writing left to right — train your hand from day one: start at the right margin. Second, pronouncing ح as a normal h and ع as a glottal stop — they are distinct letters with distinct sounds, and mixing them changes words. Third, ignoring the emphatic letters — ص and س are different letters; صبر (ṣabr, patience) is not سبر.

Practice: Write the full alphabet in order, right to left, three times, saying each letter's name aloud. Then write these words and read them: باب (bāb, door), بيت (bayt, house), كتاب (kitāb, book), قلم (qalam, pen), مدرسة (madrasah, school), شمس (shams, sun), قمر (qamar, moon). Finally, read this sentence aloud slowly: أنا طالب (anā ṭālib — I am a student). It uses only sounds from this lesson, and it is your first real Arabic sentence.
