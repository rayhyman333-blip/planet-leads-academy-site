This lesson teaches you to talk about the future — making plans, accepting invitations, and handling two of the most useful real-world conversations: ordering in a restaurant and navigating travel. Future talk is what turns you from a tourist into someone who can actually live in Italian: booking, inviting, promising, and planning.

Italian has two easy ways to express the future. The everyday one is the PRESENT TENSE with a future time word — Italians do this constantly: Studio domani (I'm studying tomorrow), Partiamo la settimana prossima (We're leaving next week), Vieni sabato? (Are you coming Saturday?). It is simple and completely natural. The second is the futuro semplice, formed by changing the infinitive ending: -are verbs → -erò, -erai, -erà, -eremo, -erete, -eranno (parlare → parlerò); -ere and -ire verbs → -irò... (credere → crederò, dormire → dormirò). Irregular stems to know: essere → sarò, avere → avrò, andare → andrò, fare → farò, potere → potrò, volere → vorrò, venire → verrò, dovere → dovrò. Use it for promises, predictions, and formal plans: Ti chiamerò domani (I will call you tomorrow), Farà bel tempo (It will be nice weather). For this level, default to present + time word in conversation and recognize the futuro when you hear it.

Restaurant vocabulary and phrases. Il menù (the menu), il piatto del giorno (dish of the day), l'antipasto (starter), il primo (first course — usually pasta), il secondo (second course — meat or fish), il dolce (dessert), la bevanda (drink). Ordering: Vorrei... (I would like... — polite), Per me... (For me...), Cosa mi consiglia? (What do you recommend?). During the meal: È squisito (It's delicious), Mi porta...? (Can you bring me...?), Il conto, per favore (The check, please). Common mistake: ordering only 'un caffè' at the bar means espresso — specify cappuccino, macchiato, or latte if you want those. Another: 'voglio' barked at the waiter is blunt — vorrei is much better. Tipping: service is usually included (coperto/servizio); rounding up is appreciated but optional.

Travel phrases. All'aeroporto (at the airport): Dov'è il gate...? (Where is gate...?), Il mio volo parte alle... (My flight leaves at...), Ho una prenotazione (I have a reservation). In hotel: Vorrei una camera doppia (I'd like a double room), A che ora è la colazione? (What time is breakfast?), C'è il wifi? (Is there wifi?). Problems: Ho perso il passaporto (I lost my passport), Ho bisogno di aiuto (I need help), Dov'è una farmacia? (Where is there a pharmacy?). Note how much of this reuses Lessons 2–5 — the course is compounding.

Dialogue 1 — at a restaurant:

Cameriere: Buonasera. Avete una prenotazione? — Good evening. Do you have a reservation?
Federica: Sì, a nome Federica. — Yes, under Federica.
Cameriere: Da questa parte, prego. Volete vedere il menù? — This way, please. Would you like to see the menu?
Federica: Sì, grazie. Qual è il piatto del giorno? — Yes, thanks. What is the dish of the day?
Cameriere: È pollo con riso. È squisito. — It's chicken with rice. It's delicious.
Federica: Vorrei quello, e un'insalata. E tu, Andrea? — I'd like that, and a salad. And you, Andrea?
Andrea: Per me, il pesce. Mi porta dell'acqua, per favore? — For me, the fish. Can you bring me water, please?
Cameriere: Subito. — Right away.
(dopo) Federica: Il conto, per favore. — The check, please.
Cameriere: Sono trentacinque euro. — It's thirty-five euros.

Dialogue 2 — making weekend plans:

Stefano: Cosa fai sabato? — What are you doing Saturday?
Martina: Vado a trovare mia sorella. E tu? — I'm going to visit my sister. And you?
Stefano: Vado al mercato la mattina. Vuoi venire? — I'm going to the market in the morning. Do you want to come?
Martina: Sì, volentieri. A che ora ci vediamo? — Yes, gladly. What time shall we meet?
Stefano: Alle nove. Dopo pranziamo insieme. — At nine. Afterwards we'll have lunch together.
Martina: Perfetto. Ti chiamerò venerdì per confermare. — Perfect. I'll call you Friday to confirm.

Practice: Write three plans for next week using the present + time word, and three promises using the futuro semplice. Then write a full restaurant dialogue (8+ lines) where you order a starter, main, drink, and dessert, ask for the check, and react to the food. Say it all aloud. Finally, plan a real weekend in Italian: where you will go, who you will see, what you will eat — five sentences minimum, out loud.
