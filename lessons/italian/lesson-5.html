This lesson turns you into someone who can get answers: how to form every kind of question, ask for directions, handle a full shopping conversation, and keep small talk going. Up to now you have mostly made statements; questions are what make you independent in an Italian-speaking place. The good news: Italian questions are simple — keep normal word order and raise your intonation, just like Spanish.

To form a question, keep statement word order and raise your intonation at the end, with the question mark doing the work in writing: Parli italiano? (Do you speak Italian?), Dov'è la stazione? (Where is the station?). For emphasis you can invert: Parla Lei italiano? The core question words: che / cosa (what — Che cos'è? = What is it?), chi (who), quale (which), dove (where), quando (when), perché (why — one word, with accent), come (how), quanto (how much), quanti (how many). Perché (why) versus perché used as 'because' in answers — same word, context decides. To ask 'why not': Perché no?

Directions vocabulary is survival-level. Dov'è...? (Where is...?), Come arrivo a...? (How do I get to...?), È vicino (It's near), È lontano (It's far), a destra (to the right), a sinistra (to the left), sempre dritto (straight ahead), all'angolo (on the corner), tra... e... (between... and...). When someone answers, listen for: giri (turn), attraversi (cross), continui (continue). Polite opener: Scusi, dov'è la stazione? (Excuse me, where is the station?). And the lifesaver follow-up: Può mostrarmi sulla mappa? (Can you show me on the map?).

Shopping questions beyond prices: Ha...? (Do you have...?), C'è...? / Ci sono...? (Is there...? / Are there...?), Posso provare? (Can I try it on?), Ha una taglia più grande / più piccola? (Do you have a bigger/smaller size?), Accettate la carta? (Do you take card?), A che ora aprite? (What time do you open?). Small-talk questions that keep conversations alive: Di dov'è? (Where are you from?), Che lavoro fa? (What do you do for work?), Da quanto tempo è qui? (How long have you been here?), Le piace la città? (Do you like the city?), Cosa mi consiglia? (What do you recommend?). Two mistakes to avoid: saying 'Qual è il tuo nome?' instead of Come ti chiami? (nobody says the first), and answering a question you only half understood — use Non ho capito bene, può ripetere? instead of guessing.

Dialogue 1 — asking directions:

Turista: Scusi, dov'è il museo? — Excuse me, where is the museum?
Passante: È vicino. Vada sempre dritto e giri a sinistra. — It's near. Go straight ahead and turn left.
Turista: È lontano a piedi? — Is it far on foot?
Passante: No, cinque minuti. È tra la banca e la farmacia. — No, five minutes. It's between the bank and the pharmacy.
Turista: Grazie mille. Come si chiama questa strada? — Thank you very much. What is this street called?
Passante: È via Dante. — It's Via Dante.

Dialogue 2 — small talk at a café:

Sofia: Di dov'è? — Where are you from?
Luca: Sono del Canada. E Lei? — I'm from Canada. And you?
Sofia: Sono di qui, di Firenze. Da quanto tempo è qui? — I'm from here, from Florence. How long have you been here?
Luca: Da due settimane. Mi piace molto la città. — For two weeks. I like the city a lot.
Sofia: Cosa mi consiglia di visitare? — What do you recommend visiting?
Luca: Voglio vedere il parco domani. Perché non viene? — I want to see the park tomorrow. Why don't you come?
Sofia: Buona idea! A che ora? — Good idea! At what time?
Luca: Alle dieci. Ci vediamo qui. — At ten. See you here.

Practice: Write ten questions — two each for directions, shopping, time, personal info, and opinions — and say each aloud with rising intonation. Then memorize the direction-giving sequence (sempre dritto, a destra, a sinistra, all'angolo) and give directions from your front door to the nearest shop, in Italian, out loud. Finally, role-play Dialogue 2 with a partner or alone, then change three details (city, time, place) and run it again — this is how small talk becomes automatic.
