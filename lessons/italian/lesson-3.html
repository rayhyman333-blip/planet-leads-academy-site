This lesson tackles the two most important verbs in Italian — essere (to be) and avere (to have) — plus the present tense, which lets you describe what you do every day. Essere and avere are the backbone of the language: they appear in nearly every sentence, and both double as helper verbs for the past tense you will meet in Lesson 6. Master their shapes now and everything later gets easier.

ESSERE (to be) — memorize this table completely: io sono (SOH-noh — I am), tu sei (seh-ee — you are, informal), lui/lei è (eh — he/she is), noi siamo (SYAH-moh — we are), voi siete (SYEH-teh — you are, plural), loro sono (SOH-noh — they are). Use it for identity, origin, description, profession, and location: Sono americano (I am American), È alta (She is tall), Siamo a Roma (We are in Rome), Sono le tre (It is three o'clock — time uses essere). Note that Italian drops the subject pronoun constantly — 'sono' already means 'I am', so 'io sono' is only used for emphasis or clarity.

AVERE (to have) — equally essential: io ho (oh — I have; the H is silent), tu hai (igh — you have), lui/lei ha (ah — he/she has), noi abbiamo (ahb-BYAH-moh — we have), voi avete (ah-VEH-teh — you have), loro hanno (AHN-noh — they have). Italian uses avere for age, hunger, thirst, and need: Ho vent'anni (I am twenty — literally 'I have twenty years'), Ho fame (I am hungry), Ho sete (I am thirsty), Ho caldo (I am hot), Ho freddo (I am cold), Ho bisogno di... (I need...). This is the single most useful avere pattern for daily life — learn ho fame, ho sete, ho caldo, ho freddo as fixed phrases today.

Now the present tense of regular verbs. Italian verbs end in -are, -ere, or -ire. For -ARE verbs like parlare (to speak): parlo, parli, parla, parliamo, parlate, parlano. For -ERE verbs like credere (to believe): credo, credi, crede, crediamo, credete, credono. For -IRE verbs like dormire (to sleep): dormo, dormi, dorme, dormiamo, dormite, dormono. (A subgroup of -ire verbs like capire inserts -isc-: capisco, capisci, capisce, capiamo, capite, capiscono — learn them as you meet them.) Practice with lavorare (to work): Lavoro in un ufficio (I work in an office), and abitare (to live): Abito a Milano (I live in Milan). Notice the musical rhythm of the endings — read every conjugation aloud.

Essential irregular verbs to learn as vocabulary: ANDARE (to go): vado, vai, va, andiamo, andate, vanno. Vado al mercato (I go to the market). FARE (to do/make): faccio, fai, fa, facciamo, fate, fanno. Faccio sport (I exercise). VOLERE (to want): voglio, vuoi, vuole, vogliamo, volete, vogliono. Voglio un caffè (I want a coffee). POTERE (to be able to): posso, puoi, può, possiamo, potete, possono. Posso aiutarla (I can help you). DARE (to give): do, dai, dà, diamo, date, danno. These appear in nearly every conversation, so they repay memorization immediately.

Two beginner mistakes to avoid. First, translating English 'do' questions word-for-word: English 'Do you speak Italian?' is simply Parli italiano? or Parla italiano? with rising intonation — Italian needs no helper verb. Second, adjective agreement: adjectives match the noun in gender and number — un uomo alto (a tall man), una donna alta (a tall woman), due bambini alti (two tall children). The endings -o/-a/-i/-e are audible, unlike French, so agreement is both visible and heard.

Dialogue — two coworkers at lunch:

Andrea: Dove lavori? — Where do you work?
Chiara: Lavoro in una banca. Sono cassiera. E tu? — I work at a bank. I am a cashier. And you?
Andrea: Sono insegnante. Lavoro in una scuola. — I am a teacher. I work at a school.
Chiara: Sei stanco oggi? — Are you tired today?
Andrea: No, sto bene. Vuoi pranzare con me? — No, I'm fine. Do you want to have lunch with me?
Chiara: Sì, volentieri. Andiamo al ristorante? — Yes, gladly. Shall we go to the restaurant?
Andrea: Andiamo. Ho fame. — Let's go. I'm hungry.

Practice: Conjugate in writing: parlare, credere, dormire, essere, avere, andare, fare. Then write six sentences about yourself: two with essere (identity/origin/profession), two with avere (age/needs/feelings), two with present-tense action verbs. Say each sentence aloud. Finally, translate to Italian: 'She is a doctor. She works in a hospital. She is tired today. She wants a coffee.' Check: È un medico. Lavora in un ospedale. È stanca oggi. Vuole un caffè.
