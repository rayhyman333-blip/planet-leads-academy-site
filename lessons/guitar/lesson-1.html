This lesson teaches you how to hold the guitar, tune it, and play your first notes. Everything in this course - chords, strumming, songs - depends on the guitar being in tune and sitting stably in your hands. A surprising number of beginners fight their instrument for weeks because it was never tuned properly or held securely. Spend this whole lesson on setup: by the end you will hold the guitar comfortably, tune all six strings, and play clear open-string notes.

Holding the guitar (these instructions assume a right-handed player; left-handed players mirror everything). Sit in a straight-backed chair with both feet flat on the floor. Rest the waist of the guitar - the narrow curved middle of the body - on your right thigh, and pull the body gently against your torso. The neck should angle slightly upward, with the headstock roughly at shoulder height. Your right forearm rests on the upper edge of the body near the elbow, and your right hand falls naturally over the strings near the soundhole. Your left hand supports the neck with the thumb resting lightly on the back of the neck, roughly behind the fingers. The guitar should feel balanced, not like you are wrestling it - if the neck dives toward the floor, pull the body closer to you.

Tuning. The six strings, from thickest to thinnest, are E, A, D, G, B, and high E - remember them with "Eddie Ate Dynamite, Good Bye Eddie." Use an electronic clip-on tuner: it is inexpensive, fast, and accurate, and there is no virtue in tuning by ear as a beginner. Pluck one string at a time and turn its tuning peg slowly while watching the tuner display - turn one way and the pitch rises, the other way and it falls. Tune up to the note rather than down to it, and always pluck the string again after turning the peg to check. New strings stretch and slip out of tune quickly, so expect to retune several times in your first weeks - that is normal, not a broken guitar.

Your first notes: the open strings. Pluck each string one at a time with your right-hand thumb or a pick, starting from the thick low E and moving to the thin high E, saying each string's name out loud: E, A, D, G, B, E. Listen for a clean, ringing tone that lasts - a dull thud means you brushed a neighboring string or barely touched it. Next, your first fretted notes: press the low E string down at the first fret with your left index finger, right behind the metal fret wire (not on top of it, not halfway to the next fret), and pluck. That is F. Move the same finger to the second fret for F sharp, then the third for G. Press just hard enough for a clean note - white knuckles mean too much force.

Time for drills. First, the tuning round: tune all six strings with the tuner, then play them open from low to high naming each one, five times through. Second, the open-string ladder: pluck E-A-D-G-B-E then reverse E-B-G-D-A-E, ten times, aiming for an even volume on every string. Third, the first-fret workout: on the low E string, play open, first fret, second fret, third fret, and back down, with the index finger only, eight times slowly - this is your first taste of fretting, so keep the thumb relaxed on the back of the neck.

Two common mistakes. The first is pressing the string halfway between frets, which produces a buzzing, dead sound. Always fret just behind the wire - close to it, not on it. The second is squeezing the neck too hard: your thumb and fingers clamp down, your hand tires in minutes, and your wrist aches. If your hand hurts, you are pressing too hard. Ease off until the note just barely rings clean, and memorize that minimum pressure.

Practice: Tune all six strings, then play the open-string ladder (low to high and back) naming each string aloud, five times. Then play the first-fret workout on the low E string eight times slowly. Finish by retuning - check every string once more with the tuner, because playing pulls new strings slightly out of tune.
