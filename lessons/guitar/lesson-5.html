This lesson turns your chords and strumming into your first full song. You will learn the techniques that make chord changes sound smooth - anticipation, pivot fingers, and rhythmic continuity - and then play an original two-part song written for this course, "First Light," which uses only the chords you know. Playing a complete song start to finish is a milestone: it is the moment practice becomes music.

Smooth switching is about what happens before the change, not during it. Anticipation: on the final beat before a chord change, your fretting hand should already be moving - the change happens in the last fraction of the beat so the new chord lands exactly on beat one. Practice this deliberately: play G for three beats, and on beat four lift and move while the right hand keeps strumming. Pivot fingers: identify fingers that barely move between two chords and let them guide the hand - for example, moving from Em to Am, your middle and ring fingers shift together as a unit. Rhythmic continuity: the right hand never stops. Even if the left hand is late, the strum keeps going - a slightly late chord in rhythm sounds fine, while a stopped strum sounds broken.

Now the song. "First Light" has two parts. The verse is Em - C - G - D, four beats each, played twice through. The chorus is C - G - D - Em, four beats each, played twice. The strumming pattern is pattern two from Lesson 3 (down, down-up, down, down-up). The structure is: verse, verse, chorus, verse, chorus, chorus. Before playing it whole, drill each chord change in the song with the one-minute change method from Lesson 4 - especially C to G and G to D, which are the trickiest pairs here.

Build it in stages. Stage one: play the verse chord loop with one simple down-strum per chord, four times through, no pattern yet - just clean changes in time. Stage two: add pattern two strumming to the verse, four times through. Stage three: the same process for the chorus. Stage four: play the full structure start to finish, slowly, without stopping. Only when the whole song is clean at a slow tempo do you bring it up to a comfortable speed. Record yourself and listen back: you are checking for steady rhythm and clean changes, not perfection.

Two common mistakes. The first is stopping the strumming hand during changes: the moment the left hand struggles, the right hand freezes. Train the right hand to be stubborn - it keeps its pattern no matter what the left hand does. If a chord is not ready, strum the open strings for that beat and land the chord on the next one; in rhythm, nobody notices. The second is practicing the song only from the beginning: the ending then stays shaky forever. Start your practice from the chorus sometimes, or from the second verse, so every section gets equal work.

Time for drills. First, the anticipation drill: play any two-chord change with the move starting on beat four, twenty repetitions. Second, the non-stop strum: play the verse progression with pattern two for three minutes, and if you fumble a change, keep strumming and rejoin - no stopping allowed. Third, the section shuffle: play the song's sections in random order (chorus first, then verse) twice through, so no section depends on what came before it.

Practice: Play "First Light" all the way through, slowly, three times without stopping - verse, verse, chorus, verse, chorus, chorus. Use pattern two strumming throughout. Record the third run and listen back, writing down the one chord change that needs the most work, and drill that change for five minutes. Then play the song a fourth time, this time starting from the chorus, to prove every section stands on its own. End by playing just the verse progression with your eyes closed once - if the changes stay clean, the shapes are truly in your hands.
