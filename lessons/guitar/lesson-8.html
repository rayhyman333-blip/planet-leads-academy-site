This lesson gives you a permanent practice routine and five songs to learn next. You now own the complete beginner toolkit: tuning, open chords, strumming patterns, smooth changes, single-note scales, and the beginnings of barre chords. What turns this toolkit into real musicianship is the same thing as in every skill: consistent, well-structured practice. This lesson builds that structure and points it at music you will love playing.

The routine has four blocks in a thirty-minute session. Block one, warm-up (five minutes): tune up, play the open-string ladder, then the spider exercise once through - waking the hands up. Block two, technique (ten minutes): rotate daily through chord-change drills, the pentatonic scale, and barre practice; work on whatever is weakest, with the metronome, slowly. Block three, songs (ten minutes): your current song, built in sections, never mindless full run-throughs - pick the worst section and drill it. Block four, fun (five minutes): play anything you enjoy, even imperfectly, and end every session wanting to come back tomorrow. The order is fixed: warm-up, technique, songs, fun.

The weekly plan. Play five or six days a week - thirty focused minutes beats a weekend marathon, because your hands and brain consolidate learning between sessions. Give each day a flavor: Monday, chord changes and the one-minute drill circuit; Tuesday, strumming patterns with the metronome; Wednesday, the pentatonic scale plus ten minutes of improvisation; Thursday, barre chord practice; Friday, song work - record yourself and listen back; weekend, one light session or rest. Keep the practice log from your materials: date, minutes, blocks completed, and one honest line about what improved. Review the log monthly - the progress you forget is the motivation you lose.

Five songs to learn next, each teaching something new. One, a 12-bar blues in A (using A7, D7, and E7 shapes): teaches seventh chords and the classic blues form that underpins rock and blues rhythm playing. Two, a three-chord folk strum in G (G-Em-C-D): teaches the world's most common progression with a new strumming pattern. Three, a minor-key arpeggio piece in Am: teaches picking individual strings inside chords, a gentle first step into fingerstyle. Four, a pentatonic melody piece: a simple composed melody using only the A minor pentatonic scale, teaching phrasing with single notes. Five, a barre-chord rocker in F (F to B flat to C): your first song built on movable barre shapes, proving the Lesson 7 work pays off. Learn them one at a time, one per two weeks, with the section method from Lesson 5, and record each one when it is clean.

Two common mistakes. The first is abandoning structure once the course ends: without the four blocks, practice dissolves into noodling on favorite chords. Keep the routine for at least three months - it is the difference between a player and someone who used to play. The second is collecting songs instead of finishing them: starting five songs and completing none teaches less than perfecting one. Finish each of the five songs - clean, in time, recorded - before moving to the next.

Time for drills. First, the routine rehearsal: do one full thirty-minute session today following the four blocks exactly, timing each block. Second, the song start: learn the A7, D7, and E7 shapes for the 12-bar blues today, using the clarity-check method from Lesson 2, ten clean repetitions each. Third, the baseline: run the one-minute-change circuit on your five weakest chord pairs and write down the scores to beat next month.

Practice: Complete one full thirty-minute session using the four-block routine. Fill in the practice log honestly. Then learn the A7, D7, and E7 shapes, running the clarity check on each ten times. Write down your baseline one-minute-change scores for your five weakest chord pairs. Finally, write your one-month goal at the top of the log in a single sentence, such as "Play First Light cleanly at full speed." Read it before every session - it turns thirty unfocused minutes into thirty minutes with a destination.
