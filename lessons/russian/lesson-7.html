This lesson teaches you to talk about the future — making plans, accepting invitations, and handling two of the most useful real-world conversations: ordering in a restaurant and navigating travel. Future talk is what turns you from a tourist into someone who can actually live in Russian: booking, inviting, promising, and planning.

Russian has two ways to express the future, matching its two verb aspects. The everyday one uses буду (BU-du, I will) plus the imperfective infinitive: Я буду работать (ya BU-du ra-BO-tat', I will work / I will be working), Мы будем путешествовать (we will travel), Ты будешь звонить? (will you call?). Just conjugate быть in the future (буду, будешь, будет, будем, будете, будут) and add any imperfective infinitive — this one structure covers most future needs: plans, intentions, ongoing future actions. The second way is the perfective future: perfective verbs have no present tense, so their "present-looking" forms mean the future — Я скажу (ya ska-ZHU, I will say/tell), Я сделаю (ya SDYE-la-yu, I will do), Мы пойдём (my pay-DYOM, we will go). Use it for single completed future actions and promises: Я позвоню завтра (ya pa-zva-NYU ZAF-tra, I will call tomorrow), Я куплю билеты (I will buy the tickets). For this level, default to буду + infinitive in conversation and recognize the perfective future when you hear it.

Restaurant vocabulary and phrases. Меню (mye-NYU, menu), счёт (shchyot, check/bill), закуска (za-KUS-ka, starter), основное блюдо (as-nav-NO-ye BLYU-da, main course), десерт (dye-SYERT), напиток (na-PI-tak, drink). Ordering: Можно меню, пожалуйста? (may I have the menu, please?). Я буду... (I will have...) is the natural ordering phrase — Я буду борщ (I'll have the borscht). Это вкусно (E-ta FKUS-na, it's delicious), Очень вкусно! Что вы посоветуете? (what do you recommend?). During the meal: Можно счёт, пожалуйста? (the check, please). Common mistake: saying "Я хочу это" barked at the waiter is grammatically fine but socially blunt — Я буду... or Можно мне... (MOZH-na mnye, may I have...) sounds much better.

Travel phrases. В аэропорту (f a-e-ra-par-TU, at the airport): Где выход на посадку? (gdye VY-khat na pa-SAT-ku, where is the boarding gate?), Мой рейс в... (my ryeys, my flight is at...), У меня бронь (u mi-NYA bron', I have a reservation). В гостинице (f ga-STI-ni-tsy, at the hotel): Мне нужен двухместный номер (mnye NU-zhen dvukh-MYEST-ny no-MYER, I need a double room), Во сколько завтрак? (what time is breakfast?), У вас есть вай-фай? (do you have wi-fi?). Problems: Я потерял паспорт (ya pa-tye-RYAL pas-PART, I lost my passport), Мне нужна помощь (mnye nuzh-NA PO-mashch', I need help), Где аптека? (where is the pharmacy?). Note how much of this reuses Lessons 2–5 — the course is compounding.

Dialogue 1 — at a restaurant:

— Добрый вечер! У вас заказан столик? (u vas za-KA-zan STO-lik) — Good evening! Do you have a table booked?
— Да, на имя Анна. (na I-mya AN-na) — Yes, under Anna.
— Проходите, пожалуйста. Меню? (pra-kha-DI-tye) — This way, please. Menus?
— Да, спасибо. Что вы посоветуете? (shto vy pa-sa-VYE-tu-i-tye) — Yes, thanks. What do you recommend?
— Борщ очень вкусный сегодня. (borshch O-chen' FKUS-ny) — The borscht is very tasty today.
— Тогда я буду борщ и салат. А ты? (tag-DA ya BU-du) — Then I'll have the borscht and a salad. And you?
— Я буду рыбу. Можно мне воду, пожалуйста? (MOZH-na mnye VO-du) — I'll have the fish. May I have some water, please?
— Конечно. (ka-NYECH-na) — Of course.
(позже) — Можно счёт, пожалуйста? — The check, please.
— С вас тысяча двести рублей. (s vas TY-sya-cha dvye-STI ru-BLYEY) — That's 1200 rubles.

Dialogue 2 — making weekend plans:

— Что ты будешь делать в субботу? (shto ty BU-dyesh DYE-lat' f su-BO-tu) — What will you do Saturday?
— Я буду навещать сестру. А ты? (na-vye-SHCHAT' syest-RU) — I will visit my sister. And you?
— Я пойду на рынок утром. Пойдёшь со мной? (pay-DU na RY-nak, pay-DYOSH sa mnoy) — I will go to the market in the morning. Will you come with me?
— Да, с удовольствием. Во сколько встретимся? (s u-da-VOL-stvi-yem, fstrye-TIM-sya) — Yes, gladly. What time shall we meet?
— В девять. Потом пообедаем вместе. (pa-TOM pa-a-BYE-da-yem vmye-STYE) — At nine. Then we'll have lunch together.
— Отлично. Я позвоню в пятницу. (at-LICH-na, pa-zva-NYU f PYAT-ni-tsu) — Great. I'll call on Friday.

Practice: Write three plans for next week using буду + infinitive, and three promises using the perfective future. Then write a full restaurant dialogue (8+ lines) where you order a starter, main, drink, and dessert, ask for the check, and react to the food. Say it all aloud. Finally, plan a real weekend in Russian: where you will go, who you will see, what you will eat — five sentences minimum, out loud.
