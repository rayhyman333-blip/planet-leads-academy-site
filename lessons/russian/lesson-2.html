This lesson gives you your first real social toolkit: greeting people politely, introducing yourself, and surviving conversations when you do not understand something. These phrases are the highest-value sentences in the language because they unlock every interaction — ordering food, asking for help, meeting people. Learn them cold before you worry about grammar, because even a flawless sentence is useless if you cannot open and close a conversation gracefully.

Start with greetings. Здравствуйте (ZDRAV-stvuy-tye) is the formal hello, used with strangers, elders, and officials. Привет (pri-VYET) is the informal hi, for friends and people your age. Time-of-day greetings: Доброе утро (DOB-ra-ye UT-ra) — good morning; Добрый день (DOB-ry dyen) — good afternoon; Добрый вечер (DOB-ry VYE-cher) — good evening; Спокойной ночи (spa-KOY-noy NO-chi) — good night (used when parting for the night, not as a greeting). For goodbyes: До свидания (da svi-DA-ni-ya) is the neutral goodbye, and Пока (pa-KA) is the informal bye. A classic beginner mistake is saying спокойной ночи as a greeting when arriving somewhere in the evening — use добрый вечер for that.

For introductions: Меня зовут... (mi-NYA za-VOOT) means "my name is" — literally "me they call". Как вас зовут? (kak vas za-VOOT) asks someone's name formally; Как тебя зовут? (kak ti-BYA za-VOOT) is the informal version for friends. Я из... (ya iz) means "I am from" — Я из Техаса (ya iz te-KHA-sa, I am from Texas). Очень приятно (O-chen pri-YAT-na) means "nice to meet you" and works for everyone. To ask how someone is: Как дела? (kak di-LA) — "how are things?" Answer with Хорошо, спасибо (kha-ra-SHO spa-SI-ba, good, thanks) or Нормально (nar-MAL-na, okay). Спасибо (spa-SI-ba) is thank you, пожалуйста (pa-ZHA-luy-sta) is please — and also "you're welcome" in response to thanks. Да (da) is yes, Нет (nyet) is no. Извините (iz-vi-NI-tye) is excuse me / sorry (formal); Простите (pra-STI-tye) is also "sorry".

Russian has a formal/informal you — Вы (vy) versus ты (ty) — and using the wrong one is a social mistake, not just a grammar mistake. Use Вы with strangers, anyone older, and in shops, banks, and offices. Switch to ты only when the other person does, or with friends and children. Every phrase in this lesson is given in the Вы form unless marked informal; that is the safe default. A second common mistake: answering Как дела? with a long life story. A short answer plus А у вас? (a u vas, and you? — formal) or А у тебя? (informal) keeps things balanced and doubles your practice.

Survival phrases are your safety net — memorize these before anything else. Я не понимаю (ya nye pa-ni-MA-yu) — I don't understand. Повторите, пожалуйста (paf-ta-RI-tye, pa-ZHA-luy-sta) — please repeat. Говорите медленнее, пожалуйста (ga-va-RI-tye MYED-lyen-ny-ye) — speak more slowly, please. Вы говорите по-английски? (vy ga-va-RI-tye pa-an-GLIY-ski) — do you speak English? Что это значит? (shto E-ta ZNA-chit) — what does this mean? Где...? (gdye) — where is...? Помогите! (pa-ma-GI-tye) — help! These seven phrases will rescue you anywhere Russian is spoken, and people respect learners who ask for clarification instead of nodding blankly.

Full dialogue — a first meeting at a conference. Read aloud with the translations:

— Здравствуйте! (ZDRAV-stvuy-tye) — Hello!
— Здравствуйте! Меня зовут Мария. А вас? (mi-NYA za-VOOT ma-RI-ya) — Hello! My name is Maria. And you?
— Меня зовут Джон. Очень приятно. (O-chen pri-YAT-na) — My name is John. Nice to meet you.
— Очень приятно. Вы откуда? (vy at-KU-da) — Nice to meet you. Where are you from?
— Я из Канады. А вы? (ya iz ka-NA-dy) — I am from Canada. And you?
— Я из России. Вы говорите по-русски? (ya iz ra-SI-i. vy ga-va-RI-tye pa-RU-ski) — I am from Russia. Do you speak Russian?
— Немного. Говорите медленнее, пожалуйста. (nye-MNO-ga) — A little. Speak more slowly, please.
— Конечно. Как дела? (ka-NYECH-na) — Of course. How are you?
— Хорошо, спасибо. До свидания! (kha-ra-SHO) — Good, thanks. Goodbye!

Practice: Write a self-introduction of five lines (greeting, name, where you are from, one feeling word, goodbye) and memorize it — this is your personal script for the next month. Then drill the survival phrases: cover the Russian column, read the English, and say the Russian from memory until you can do all seven without peeking. Finally, role-play the dialogue above twice, switching roles, saying every line aloud with the pronunciation rules from Lesson 1.
