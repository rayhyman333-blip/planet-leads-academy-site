This lesson gives you the past tense so you can say what you did, where you went, and what happened. Without a past tense you are stuck in the eternal present; with it, you can tell stories, explain your day, and answer the most common small-talk question in any language: Что вы делали вчера? (shto vy DYE-la-li fchye-RA, what did you do yesterday?). Russian past tense is refreshingly simple: one form per gender, no person-by-person conjugation.

Forming the past: take the infinitive, drop -ть, and add -л for masculine, -ла for feminine, -ло for neuter, -ли for plural. Говорить → говорил/говорила/говорили (ga-va-RIL/ga-va-RI-la). So: Я говорил (ya ga-va-RIL, I spoke — said by a man), Я говорила (ya ga-va-RI-la, I spoke — said by a woman), Они говорили (a-NI ga-va-RI-li, they spoke). More: работать → работал/работала — Вчера я работал допоздна (fchye-RA ya ra-BO-tal da-paz-DA, yesterday I worked late). Делать → делал/делала — Что ты делал? (what were you doing?). Жить → жил/жила — Я жил в Москве (I lived in Moscow). The verb "to be" in the past is был/была/было/были: Вчера был хороший день (fchye-RA byl kha-RO-shy dyen', yesterday was a good day); Она была дома (she was at home). Notice the past tense agrees with the subject's gender, not the speaker's politeness level — был for a man, была for a woman, always.

A few verbs change their stem in the past — learn these as set phrases. Идти (to go, on foot) → шёл/шла/шли: Я шёл в магазин (ya shyol f ma-ga-ZIN, I was going/went to the shop). Ехать (to go by vehicle) → ехал/ехала: Мы ехали на автобусе (we went by bus). Мочь (to be able) → мог/могла: Я не мог прийти (ya nye mok priy-TI, I couldn't come). Есть (to eat) → ел/ела: Мы ели суп (we ate soup). Хотеть → хотел/хотела: Я хотел позвонить (I wanted to call). These cover most daily storytelling.

Time words are your best friends in the past tense: вчера (fchye-RA, yesterday), позавчера (pa-za-fchye-RA, the day before yesterday), сегодня утром (this morning), на прошлой неделе (na PROSH-lay nye-DYE-lye, last week), в прошлом году (f PROSH-lam ga-DU, last year), тогда (tag-DA, then). Two beginner warnings. First, Russian has two verb aspects — imperfective (ongoing/repeated) and perfective (completed) — and this lesson uses imperfective past forms, which is correct for describing what was happening or what you used to do. Do not worry about the perfective yet; using imperfective past for everything at first is understandable and will not block communication. Second, negation: Я не говорил (I didn't speak), Она не была (she wasn't) — не goes right before the verb, exactly like the present.

Dialogue — talking about the weekend:

— Что вы делали в выходные? (shto vy DYE-la-li v vy-khad-NY-ye) — What did you do on the weekend?
— Я ездил на дачу с семьёй. (YA YEZ-dil na DA-chu s syem-YOY) — I went to the country house with my family.
— Как было? Была хорошая погода? (kak BY-la? by-LA kha-RO-sha-ya pa-GO-da) — How was it? Was the weather good?
— Да, было очень тепло. Мы гуляли и ели шашлык. (TYOP-la, gu-LYA-li) — Yes, it was very warm. We walked and ate barbecue.
— Как хорошо! А я остался дома. Я смотрел фильм и читал книгу. (a-STAL-sya DO-ma, sma-TRYEL fil'm) — How nice! I stayed home. I watched a film and read a book.
— Тебе понравился фильм? (tye-BYE pa-NRA-vil-sya fil'm) — Did you like the film?
— Да, очень понравился. А что ты делал в воскресенье? — Yes, I liked it a lot. What did you do Sunday?
— Я спал допоздна и ходил к родителям. (spal da-paz-DA, kha-DIL k ra-di-TYE-lyam) — I slept in and went to my parents'.

Practice: Write a paragraph of 8–10 sentences about what you did yesterday, using at least five different past-tense verbs (mix masculine/feminine forms correctly for yourself). Read it aloud twice. Then write the past forms of: говорить, работать, жить, быть, идти — all four genders/numbers each. Finally, answer these aloud in complete Russian sentences: Что вы ели вчера? Во сколько вы встали? Что вы делали в прошлые выходные? Вы смотрели фильм на этой неделе?
