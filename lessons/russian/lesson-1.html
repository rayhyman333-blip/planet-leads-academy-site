This lesson teaches you to read the Cyrillic alphabet and pronounce Russian clearly from the start. The alphabet has 33 letters, and the good news is that about a third of them look and sound like English letters, another group looks familiar but sounds different, and only a handful are completely new. Your goal in this lesson is not a perfect accent — it is clarity. If you can read the letters and handle the five key pronunciation rules below, Russian speakers will understand you even while you are still learning words.

Start with the friendly group — letters that look and sound like English: А а (a, as in father), К к (k), М м (m), О о (o, as in go), Т т (t). Words like мама (mama, mother) and кот (kot, cat) already look readable. Next, the false friends — letters that look like English but sound different, and these cause most beginner reading mistakes: В в sounds like V (not B), Е е sounds like YE (yes), Ё ё sounds like YO, Н н sounds like N (not H), Р р is a rolled R (not P), С с sounds like S (not C), Х х sounds like KH (a throaty sound, as in the Scottish word loch — not KS), У у sounds like OO (moon — not Y). Read these carefully: нет (nyet, no), ресторан (restoran, restaurant), хорошо (khorosho, good). The remaining letters are new shapes: Б б (b), Г г (g, as in go), Д д (d), Ж ж (zh, as in measure), З з (z), И и (ee, as in see), Й й (y, as in boy), Л л (l), П п (p), Ф ф (f), Ц ц (ts, as in cats), Ч ч (ch, as in chair), Ш ш (sh), Щ щ (shch, a long soft sh), Ъ ъ (hard sign — no sound of its own), Ы ы (a deep "i" sound made with the tongue pulled back), Ь ь (soft sign — softens the consonant before it), Э э (e, as in bet), Ю ю (yu), Я я (ya).

Now the five pronunciation rules that make you sound right. Rule 1: the unstressed O. When О is not stressed, it sounds like A. This is the single most audible marker of a beginner. Молоко (milk) is pronounced "muh-lah-KOH", not "moh-loh-koh". Москва (Moscow) is "mahsk-VAH". Rule 2: final devoicing. At the end of a word, voiced consonants go quiet: В sounds like F (хлеб, bread, is "khlyep"), Д sounds like T (город, city, is "gorat"), Б sounds like P, Ж sounds like SH, З sounds like S, Г sounds like K. Rule 3: Е, Ё, Ю, Я after a vowel or at the start of a word begin with a Y sound — моё (my) is "mah-YOH", я (I) is "ya". Rule 4: the soft sign Ь softens the consonant before it — compare брат (brat, brother) with брать (brat', to take), where the т becomes light and palatalized, almost with a tiny "y" after it. The hard sign Ъ does the opposite: it keeps the consonant hard before a Y-vowel, as in объект (ob-yekt, object). Rule 5: stress is free and it matters. Russian stress can fall on any syllable and it changes meaning: ЗАмок (zamok, lock) versus заМОК (zamok, castle). When you learn a new word, always learn its stress with it — dictionaries mark it, and this course marks stressed syllables in transliteration with capital letters.

The rolled R deserves its own note. Put the tip of your tongue behind your upper teeth and blow air so the tip vibrates — like a quick drum roll. Some learners get it in a day, others need weeks; both are normal. Until it comes, a single tapped R (like the quick "tt" in the American pronunciation of "butter") is close enough to be understood.

Here is your first dialogue. Read it aloud slowly, using the transliteration:

— Здравствуйте! (ZDRAV-stvuy-tye) — Hello! (formal)
— Здравствуйте! Как вас зовут? (kak vas za-VOOT) — Hello! What is your name? (formal)
— Меня зовут Анна. (mi-NYA za-VOOT AN-na) — My name is Anna.
— Очень приятно. Я Иван. (O-chen pri-YAT-na. ya i-VAN) — Nice to meet you. I am Ivan.
— До свидания! (da svi-DA-ni-ya) — Goodbye!
— До свидания! (da svi-DA-ni-ya) — Goodbye!

Notice the unstressed O rule working in здравствуйте and до свидания, and the final devoicing you can hear in the transliteration. Say the whole dialogue five times today; it is your first Russian conversation.

Practice: Write out all 33 letters by hand twice — handwriting cements the shapes. Then read these words aloud using the rules, checking yourself against the transliteration: мама (MA-ma), папа (PA-pa), дом (dom, house), нет (nyet, no), да (da, yes), книга (KNI-ga, book), стол (stol, table), вода (va-DA, water — hear the unstressed O), хлеб (khlyep, bread — hear the final devoicing), Москва (mahsk-VA). Finally, learn the stress of ten new words from this lesson by heart — say each one three times with the stress exaggerated, then normally.
