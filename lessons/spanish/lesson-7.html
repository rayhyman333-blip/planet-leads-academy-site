This lesson teaches you to talk about the future — making plans, accepting invitations, and handling two of the most useful real-world conversations: ordering in a restaurant and navigating travel. Future talk is what turns you from a tourist into someone who can actually live in Spanish: booking, inviting, promising, and planning.

Spanish has two easy ways to express the future. The everyday one is IR + A + INFINITIVE: Voy a estudiar (I'm going to study), Vamos a viajar (We are going to travel), ¿Vas a venir? (Are you coming?). Just conjugate ir (voy, vas, va, vamos, van) and add any infinitive — this one structure covers most future needs. The second is the simple future tense, formed by adding endings to the infinitive: hablaré, hablarás, hablará, hablaremos, hablarán (I/you/he/we/they will speak); comeré, viviré follow the same pattern. Use it for promises, predictions, and formal plans: Te llamaré mañana (I will call you tomorrow), Hará sol (It will be sunny). For this level, default to ir + a + infinitive in conversation and recognize the simple future when you hear it.

Restaurant vocabulary and phrases. La carta / el menú (the menu), el plato del día (dish of the day), la entrada (starter), el plato principal (main course), el postre (dessert), la bebida (drink). Ordering: Quisiera... (I would like... — polite), Para mí... (For me...), ¿Qué me recomienda? (What do you recommend?). During the meal: Está riquísimo (It's delicious), ¿Me trae...? (Can you bring me...?), La cuenta, por favor (The check, please). Common mistake: saying 'estoy caliente' to mean 'I'm hot' (temperature) — caliente with estar means aroused. Say tengo calor (I'm hot) and tengo frío (I'm cold). Another: ordering with 'quiero' barked at the waiter is grammatically fine but socially blunt — quisiera or me gustaría sounds much better.

Travel phrases. En el aeropuerto (at the airport): ¿Dónde está la puerta...? (Where is gate ...?), Mi vuelo sale a las... (My flight leaves at...), Tengo una reserva (I have a reservation). En el hotel: Quisiera una habitación doble (I'd like a double room), ¿A qué hora es el desayuno? (What time is breakfast?), ¿Tienen wifi? (Do you have wifi?). Problems: Perdí mi pasaporte (I lost my passport), Necesito ayuda (I need help), ¿Dónde hay una farmacia? (Where is there a pharmacy?). Note how much of this reuses Lessons 2–5 — the course is compounding.

Dialogue 1 — at a restaurant:

Camarero: Buenas noches. ¿Tienen reserva? — Good evening. Do you have a reservation?
Marta: Sí, a nombre de Marta. — Yes, under Marta.
Camarero: Por aquí, por favor. ¿Desean ver la carta? — This way, please. Would you like to see the menu?
Marta: Sí, gracias. ¿Cuál es el plato del día? — Yes, thanks. What is the dish of the day?
Camarero: Es pollo con arroz. Está riquísimo. — It's chicken with rice. It's delicious.
Marta: Quisiera eso, y una ensalada. ¿Y tú, Juan? — I'd like that, and a salad. And you, Juan?
Juan: Para mí, el pescado. ¿Me trae agua, por favor? — For me, the fish. Can you bring me water, please?
Camarero: Enseguida. — Right away.
(later) Marta: La cuenta, por favor. — The check, please.
Camarero: Son treinta y cinco dólares. — It's thirty-five dollars.

Dialogue 2 — making weekend plans:

Pablo: ¿Qué vas a hacer el sábado? — What are you going to do Saturday?
Carmen: Voy a visitar a mi hermana. ¿Y tú? — I'm going to visit my sister. And you?
Pablo: Voy a ir al mercado por la mañana. ¿Quieres venir? — I'm going to go to the market in the morning. Do you want to come?
Carmen: Sí, me gustaría. ¿A qué hora nos vemos? — Yes, I'd like to. What time shall we meet?
Pablo: A las nueve. Después vamos a almorzar juntos. — At nine. Afterwards we'll have lunch together.
Carmen: Perfecto. Te llamaré el viernes para confirmar. — Perfect. I'll call you Friday to confirm.

Practice: Write three plans for next week using ir + a + infinitive, and three promises using the simple future. Then write a full restaurant dialogue (8+ lines) where you order a starter, main, drink, and dessert, ask for the check, and react to the food. Say it all aloud. Finally, plan a real weekend in Spanish: where you will go, who you will see, what you will eat — five sentences minimum, out loud.
