This lesson gives you the past tense so you can say what you did, where you went, and what happened. Without a past tense you are stuck in the eternal present; with it, you can tell stories, explain your day, and answer the most common small-talk question in any language: きのうなにをしましたか (kinō nani o shimashita ka) — what did you do yesterday? Japanese past tense is wonderfully regular: change the ます ending you already know.

For verbs, replace ます with ました: たべます (I eat) → たべました (tabemashita, I ate). のみました (I drank), いきました (I went), きました (I came), しました (I did), みました (I watched), かいました (I bought), よみました (I read). The negative past replaces ません with ませんでした: たべませんでした (tabemasendeshita) — I didn't eat. For です sentences, です becomes でした: がくせいでした (gakusei deshita) — I was a student. The negative: じゃありませんでした (ja arimasendeshita) — I was not. Read those aloud and feel the pattern — one ending change covers every verb you know, with no irregular verbs to memorize at this level.

い-adjectives form the past by changing the final い to かった: たかい (expensive) → たかかった (takakatta, was expensive). おいしい → おいしかった (oishikatta, was delicious). あたらしい → あたらしかった (was new). The negative past: たかくなかった (takakunakatta, was not expensive). な-adjectives and nouns use でした: きれいでした (kirei deshita, was pretty), しずかでした (shizuka deshita, was quiet), たのしかったです is actually an い-adjective — たのしい (tanoshii, fun) → たのしかった (tanoshikatta, was fun). Two stars of past-tense conversation: よかったです (yokatta desu, it was good) and たのしかったです (it was fun) — you will use these constantly when talking about your weekend.

Time words anchor the past: きのう (kinō, yesterday), おととい (ototoi, the day before yesterday), せんしゅう (senshū, last week), せんげつ (sengetsu, last month), きょねん (kyonen, last year), さっき (sakki, a little while ago). Combine them with the past tense: きのうえいがをみました (kinō eiga o mimashita) — I watched a movie yesterday. せんしゅうとうきょうへいきました (senshū tōkyō e ikimashita) — I went to Tokyo last week. You can also list several past actions with 〜たり〜たりします: えいがをみたり、かいものをしたりしました (eiga o mitari, kaimono o shitari shimashita) — I did things like watching a movie and shopping. This "doing things like X and Y" pattern is extremely natural in conversation.

Two beginner warnings. First, don't use the past tense for things still true now: if you still live in Osaka, say おおさかにすんでいます, not すみました. The past tense means finished and over. Second, watch the い-adjective past spelling: たかい → たかかった doubles the か — writing たかいでした is the classic beginner error. For な-adjectives it really is でした (きれいでした), so keep the two families straight.

Dialogue — talking about the weekend:

Sora: しゅうまつはなにをしましたか。(Shūmatsu wa nani o shimashita ka.) — What did you do on the weekend?
Hana: どようびにとうきょうへいきました。(Doyōbi ni tōkyō e ikimashita.) — I went to Tokyo on Saturday.
Sora: どうでしたか。たのしかったですか。(Dō deshita ka. Tanoshikatta desu ka.) — How was it? Was it fun?
Hana: はい、とてもたのしかったです。ともだちとすしをたべました。(Hai, totemo tanoshikatta desu. Tomodachi to sushi o tabemashita.) — Yes, it was really fun. I ate sushi with a friend.
Sora: いいですね。わたしはいえでえいがをみました。(Ī desu ne. Watashi wa ie de eiga o mimashita.) — Nice. I watched a movie at home.
Hana: おもしろかったですか。(Omoshirokatta desu ka.) — Was it interesting?
Sora: はい、とてもおもしろかったです。にちようびはかいものをしました。(Hai, totemo omoshirokatta desu. Nichiyōbi wa kaimono o shimashita.) — Yes, very interesting. On Sunday I went shopping.

Practice: Write a paragraph of 8–10 sentences about what you did yesterday, using at least five different past-tense verbs plus one い-adjective past and one でした sentence. Read it aloud twice. Then convert these to the past: のみます, かいます, よみます, たかいです, きれいです, がくせいです. Finally, answer aloud in complete Japanese sentences: きのうなにをたべましたか。せんしゅうどこへいきましたか。しゅうまつはたのしかったですか。Then write three more sentences using 〜たり〜たりしました to describe a busy day, and read your whole paragraph to a friend or record yourself and listen carefully for the ました endings on every verb.
