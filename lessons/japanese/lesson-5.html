This lesson turns you into someone who can get answers: how to use every question word, ask for directions, handle a full shopping conversation, and keep small talk going. Up to now you have mostly made statements; questions are what make you independent in Japan. The good news: Japanese questions are simple — add か to a statement with rising intonation, and question words just slot into the sentence where the answer would go.

The core question words: だれ (dare, who), なに/なん (nani/nan, what), どこ (doko, where), いつ (itsu, when), なぜ/どうして (naze/dōshite, why), どうやって (dōyatte, how — by what means), いくら (ikura, how much), どれ (dore, which one), どの (dono, which — before a noun), いくつ (ikutsu, how many). One rule to memorize: use なん before words starting with d, t, or n sounds, and なに everywhere else. なんですか (nan desu ka) — what is it? なんじですか (nanji desu ka) — what time is it? なにをたべますか (nani o tabemasu ka) — what will you eat? Question words never take は — they take が: だれがきましたか (dare ga kimashita ka) — who came? A classic beginner mistake is だれはきましたか, which sounds wrong to every Japanese ear.

Directions vocabulary is survival-level. 〜はどこですか (〜 wa doko desu ka) — where is 〜? えきはどこですか (eki wa doko desu ka) — where is the station? みぎ (migi, right), ひだり (hidari, left), まっすぐ (massugu, straight), ちかく (chikaku, near), とおく (tōku, far), かど (kado, corner), しんごう (shingō, traffic light). Ask how to get somewhere: とうきょうえきへいくにはどうやって (tōkyō eki e iku ni wa dōyatte) — how do I get to Tokyo station? If you are lost: みちにまよいました (michi ni mayoimashita) — I'm lost. The lifesaver follow-up: ちずをみせてください (chizu o misete kudasai) — please show me on the map. When someone answers, listen for みぎにいってください (please go right) and ひだりをまがってください (please turn left).

Shopping questions beyond prices use これ/それ/あれ (this one near me / that one near you / that one over there): これはなんですか (kore wa nan desu ka) — what is this? それはいくらですか (sore wa ikura desu ka) — how much is that? あれをみせてください (are o misete kudasai) — please show me that one over there. Sizes: おおきいサイズはありますか (ōkii saizu wa arimasu ka) — do you have a large size? Payment: カードでおねがいします (kādo de onegai shimasu) — by card, please. Bargaining politely: もうすこしやすくしてください (mō sukoshi yasuku shite kudasai) — please make it a little cheaper. Small-talk questions that keep conversations alive: しゅみはなんですか (shumi wa nan desu ka) — what is your hobby? どこからきましたか (doko kara kimashita ka) — where did you come from? にほんははじめてですか (nihon wa hajimete desu ka) — is this your first time in Japan? なにがすきですか (nani ga suki desu ka) — what do you like?

Dialogue 1 — asking directions:

Tourist: すみません。えきはどこですか。(Sumimasen. Eki wa doko desu ka.) — Excuse me. Where is the station?
Local: あそこです。まっすぐいって、かどをみぎにまがってください。(Asoko desu. Massugu itte, kado o migi ni magatte kudasai.) — Over there. Go straight and turn right at the corner.
Tourist: あるいてとおいですか。(Aruite tōi desu ka.) — Is it far on foot?
Local: いいえ、ちかいです。ごふんぐらいです。(Īe, chikai desu. Gofun gurai desu.) — No, it's close. About five minutes.
Tourist: ありがとうございます。(Arigatō gozaimasu.) — Thank you very much.

Dialogue 2 — small talk at a café:

Emi: しゅみはなんですか。(Shumi wa nan desu ka.) — What is your hobby?
Dan: えいががすきです。あなたは。(Eiga ga suki desu. Anata wa.) — I like movies. And you?
Emi: わたしはりょうりがすきです。にほんははじめてですか。(Watashi wa ryōri ga suki desu. Nihon wa hajimete desu ka.) — I like cooking. Is this your first time in Japan?
Dan: はい、はじめてです。らいしゅうきょうとへいきます。(Hai, hajimete desu. Raishū kyōto e ikimasu.) — Yes, my first time. Next week I'm going to Kyoto.
Emi: きょうとはとてもきれいですよ。(Kyōto wa totemo kirei desu yo.) — Kyoto is very beautiful.
Dan: たのしみです。(Tanoshimi desu.) — I'm looking forward to it.

Practice: Write ten questions — two each for directions, shopping, time, personal info, and opinions — and say each aloud with rising intonation on か. Memorize the direction sequence (まっすぐ, みぎ, ひだり, かど) and give directions from your front door to the nearest shop, in Japanese, out loud. Finally, role-play Dialogue 2 with changed details (your hobby, your next trip) until the question patterns feel automatic.
