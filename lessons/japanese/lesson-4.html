This lesson builds your everyday number vocabulary: counting, telling time, and using days, months, and dates. Numbers are pure utility — prices, phone numbers, addresses, schedules — and telling the time unlocks appointments, opening hours, and travel. Japanese has a few sound changes in its numbers that you must learn exactly, so this lesson is built for repetition and out-loud drilling.

Numbers 1–10: いち (ichi, 1), に (ni, 2), さん (san, 3), よん (yon, 4 — し shi is avoided because it also means death), ご (go, 5), ろく (roku, 6), なな (nana, 7 — しち shichi also exists), はち (hachi, 8), きゅう (kyū, 9 — く ku also exists), じゅう (jū, 10). From 11 up, combine: じゅういち (jūichi, 11), にじゅう (nijū, 20), さんじゅうご (sanjūgo, 35). Hundreds: ひゃく (hyaku, 100) — but watch the sound changes: さんびゃく (sanbyaku, 300), ろっぴゃく (roppyaku, 600), はっぴゃく (happyaku, 800). Thousands: せん (sen, 1000) — さんぜん (sanzen, 3000), はっせん (hassen, 8000). Ten thousand: いちまん (ichiman, 10000) — Japanese counts in ten-thousands, so 100000 is じゅうまん (jūman). Drill these changes until they are automatic; they appear in every price you will ever hear.

Counting things uses counters, and the counter changes with what you count. The general counter 〜つ (tsu) works for small numbers of objects: ひとつ (hitotsu, 1), ふたつ (futatsu, 2), みっつ (mittsu, 3), よっつ (yottsu, 4), いつつ (itsutsu, 5), むっつ (muttsu, 6), ななつ (nanatsu, 7), やっつ (yattsu, 8), ここのつ (kokonotsu, 9), とお (tō, 10). People: ひとり (hitori, 1 person), ふたり (futari, 2 people), then さんにん (sannin, 3 people), よにん (yonin, 4). Age: 〜さい — にじゅうごさい (nijūgosai, 25 years old). Flat things like paper and shirts: 〜まい — さんまい (sanmai, 3). Long things like bottles and pencils: 〜ほん — いっぽん (ippon, 1), さんぼん (sanbon, 3). Books: 〜さつ — いっさつ (issatsu, 1). Times/frequency: 〜かい — いっかい (ikkai, once). The pattern ひとつ/ふたつ/みっつ is worth memorizing cold, because it is the fallback when you forget a specific counter.

Telling time: add じ (ji) to the number — いちじ (ichiji, 1 o'clock), にじ (niji, 2), さんじ (sanji, 3) — but memorize the three irregulars: よじ (yoji, 4 o'clock), しちじ (shichiji, 7 o'clock), くじ (kuji, 9 o'clock). Minutes use ふん/ぷん: いっぷん (ippun, 1 minute), にふん (nifun, 2), さんぷん (sanpun, 3), よんぷん (yonpun, 4), ろっぷん (roppun, 6), はっぷん (happun, 8), じゅっぷん (juppun, 10). はん (han) means half: さんじはん (sanjihan, 3:30). ごぜん (gozen) is AM, ごご (gogo) is PM: ごごさんじ (gogo sanji, 3 PM). Ask the time with いまなんじですか (ima nanji desu ka) — what time is it now? A classic mistake is saying いちふん for one minute — the ぷん sound change is mandatory: いっぷん.

Days of the week: にちようび (nichiyōbi, Sunday), げつようび (getsuyōbi, Monday), かようび (kayōbi, Tuesday), すいようび (suiyōbi, Wednesday), もくようび (mokuyōbi, Thursday), きんようび (kinyōbi, Friday), どようび (doyōbi, Saturday). きょう (kyō) is today, あした (ashita) is tomorrow, きのう (kinō) is yesterday. Months: いちがつ (ichigatsu, January) through じゅうにがつ (jūnigatsu, December), with three irregulars: しがつ (shigatsu, April), しちがつ (shichigatsu, July), くがつ (kugatsu, September). Dates of the month reuse the 〜つ counting for days 1–10: ついたち (tsuitachi, the 1st), ふつか (futsuka, 2nd), みっか (mikka, 3rd), よっか (yokka, 4th), いつか (itsuka, 5th), むいか (muika, 6th), なのか (nanoka, 7th), ようか (yōka, 8th), ここのか (kokonoka, 9th), とおか (tōka, 10th) — then じゅうよっか (jūyokka, 14th), はつか (hatsuka, 20th), にじゅうよっか (nijūyokka, 24th). So May 5th is ごがついつか (gogatsu itsuka).

Money and shopping vocabulary, since numbers live there: いくらですか (ikura desu ka) — how much is it? 〜えん (en) marks yen: せんえん (sen'en, 1000 yen). たかいですね (takai desu ne) — it's expensive, isn't it. やすいですね (yasui desu ne) — it's cheap, isn't it. これをください (kore o kudasai) — this one, please.

Dialogue — at a shop:

Customer: すみません。これはいくらですか。(Sumimasen. Kore wa ikura desu ka.) — Excuse me. How much is this?
Clerk: さんぜんえんです。(Sanzen'en desu.) — It's 3000 yen.
Customer: たかいですね。もうすこしやすいのはありますか。(Takai desu ne. Mō sukoshi yasui no wa arimasu ka.) — That's expensive. Do you have a slightly cheaper one?
Clerk: はい、これです。にせんえんです。(Hai, kore desu. Nisen'en desu.) — Yes, this one. It's 2000 yen.
Customer: じゃあ、これをふたつください。(Jā, kore o futatsu kudasai.) — Then I'll take two of these, please.
Clerk: ありがとうございます。よんせんえんです。(Arigatō gozaimasu. Yonsen'en desu.) — Thank you. That's 4000 yen.

Practice: Count aloud from 1 to 100 without stopping, then backwards from 50 to 1. Write today's date, your birthday, and three appointment times in Japanese. Drill the irregular times (よじ, しちじ, くじ) and minutes (いっぷん, さんぷん, ろっぷん, はっぷん, じゅっぷん) until they are automatic. Finally, memorize the shop dialogue's price lines and role-play buying three different items, asking いくらですか each time.
