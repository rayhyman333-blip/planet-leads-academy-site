This lesson teaches the sentence pattern behind most polite Japanese: the です (desu) and ます (masu) forms, plus the small particles that mark each word's job. Japanese word order is subject-object-verb — the verb always comes last — and particles, not word order, tell you who did what to whom. Master this lesson and you can build thousands of correct sentences with vocabulary you pick up anywhere.

The core pattern is XはYです (X wa Y desu) — "as for X, it is Y." わたしはがくせいです (watashi wa gakusei desu) — I am a student. これはほんです (kore wa hon desu) — this is a book. きょうはあついです (kyō wa atsui desu) — today is hot. The は (pronounced "wa") marks the topic — what the sentence is about. Make it negative with じゃありません: わたしはせんせいじゃありません (watashi wa sensei ja arimasen) — I am not a teacher. Make it a question by adding か at the end with rising intonation: がくせいですか (gakusei desu ka) — are you a student? Answer はい、がくせいです (yes, I am) or いいえ、がくせいじゃありません (no, I am not).

Particles are the grammar's engine. が (ga) marks the subject of a specific action or the thing that is: ねこがすきです (neko ga suki desu) — I like cats. を (pronounced "o") marks the direct object: すしをたべます (sushi o tabemasu) — I eat sushi. に (ni) marks a destination, a target, or a time: とうきょうにいきます (tōkyō ni ikimasu) — I go to Tokyo; さんじにあいます (sanji ni aimasu) — I meet at 3 o'clock. へ (pronounced "e") also marks direction: にほんへいきます (nihon e ikimasu) — I go to Japan. で (de) marks the place where an action happens or the means used: レストランでたべます (resutoran de tabemasu) — I eat at a restaurant; はしでたべます (hashi de tabemasu) — I eat with chopsticks. と (to) means "and" between nouns or "with" a person: ともだちときました (tomodachi to kimashita) — I came with a friend. の (no) shows possession like 's: わたしのほん (watashi no hon) — my book. も (mo) means "also": わたしもいきます (watashi mo ikimasu) — I will go too.

Present-tense verbs in polite speech end in ます. Learn these eight deeply, because they appear everywhere: たべます (tabemasu, eat), のみます (nomimasu, drink), いきます (ikimasu, go), きます (kimasu, come), します (shimasu, do), みます (mimasu, see/watch), かいます (kaimasu, buy), よみます (yomimasu, read). The negative replaces ます with ません: たべません (tabemasen) — I don't eat. The past (Lesson 6) changes ます to ました. A full sentence: まいにちコーヒーをのみます (mainichi kōhī o nomimasu) — I drink coffee every day. Notice the verb is last, the object takes を, and the time word まいにち (every day) floats near the front.

Adjectives come in two families. い-adjectives end in い and describe directly: たかい (takai, expensive/high), やすい (yasui, cheap), おいしい (oishii, delicious), あたらしい (atarashii, new), おおきい (ōkii, big), ちいさい (chiisai, small). な-adjectives need な before a noun: きれい (kirei, pretty/clean), しずか (shizuka, quiet), ゆうめい (yūmei, famous), べんり (benri, convenient). In polite sentences both take です: このケーキはおいしいです (kono kēki wa oishii desu) — this cake is delicious. とうきょうはべんりです (tōkyō wa benri desu) — Tokyo is convenient. この/その/あの (this/that/that over there) go before nouns: このほん (this book), そのかばん (that bag).

Two beginner mistakes to avoid. First, dropping particles because English doesn't need them — すしたべます is understandable but wrong; すしをたべます is correct. Particles are not decoration; they are the grammar. Second, putting the verb anywhere but last. English habits produce わたしはたべますすし (watashi wa tabemasu sushi) — always rearrange to わたしはすしをたべます.

Dialogue — two coworkers introducing themselves:

Aya: はじめまして。わたしはあやです。かいしゃいんです。(Hajimemashite. Watashi wa Aya desu. Kaishain desu.) — Nice to meet you. I am Aya. I am an office worker.
Riku: はじめまして。わたしはりくです。がくせいです。(Hajimemashite. Watashi wa Riku desu. Gakusei desu.) — Nice to meet you. I am Riku. I am a student.
Aya: まいにちでんしゃでかいしゃへいきます。(Mainichi densha de kaisha e ikimasu.) — I go to the office by train every day.
Riku: わたしはあるいてがっこうへいきます。(Watashi wa aruite gakkō e ikimasu.) — I walk to school.
Aya: がっこうはとおいですか。(Gakkō wa tōi desu ka.) — Is the school far?
Riku: いいえ、ちかいです。とてもべんりです。(Īe, chikai desu. Totemo benri desu.) — No, it's close. It's very convenient.

Practice: Write ten sentences using XはYです with words about yourself (name, job, origin, hobby). Then write eight verb sentences using the eight verbs above, each with a particle (を, に, へ, or で). Say every sentence aloud. Finally, translate to Japanese: "I drink coffee every day. I go to Tokyo by train. This book is interesting (おもしろい)." Check: まいにちコーヒーをのみます。でんしゃでとうきょうへいきます。このほんはおもしろいです。
