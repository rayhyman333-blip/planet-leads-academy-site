This lesson teaches you to talk about the future — making plans, expressing wants, inviting people — and handles two of the most useful real-world conversations: ordering in a restaurant and navigating travel. Future talk turns you from a visitor into someone who can actually live in Japanese: booking, inviting, promising, and planning.

Japanese has no separate future tense — the present ます form plus a future time word does the job: あしたとうきょうへいきます (ashita tōkyō e ikimasu) — I will go to Tokyo tomorrow. らいしゅうともだちにあいます (raishū tomodachi ni aimasu) — I will meet a friend next week. らいねんにほんへいきます (rainen nihon e ikimasu) — I will go to Japan next year. Future time words: あした (tomorrow), あさって (day after tomorrow), こんしゅう (this week), らいしゅう (next week), らいげつ (next month), らいねん (next year), こんばん (tonight).

To say you want to do something, change ます to たいです: たべたいです (tabetai desu) — I want to eat. にほんへいきたいです (nihon e ikitai desu) — I want to go to Japan. すしをたべたいです (sushi o tabetai desu) — I want to eat sushi. The negative is たくないです: いきたくないです (ikitakunai desu) — I don't want to go. Note that たいです only describes your own wants or asks about someone else's in a question — never state what a third person wants with たいです alone; use 〜たがっています for others, which this course mentions only so you recognize it.

Invitations have two friendly patterns. 〜ませんか invites: いっしょにたべませんか (issho ni tabemasen ka) — won't you eat with me? えいがをみませんか (eiga o mimasen ka) — shall we watch a movie? 〜ましょう proposes "let's": いきましょう (ikimashō) — let's go. たべましょう (tabemashō) — let's eat. Accept with いいですね、いきましょう (ii desu ne, ikimashō) — sounds good, let's go. Decline softly with ちょっと… (chotto…) — "a little…" — which every Japanese speaker understands as a polite no.

Restaurant phrases. The staff greets you with いらっしゃいませ (irasshaimase) — welcome. Ask for the menu: メニューをください (menyū o kudasai). Ask for a recommendation: おすすめはなんですか (osusume wa nan desu ka). Order with 〜をください or the softer 〜をおねがいします: すしをおねがいします (sushi o onegai shimasu). React to food: おいしいです (oishii desu) — delicious. Ask for the check: おかんじょうをおねがいします (okanjō o onegai shimasu). After eating, say ごちそうさまでした (gochisōsama deshita) — thank you for the meal. Common mistake: pointing and saying just これ (kore, this) — it works but sounds blunt; これをください is polite and natural.

Travel phrases. くうこう (kūkō, airport), ひこうき (hikōki, airplane), ホテル (hoteru, hotel), でんしゃ (densha, train), きっぷ (kippu, ticket), よやく (yoyaku, reservation). At the hotel: よやくがあります (yoyaku ga arimasu) — I have a reservation. チェックインをおねがいします (chekkuin o onegai shimasu) — check-in, please. あさごはんはなんじからですか (asagohan wa nanji kara desu ka) — from what time is breakfast? Problems: パスポートをなくしました (pasupōto o nakushimashita) — I lost my passport. たすけてください (tasukete kudasai) — please help me.

Dialogue 1 — at a restaurant:

Staff: いらっしゃいませ。なんめいさまですか。(Irasshaimase. Nanmeisama desu ka.) — Welcome. How many people?
Ren: ふたりです。(Futari desu.) — Two people.
Staff: こちらへどうぞ。メニューをどうぞ。(Kochira e dōzo. Menyū o dōzo.) — This way, please. Here is the menu.
Ren: おすすめはなんですか。(Osusume wa nan desu ka.) — What do you recommend?
Staff: さしみがおすすめです。(Sashimi ga osusume desu.) — The sashimi is recommended.
Ren: じゃあ、さしみとごはんをおねがいします。(Jā, sashimi to gohan o onegai shimasu.) — Then sashimi and rice, please.
(later) Ren: おいしかったです。おかんじょうをおねがいします。(Oishikatta desu. Okanjō o onegai shimasu.) — It was delicious. The check, please.
Staff: さんぜんごひゃくえんです。(Sanzen gohyaku'en desu.) — That's 3500 yen.

Dialogue 2 — making weekend plans:

Kaito: どようびなにをしますか。(Doyōbi nani o shimasu ka.) — What will you do Saturday?
Mei: ともだちとえいがをみたいです。あなたは。(Tomodachi to eiga o mitai desu. Anata wa.) — I want to watch a movie with a friend. And you?
Kaito: ぼくはかいものをしたいです。いっしょにいきませんか。(Boku wa kaimono o shitai desu. Issho ni ikimasen ka.) — I want to go shopping. Won't you come along?
Mei: いいですね。なんじにあいますか。(Ī desu ne. Nanji ni aimasu ka.) — Sounds good. What time shall we meet?
Kaito: じゅうじにえきのまえであいましょう。(Jūji ni eki no mae de aimashō.) — Let's meet in front of the station at ten.
Mei: わかりました。たのしみです。(Wakarimashita. Tanoshimi desu.) — Got it. I'm looking forward to it.

Practice: Write three plans for next week using the ます form with future time words, and three wants using たいです. Write a full restaurant dialogue (8+ lines) where you enter, order food and a drink, react to the meal, and ask for the check. Say it all aloud. Finally, invite a friend somewhere in Japanese using 〜ませんか, and answer your own invitation with 〜ましょう.
