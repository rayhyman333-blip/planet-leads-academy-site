This lesson teaches you to talk about the future — making plans, accepting invitations, and handling two of the most useful real-world conversations: ordering in a restaurant and navigating travel. Future talk is what turns you from a tourist into someone who can actually live in Portuguese: booking, inviting, promising, and planning.

Portuguese has two easy ways to express the future. The everyday Brazilian one is IR + INFINITIVE: Vou estudar (I'm going to study), Vamos viajar (We are going to travel), Você vai vir? (Are you coming?). Just conjugate ir (vou, vai, vai, vamos, vão, vão) and add any infinitive — this one structure covers nearly all future needs in Brazil. The second is the futuro simples, formed by adding endings to the infinitive: falarei, falará, falará, falaremos, falarão (I/you/he/we/they will speak); comerei, partirei follow the same pattern. Irregular stems: ser → serei, ter → terei, fazer → farei, poder → poderei, querer → quererei, vir → virei. Use it for promises, predictions, and formal plans: Vou te ligar amanhã is more Brazilian; Te ligarei amanhã is more formal. For this level, default to ir + infinitive in conversation and recognize the futuro simples when you hear it.

Restaurant vocabulary and phrases. O cardápio (the menu), o prato do dia (dish of the day), a entrada (starter), o prato principal (main course), a sobremesa (dessert), a bebida (drink). Ordering: Eu gostaria... (I would like... — polite), Para mim... (For me...), O que você recomenda? (What do you recommend?). During the meal: Está uma delícia (It's delicious), Pode me trazer...? (Can you bring me...?), A conta, por favor (The check, please). Common mistake: ordering with 'eu quero' barked at the waiter is grammatically fine but socially blunt — eu gostaria or por favor with a smile sounds much better. Tipping: the 10% service charge (taxa de serviço) is usually added to the bill — check before tipping extra.

Travel phrases. No aeroporto (at the airport): Onde fica o portão...? (Where is gate...?), Meu voo sai às... (My flight leaves at...), Tenho uma reserva (I have a reservation). No hotel: Gostaria de um quarto duplo (I'd like a double room), A que horas é o café da manhã? (What time is breakfast?), Tem wifi? (Do you have wifi?). Problems: Perdi meu passaporte (I lost my passport), Preciso de ajuda (I need help), Onde tem uma farmácia? (Where is there a pharmacy?). Note how much of this reuses Lessons 2–5 — the course is compounding.

Dialogue 1 — at a restaurant:

Garçom: Boa noite. Tem reserva? — Good evening. Do you have a reservation?
Renata: Sim, no nome de Renata. — Yes, under Renata.
Garçom: Por aqui, por favor. Querem ver o cardápio? — This way, please. Would you like to see the menu?
Renata: Sim, obrigada. Qual é o prato do dia? — Yes, thanks. What is the dish of the day?
Garçom: É frango com arroz. Está uma delícia. — It's chicken with rice. It's delicious.
Renata: Gostaria disso, e uma salada. E você, Bruno? — I'd like that, and a salad. And you, Bruno?
Bruno: Para mim, o peixe. Pode me trazer água, por favor? — For me, the fish. Can you bring me water, please?
Garçom: Já vou trazer. — I'll bring it right away.
(depois) Renata: A conta, por favor. — The check, please.
Garçom: São trinta e cinco reais. — It's thirty-five reais.

Dialogue 2 — making weekend plans:

Felipe: O que você vai fazer no sábado? — What are you going to do Saturday?
Aline: Vou visitar minha irmã. E você? — I'm going to visit my sister. And you?
Felipe: Vou à feira de manhã. Quer vir? — I'm going to the market in the morning. Do you want to come?
Aline: Sim, adoraria. A que horas nos vemos? — Yes, I'd love to. What time shall we meet?
Felipe: Às nove. Depois vamos almoçar juntos. — At nine. Afterwards we'll have lunch together.
Aline: Perfeito. Vou te ligar na sexta para confirmar. — Perfect. I'll call you Friday to confirm.

Practice: Write three plans for next week using ir + infinitive, and three promises using the futuro simples. Then write a full restaurant dialogue (8+ lines) where you order a starter, main, drink, and dessert, ask for the check, and react to the food. Say it all aloud. Finally, plan a real weekend in Portuguese: where you will go, who you will see, what you will eat — five sentences minimum, out loud.
