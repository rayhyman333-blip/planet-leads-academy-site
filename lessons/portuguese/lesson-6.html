This lesson gives you the past tense — the pretérito perfeito — so you can say what you did, where you went, and what happened. Without a past tense you are stuck in the eternal present; with it, you can tell stories, explain your day, and answer the most common small-talk question in any language: O que você fez ontem? (What did you do yesterday?). The pretérito perfeito is for completed, finished actions with a clear end: yesterday, last week, this morning — and it is the everyday past tense of Brazilian speech.

Regular pretérito perfeito endings. For -AR verbs like falar: falei, falou, falou, falamos, falaram. For -ER verbs like comer: comi, comeu, comeu, comemos, comeram. For -IR verbs like partir: parti, partiu, partiu, partimos, partiram. Read those aloud and feel the rhythm — the endings carry the meaning: Falei com a Maria (I spoke with Maria). Comemos pizza (We ate pizza). Time words anchor the past: ontem (yesterday), ontem à noite (last night), semana passada (last week), ano passado (last year), hoje de manhã (this morning). In everyday Brazilian speech, você/ele/nós/vocês/eles forms are the ones you will hear most.

The verbs you use most in the past are irregular, so learn these as set phrases. IR (to go): fui, foi, foi, fomos, foram. Fui ao médico (I went to the doctor). SER shares fui/foi in the past — context decides: Foi um bom dia (It was a good day). TER: tive, teve, teve, tivemos, tiveram — Tive um problema (I had a problem). FAZER: fiz, fez, fez, fizemos, fizeram — Fiz a tarefa (I did the homework). ESTAR: estive, esteve, esteve, estivemos, estiveram — Estive doente (I was sick). DIZER (to say/tell): disse, disse, disse, dissemos, disseram — Ele me disse a verdade (He told me the truth). QUERER: quis, quis, quis, quisemos, quiseram — Quis te ligar (I tried to call you).

Two important beginner warnings. First, Portuguese has another past tense — the pretérito imperfeito, for ongoing and habitual past actions (falava = I used to speak / was speaking) — and this course teaches only the pretérito perfeito, which is enough for everyday conversation. Using the perfeito for everything at first is understandable and will not block communication. Second, watch the first-person ending: falei (I spoke) versus fala (he speaks) — one letter changes the person AND the tense. Write the endings carefully every time until your hand does it automatically.

Dialogue — talking about the weekend:

Larissa: O que você fez no fim de semana? — What did you do on the weekend?
Thiago: Fui à praia com minha família. — I went to the beach with my family.
Larissa: Como foi? Fez sol? — How was it? Was it sunny?
Thiago: Sim, fez muito sol. Nadamos e comemos peixe. — Yes, it was sunny all day. We swam and ate fish.
Larissa: Que bom! Eu fiquei em casa. Vi um filme e li um livro. — How nice! I stayed home. I watched a movie and read a book.
Thiago: Você gostou do filme? — Did you like the movie?
Larissa: Sim, adorei. O que você fez no domingo? — Yes, I loved it. What did you do Sunday?
Thiago: Dormi até tarde e visitei meus pais. — I slept in and visited my parents.

Practice: Write a paragraph of 8–10 sentences about what you did yesterday, using at least five different pretérito perfeito verbs (mix regular and irregular). Read it aloud twice. Then conjugate in writing: falar, comer, ir, ter, fazer — full pretérito perfeito, all persons. Finally, answer these aloud in complete Portuguese sentences: O que você comeu ontem? A que horas você acordou? O que você fez no fim de semana passado? Você viu um filme esta semana?
