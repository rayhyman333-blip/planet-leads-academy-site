This lesson gives you your first real social toolkit: greeting people politely, introducing yourself, and surviving conversations when you do not understand something. These phrases are the highest-value sentences in the language because they unlock every interaction — ordering food, asking for help, meeting people. Learn them cold before you worry about grammar, because even a flawless sentence is useless if you cannot open and close a conversation gracefully.

Start with greetings. Bom dia (boh-oom DEE-ah) means good morning. Boa tarde (BOH-ah TAHR-jee) is good afternoon, boa noite (BOH-ah NOY-chee) is good evening or good night. Oi (oy) is hi — warm, casual, and universal in Brazil. Olá (oh-LAH) is a slightly more neutral hello. Tchau (chow) is bye, até logo (ah-TEH LOH-goo) is see you later, and tudo bem? (TOO-doo beh-eem) means 'all good?' — the all-purpose greeting Brazilians actually use most. For introductions: Me chamo... (mee SHAH-moo) or Meu nome é... (meh-oo NOH-mee eh) means 'my name is'. Como você se chama? (KOH-moo voh-SEH see SHAH-mah) asks someone's name. Prazer (prah-ZEHR) means pleased to meet you; muito prazer is the warmer version.

Now the small-talk core. Como vai? (KOH-moo vigh) or Tudo bem? is 'how are you' — answer with Tudo bem (all good), Tudo ótimo (great), Mais ou menos (so-so), or Estou cansado (m) / cansada (f) (I am tired). E você? (ee voh-SEH) means 'and you?' — short and essential. Por favor (poor fah-VOHR) is please, obrigado/obrigada is thank you (men say obrigado, women say obrigada — match it to YOUR gender, not the listener's), de nada (jee NAH-dah) is you're welcome. A frequent beginner mistake is answering Tudo bem? with a long life story; a short answer plus E você? keeps the conversation balanced and gives you twice the practice.

Survival phrases are your safety net — memorize these before anything else. Não entendo (nah-oom ehn-TEHN-doo) — I don't understand. Pode repetir, por favor? (POH-jee heh-peh-CHEER) — Can you repeat, please? Fala mais devagar, por favor (FAH-lah migh jee-vah-GAHR) — Speak more slowly, please. Você fala inglês? (voh-SEH FAH-lah een-GLEHS) — Do you speak English? Com licença (koh-oom lee-SEHN-sah) — excuse me (to get attention); Desculpa (jees-KOOL-pah) — sorry (informal); Desculpe (jees-KOOL-pee) — sorry (formal). Sinto muito (SEEN-too MOOY-too) — I'm very sorry. O que significa...? (oo kee seen-yee-FEE-kah) — What does ... mean? These phrases will rescue you anywhere in Brazil, and people respect learners who ask for clarification instead of nodding blankly.

Watch for this social note: Brazilian Portuguese is warm and informal compared to European Portuguese — você is the standard 'you' everywhere in Brazil (tu is regional), and Brazilians happily use first names quickly. Physical warmth matters too: a hug or cheek kiss is normal among new acquaintances in social settings. A classic false friend: 'pasta' in Portuguese means folder or paste — the food is macarrão or massa. And 'parentes' means relatives, not parents (pais).

Full dialogue — a first meeting. Read aloud with the translations:

Camila: Oi! Tudo bem? Como você se chama? — Hi! All good? What is your name?
Diego: Oi! Tudo bem. Me chamo Diego. E você? — Hi! All good. My name is Diego. And you?
Camila: Me chamo Camila. Prazer. — My name is Camila. Pleased to meet you.
Diego: Prazer, Camila. De onde você é? — Pleased to meet you, Camila. Where are you from?
Camila: Sou do Texas. E você? — I'm from Texas. And you?
Diego: Sou de São Paulo. Você fala português? — I'm from São Paulo. Do you speak Portuguese?
Camila: Um pouco. Fala mais devagar, por favor. — A little. Speak more slowly, please.
Diego: Claro. Como você está hoje? — Of course. How are you today?
Camila: Tudo ótimo, obrigada. E você? — Great, thanks. And you?
Diego: Tudo ótimo. Tchau! — Great. Bye!

Practice: Write a self-introduction of five lines (greeting, name, where you are from, one feeling word, goodbye) and memorize it — this is your personal script for the next month. Then practice the survival phrases: cover the Portuguese column, read the English, and say the Portuguese from memory until you can do all seven without peeking. Finally, role-play the dialogue above twice, switching roles, saying every line aloud with the nasal vowels from Lesson 1.
