This lesson gives you your first real social toolkit: greeting people politely, introducing yourself, and surviving conversations when you do not understand something. These phrases are the highest-value sentences in the language because they unlock every interaction — ordering food, asking for help, meeting people. Learn them cold before you worry about grammar, because even a flawless sentence is useless if you cannot open and close a conversation gracefully. Dutch people are famously direct, and they appreciate learners who try Dutch first, even for a few sentences, before switching to English.

Start with greetings. Hallo (hello) works any time. Goedemorgen (good morning) is used until about noon, goedemiddag (good afternoon) from noon until about six, and goedenavond (good evening) after six. Hoi and doei are informal hi and bye among friends — never use them with a shopkeeper or official on first meeting. Tot ziens (until we see each other) is the standard goodbye, tot straks (see you later today) is for later the same day, and tot morgen (until tomorrow) is for tomorrow. Fijne dag (have a nice day) is what shopkeepers say as you leave. For introductions: Ik heet... (my name is) or Mijn naam is... (my name is). Hoe heet jij? asks someone's name informally; Hoe heet u? is the formal version. Aangenaam (pleased to meet you) and Leuk je te ontmoeten (nice to meet you) both work after introductions.

Now the small-talk core. Hoe gaat het? (How are you?) is the standard question; the casual short form is Hoe gaat ie? (How is it going?). Common answers: Goed, dank je (Good, thanks), Het gaat goed (It is going well), Prima (Fine, great), Niet zo goed (Not so good), Ik ben moe (I am tired). En met jou? (And with you?) or En met u? (formal) keeps the conversation balanced. Alstublieft is please (formal), alsjeblieft the informal version; dank je wel is thank you (informal), dank u wel is thank you (formal), heel erg bedankt is thank you very much, and graag gedaan (gladly done) or geen probleem (no problem) is you are welcome. A frequent beginner mistake is answering Hoe gaat het? with a long life story; a short answer plus En met jou? gives you twice the practice and sounds natural.

Survival phrases are your safety net — memorize these before anything else. Ik begrijp het niet (I do not understand). Kunt u dat herhalen, alstublieft? (Can you repeat that, please?). Spreek wat langzamer, alstublieft (Speak a bit more slowly, please). Spreekt u Engels? (Do you speak English?). Pardon (excuse me / sorry). Het spijt me (I am sorry). Wat betekent dat? (What does that mean?). Kunt u me helpen? (Can you help me?). Ik spreek een beetje Nederlands (I speak a little Dutch). These phrases will rescue you in any Dutch-speaking place, and people respect learners who ask for clarification instead of nodding blankly.

Watch for this social mistake: Dutch runs on the je/jij versus u distinction. Use u with strangers, shopkeepers, elders, and in any formal setting; switch to je or jij only when the other person does or with clear peers and friends. Using je with a stranger is not a grammar failure — it is a social one, and it can come across as too familiar. When in doubt, start with u. A second classic trap: saying ik ben warm to mean I am hot (temperature). Dutch uses hebben for that feeling — ik heb het warm (I have it warm) — which you will meet properly in Lesson 3. Saying ik ben warm sounds like something else entirely, so learn ik heb het warm and ik heb het koud (I am cold) as fixed phrases today.

Full dialogue — a first meeting. Read aloud with the translations:

Sanne: Goedemorgen! Hoe heet u? — Good morning! What is your name?
Tom: Goedemorgen. Ik heet Tom. En u? — Good morning. My name is Tom. And you?
Sanne: Ik heet Sanne. Aangenaam. — My name is Sanne. Pleased to meet you.
Tom: Aangenaam, Sanne. Waar komt u vandaan? — Pleased to meet you, Sanne. Where are you from?
Sanne: Ik kom uit Londen. En u? — I am from London. And you?
Tom: Ik kom uit Utrecht. Spreekt u Nederlands? — I am from Utrecht. Do you speak Dutch?
Sanne: Een beetje. Spreek wat langzamer, alstublieft. — A little. Speak a bit more slowly, please.
Tom: Natuurlijk. Hoe gaat het vandaag? — Of course. How are you today?
Sanne: Goed, dank u. En met u? — Good, thanks. And with you?
Tom: Goed. Tot ziens! — Good. Goodbye!

Practice: Write a self-introduction of five lines (greeting, name, where you are from, one feeling word, goodbye) and memorize it — this is your personal script for the next month. Then practice the survival phrases: cover the Dutch column, read the English, and say the Dutch from memory until you can do all eight without peeking. Finally, role-play the dialogue above twice, switching roles, saying every line aloud with the guttural g and diphthongs from Lesson 1.
