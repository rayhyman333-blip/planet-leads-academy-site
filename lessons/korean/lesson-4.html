This lesson builds your everyday number vocabulary: counting, telling time, and using days, months, and dates. Korean has TWO number systems — native Korean and Sino-Korean — and using the wrong one is the classic beginner giveaway. Learn which system goes where, and numbers become pure utility: prices, phone numbers, addresses, schedules.

Native Korean numbers (for counting things, age, hours): 하나 (ha-na, 1), 둘 (dul, 2), 셋 (set, 3), 넷 (net, 4), 다섯 (da-seot, 5), 여섯 (yeo-seot, 6), 일곱 (il-gop, 7), 여덟 (yeo-deol, 8), 아홉 (a-hop, 9), 열 (yeol, 10). Eleven is 열하나, twelve 열둘, twenty 스물 (seu-mul), thirty 서른 (seo-reun). Before a counter, 1–4 shorten: 한 (han), 두 (du), 세 (se), 네 (ne) — 한 개 (han gae, one thing), 두 명 (du myeong, two people), 세 살 (se sal, three years old).

Sino-Korean numbers (for money, minutes, dates, phone numbers): 일 (il, 1), 이 (i, 2), 삼 (sam, 3), 사 (sa, 4), 오 (o, 5), 육 (yuk, 6), 칠 (chil, 7), 팔 (pal, 8), 구 (gu, 9), 십 (ship, 10). Eleven is 십일, twenty is 이십, one hundred is 백 (baek), one thousand is 천 (cheon), ten thousand is 만 (man) — Korean counts in ten-thousands like Japanese: 십만 (ship-man) is 100,000.

Which system goes where — memorize this table. Native: age (스물다섯 살 — 25 years old), counting objects with counters (사과 세 개 — three apples), hours (세 시 — 3 o'clock). Sino: money (삼천 원 — 3000 won), minutes (삼십 분 — 30 minutes), months and dates (3월, 5일), phone numbers, floors. The hour/minute split is the famous one: 세 시 삼십 분 (se shi sam-ship bun) — 3:30 — native 세 for the hour, Sino 삼십 for the minutes. Mixing them (삼 시 세십 분) is wrong every time.

Counters pair with native numbers: 개 (gae, things), 명/분 (myeong/bun, people — 분 is respectful), 살 (sal, age), 잔 (jan, cups), 병 (byeong, bottles), 마리 (ma-ri, animals), 권 (gwon, books), 장 (jang, flat sheets), 대 (dae, machines/cars). Examples: 커피 두 잔 (keo-pi du jan — two cups of coffee), 책 세 권 (chaek se gwon — three books), 고양이 한 마리 (go-yang-i han ma-ri — one cat).

Telling time: native number + 시 (shi) for hours — 한 시 (1 o'clock), 두 시 (2), 열두 시 (12). Sino number + 분 (bun) for minutes — 십 분 (10 minutes), 사십오 분 (45 minutes). 반 (ban) means half: 세 시 반 (3:30). 오전 (o-jeon) is AM, 오후 (o-hu) is PM: 오후 세 시 (3 PM). Ask the time with 지금 몇 시예요? (ji-geum myeot shi-ye-yo? — what time is it now?). A classic mistake is saying 삼 시 for 3 o'clock — hours are native: 세 시.

Days of the week: 월요일 (wo-ryo-il, Monday), 화요일 (hwa-yo-il, Tuesday), 수요일 (su-yo-il, Wednesday), 목요일 (mo-gyo-il, Thursday), 금요일 (geu-myo-il, Friday), 토요일 (to-yo-il, Saturday), 일요일 (i-ryo-il, Sunday). 오늘 (o-neul) is today, 내일 (nae-il) is tomorrow, 어제 (eo-je) is yesterday. Months: Sino number + 월 (wol) — 1월 (i-rwol, January), 12월 (shi-bi-wol, December). Dates: Sino number + 일 (il) — 5일 (o-il, the 5th). So May 5th, Monday is 5월 5일 월요일 (o-wol o-il wo-ryo-il). The order is always biggest to smallest: year, month, day.

Money and shopping vocabulary: 얼마예요? (eol-ma-ye-yo? — how much is it?). 원 (won) is the currency: 삼천 원 (sam-cheon won, 3000 won). 비싸요 (bi-ssa-yo) — expensive. 싸요 (ssa-yo) — cheap. 이거 주세요 (i-geo ju-se-yo) — this one, please.

Dialogue — at a shop:

Customer: 이거 얼마예요? (I-geo eol-ma-ye-yo?) — How much is this?
Clerk: 삼천 원이에요. (Sam-cheon wo-ni-e-yo.) — It's 3000 won.
Customer: 비싸네요. 좀 싼 거 있어요? (Bi-ssa-ne-yo. Jom ssan geo i-sseo-yo?) — That's expensive. Do you have a cheaper one?
Clerk: 네, 이거는 이천 원이에요. (Ne, i-geo-neun i-cheon wo-ni-e-yo.) — Yes, this one is 2000 won.
Customer: 그럼 이거 두 개 주세요. (Geu-reom i-geo du gae ju-se-yo.) — Then give me two of these, please.
Clerk: 감사합니다. 사천 원입니다. (Gam-sa-ham-ni-da. Sa-cheon wo-nim-ni-da.) — Thank you. That's 4000 won.

Practice: Count aloud 1–20 in both systems without stopping. Write today's date, your birthday, and three appointment times in Korean. Drill the hour/minute split (세 시 삼십 분) until it is automatic. Finally, memorize the shop dialogue's price lines and role-play buying three different items, asking 얼마예요? each time.
