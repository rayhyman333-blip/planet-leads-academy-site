This lesson teaches you to talk about the future — making plans, expressing wants, inviting people — and handles two of the most useful real-world conversations: ordering in a restaurant and navigating travel. Future talk turns you from a visitor into someone who can actually live in Korean: booking, inviting, promising, and planning.

Korean marks the future with (으)ㄹ 거예요 attached to the verb stem: add ㄹ 거예요 after a vowel stem, 을 거예요 after a consonant stem. 가다 → 갈 거예요 (gal geo-ye-yo) — I will go. 먹다 → 먹을 거예요 (meo-geul geo-ye-yo) — I will eat. 하다 → 할 거예요 (hal geo-ye-yo) — I will do. 보다 → 볼 거예요 (bol geo-ye-yo) — I will watch. Future time words: 내일 (tomorrow), 모레 (the day after tomorrow), 이번 주 (this week), 다음 주 (next week), 다음 달 (next month), 내년 (next year), 오늘 저녁 (this evening). Combine freely: 내일 서울에 갈 거예요 (nae-il Seo-u-re gal geo-ye-yo) — I will go to Seoul tomorrow.

To say you want to do something, use -고 싶어요: 먹고 싶어요 (meok-go si-peo-yo) — I want to eat. 가고 싶어요 (ga-go si-peo-yo) — I want to go. 한국어를 배우고 싶어요 (han-gu-geo-reul bae-u-go si-peo-yo) — I want to learn Korean. The negative is -고 싶지 않아요: 가고 싶지 않아요 — I don't want to go. For suggestions, use -(으)ㄹ까요?: 같이 갈까요? (ga-chi gal-kka-yo? — shall we go together?). For "let's," use -(으)ㅂ시다 in formal speech or -자 in casual: 갑시다 (gap-shi-da) — let's go. Accept with 좋아요! (jo-a-yo! — sounds good!).

Restaurant phrases. The staff greets you with 어서 오세요 (eo-seo o-se-yo) — welcome. Ask for the menu: 메뉴 주세요 (me-nyu ju-se-yo). Ask for a recommendation: 뭐가 맛있어요? (mwo-ga ma-shi-sseo-yo? — what is delicious?). Order with ...주세요 or the softer 주문할게요 (ju-mun-hal-ge-yo — I'll order): 불고기 주세요 (bul-go-gi ju-se-yo). React to food: 맛있어요! (ma-shi-sseo-yo! — delicious!). After eating, say 잘 먹었습니다 (jal meo-geo-sseum-ni-da) — thank you for the meal. Ask for the bill: 계산서 주세요 (gye-san-seo ju-se-yo). Common mistake: shouting or waving wildly for attention — a raised hand with 여기요 (yeo-gi-yo, over here) is polite and effective.

Travel phrases. 공항 (gong-hang, airport), 비행기 (bi-haeng-gi, airplane), 호텔 (ho-tel, hotel), 기차 (gi-cha, train), 표 (pyo, ticket), 예약 (ye-yak, reservation). At the hotel: 예약했어요 (ye-ya-kae-sseo-yo) — I have a reservation. 체크인 하고 싶어요 (che-keu-in ha-go si-peo-yo) — I'd like to check in. 조식은 몇 시부터예요? (jo-shi-geun myeot shi-bu-teo-ye-yo? — from what time is breakfast?). Problems: 여권을 잃어버렸어요 (yeo-gwo-neul i-reo-beo-ryeo-sseo-yo) — I lost my passport. 도와주세요 (do-wa-ju-se-yo) — please help me.

Dialogue 1 — at a restaurant:

Staff: 어서 오세요. 몇 분이세요? (Eo-seo o-se-yo. Myeot bu-ni-se-yo?) — Welcome. How many people?
Seo: 두 명이에요. 창가 자리 있어요? (Du myeong-i-e-yo. Chang-ga ja-ri i-sseo-yo?) — Two people. Do you have a window seat?
Staff: 네, 이쪽으로 오세요. 메뉴입니다. (Ne, i-jjo-geu-ro o-se-yo. Me-nyu-im-ni-da.) — Yes, this way please. Here is the menu.
Seo: 뭐가 맛있어요? (Mwo-ga ma-shi-sseo-yo?) — What is delicious?
Staff: 불고기가 인기 많아요. (Bul-go-gi-ga in-gi ma-na-yo.) — Bulgogi is very popular.
Seo: 그럼 불고기하고 밥 두 공기 주문할게요. (Geu-reom bul-go-gi-ha-go bap du gong-gi ju-mun-hal-ge-yo.) — Then I'll order bulgogi and two bowls of rice.
(later) Seo: 잘 먹었습니다! 계산서 주세요. (Jal meo-geo-sseum-ni-da! Gye-san-seo ju-se-yo.) — Thank you for the meal! The check, please.
Staff: 만 오천 원입니다. (Ma-no-cheon wo-nim-ni-da.) — That's 15,000 won.

Dialogue 2 — making weekend plans:

Yuna: 토요일에 뭐 할 거예요? (To-yo-i-re mwo hal geo-ye-yo?) — What will you do Saturday?
Jiho: 공원에 가고 싶어요. 유나 씨는요? (Gong-wo-ne ga-go si-peo-yo. Yu-na sshi-neun-yo?) — I want to go to the park. And you, Yuna?
Jiho: 저는 쇼핑하고 싶어요. 같이 갈까요? (Jeo-neun syo-ping-ha-go si-peo-yo. Ga-chi gal-kka-yo?) — I want to go shopping. Shall we go together?
Yuna: 좋아요! 몇 시에 만날까요? (Jo-a-yo! Myeot shi-e man-nal-kka-yo?) — Sounds good! What time shall we meet?
Jiho: 열 시에 역 앞에서 만나요. (Yeol shi-e yeok a-pe-seo man-na-yo.) — Let's meet in front of the station at ten.
Yuna: 알겠어요. 기대돼요! (Al-ge-sseo-yo. Gi-dae-dwae-yo!) — Got it. I'm looking forward to it!

Practice: Write three plans for next week using (으)ㄹ 거예요, and three wants using -고 싶어요. Write a full restaurant dialogue (8+ lines) where you enter, order two dishes and drinks, react to the food, and ask for the bill. Say it all aloud. Finally, invite a friend somewhere in Korean using -(으)ㄹ까요?, and answer your own invitation.
