This lesson gives you your first real social toolkit: greeting people politely, introducing yourself, and surviving conversations when you do not understand something. These phrases are the highest-value sentences in the language because they unlock every interaction — ordering food, asking for help, meeting people. Learn them cold before you worry about grammar, because even a flawless sentence is useless if you cannot open and close a conversation gracefully.

The universal greeting is 안녕하세요 (an-nyeong-ha-se-yo) — hello. It works morning, noon, and night, with everyone. When parting: 안녕히 가세요 (an-nyeong-hi ga-se-yo) to someone who is leaving, 안녕히 계세요 (an-nyeong-hi gye-se-yo) to someone who is staying — mixing these up is the classic beginner error, so learn the pair together: 가세요 = go well (you leave), 계세요 = stay well (you stay). For introductions: 처음 뵙겠습니다 (cheo-eum boep-ge-sseum-ni-da) — nice to meet you (formal, first meeting). 저는 민준입니다 (jeo-neun Minjun-im-ni-da) — I am Minjun (use your own name). 저는 [place]에서 왔어요 (jeo-neun [place]-e-seo wa-sseo-yo) — I come from [place]. 저는 스물다섯 살이에요 (jeo-neun seu-mul-da-seot sa-ri-e-yo) — I am 25 years old. 만나서 반갑습니다 (man-na-seo ban-gap-seum-ni-da) — pleased to meet you.

Politeness words carry enormous weight. 감사합니다 (gam-sa-ham-ni-da) is thank you — formal and safe everywhere; 고맙습니다 (go-map-seum-ni-da) is equally polite and slightly warmer. 고마워요 (go-ma-wo-yo) is the casual version for friends. 천만에요 (cheon-ma-ne-yo) is you're welcome. 죄송합니다 (joe-song-ham-ni-da) is I'm sorry for a real mistake; 실례합니다 (sil-lye-ham-ni-da) is excuse me to get attention or to pass by. A frequent beginner mistake is using 반말 (casual speech) with strangers because it sounds easier — with anyone older or unfamiliar, stay in the polite 요/습니다 forms this course teaches.

Survival phrases are your safety net — memorize these before anything else. 잘 모르겠어요 (jal mo-reu-ge-sseo-yo) — I don't understand well. 천천히 말씀해 주세요 (cheon-cheo-ni mal-sseum-hae ju-se-yo) — please speak slowly. 영어 하세요? (yeong-eo ha-se-yo? — do you speak English?). 화장실이 어디예요? (hwa-jang-si-ri eo-di-ye-yo? — where is the bathroom?). 도와주세요 (do-wa-ju-se-yo) — please help me. 다시 한 번 말씀해 주세요 (da-shi han beon mal-sseum-hae ju-se-yo) — please say it once more. These six phrases will rescue you anywhere in Korea, and people respect learners who ask for clarification instead of nodding blankly.

Two cultural notes that prevent real mistakes. First, bowing accompanies greetings — a small nod with 안녕하세요 is natural; a deep bow is for formal apologies or elders. Second, Koreans often ask age early in conversation (몇 살이에요? — how old are you?) — it is not rude; age determines speech levels and social roles, so answer cheerfully.

Full dialogue — a first meeting. Read aloud with romanization and translations:

Jiwon: 안녕하세요! 처음 뵙겠습니다. (An-nyeong-ha-se-yo! Cheo-eum boep-ge-sseum-ni-da.) — Hello! Nice to meet you.
Minjun: 안녕하세요! 저는 민준입니다. (An-nyeong-ha-se-yo! Jeo-neun Min-jun-im-ni-da.) — Hello! I am Minjun.
Jiwon: 저는 지원입니다. 미국에서 왔어요. (Jeo-neun Ji-won-im-ni-da. Mi-gu-ge-seo wa-sseo-yo.) — I am Jiwon. I come from America.
Minjun: 아, 그렇군요. 만나서 반갑습니다. (A, geu-reo-kun-yo. Man-na-seo ban-gap-seum-ni-da.) — Ah, I see. Pleased to meet you.
Jiwon: 한국어를 잘 못해요. 천천히 말씀해 주세요. (Han-gu-geo-reul jal mo-tae-yo. Cheon-cheo-ni mal-sseum-hae ju-se-yo.) — I'm not good at Korean. Please speak slowly.
Minjun: 네, 알겠습니다. (Ne, al-ge-sseum-ni-da.) — Yes, understood.
Jiwon: 감사합니다! 안녕히 가세요. (Gam-sa-ham-ni-da! An-nyeong-hi ga-se-yo.) — Thank you! Goodbye (you're leaving).

Practice: Write a self-introduction of five lines (greeting, name, where you are from, your age or job, nice to meet you) and memorize it — this is your personal script for the next month. Then drill the survival phrases: cover the Korean, read the English, and say the Korean from memory until you can do all six without peeking. Finally, role-play the dialogue above twice, switching roles, saying every line aloud with correct syllable blocks.
