This lesson turns you into someone who can get answers: how to use every question word, ask for directions, handle a full shopping conversation, and keep small talk going. Up to now you have mostly made statements; questions are what make you independent in Korea. The good news: Korean questions keep statement word order — you just swap in a question word where the answer goes, with rising intonation.

The core question words: 뭐/무엇 (mwo/mu-eot, what — 뭐 is casual speech, 무엇 is the full form), 어디 (eo-di, where), 누구 (nu-gu, who), 언제 (eon-je, when), 어떻게 (eo-tteo-ke, how), 얼마 (eol-ma, how much), 왜 (wae, why), 몇 (myeot, how many — before counters). They sit where the answer would be: 이게 뭐예요? (i-ge mwo-ye-yo? — what is this?), 어디에 가요? (eo-di-e ga-yo? — where are you going?), 누구예요? (nu-gu-ye-yo? — who is it?), 언제 와요? (eon-je wa-yo? — when are you coming?). Question words take 이/가, never 은/는: 누가 왔어요? (nu-ga wa-sseo-yo? — who came?) — saying 누가는 is the classic beginner error. 몇 pairs with counters: 몇 개? (myeot gae? — how many things?), 몇 살이에요? (myeot sa-ri-e-yo? — how old are you?).

Directions vocabulary is survival-level. ...이/가 어디예요? (...i/ga eo-di-ye-yo? — where is...?): 역이 어디예요? (yeo-gi eo-di-ye-yo? — where is the station?). 어떻게 가요? (eo-tteo-ke ga-yo? — how do I get there?). 오른쪽 (o-reun-jjok, right), 왼쪽 (oen-jjok, left), 직진 (jik-jin, straight ahead), 가까워요 (ga-kka-wo-yo, it's near), 멀어요 (meo-reo-yo, it's far), 사거리 (sa-geo-ri, intersection), 신호등 (sin-ho-deung, traffic light). If you are lost: 길을 잃었어요 (gi-reul i-reo-sseo-yo) — I'm lost. The lifesaver follow-up: 지도에서 보여 주세요 (ji-do-e-seo bo-yeo ju-se-yo) — please show me on the map. When someone answers, listen for 오른쪽으로 가세요 (go to the right) and 왼쪽으로 도세요 (turn left).

Shopping questions beyond prices: ...있어요? (...i-sseo-yo? — do you have...?): 큰 사이즈 있어요? (keun sa-i-jeu i-sseo-yo? — do you have a large size?). 입어 봐도 돼요? (i-beo bwa-do dwae-yo? — may I try it on?). 카드 돼요? (ka-deu dwae-yo? — can I pay by card?). 몇 시에 열어요? (myeot shi-e yeo-reo-yo? — what time do you open?). 깎아 주세요 (kka-kka ju-se-yo) — please give me a discount. Small-talk questions that keep conversations alive: 어디에서 왔어요? (eo-di-e-seo wa-sseo-yo? — where did you come from?), 무슨 일을 하세요? (mu-seun i-reul ha-se-yo? — what work do you do?), 여기 온 지 얼마나 됐어요? (yeo-gi on ji eol-ma-na dwae-sseo-yo? — how long have you been here?), 이 도시가 마음에 들어요? (i do-shi-ga ma-eu-me deu-reo-yo? — do you like this city?), 뭐가 맛있어요? (mwo-ga ma-shi-sseo-yo? — what is delicious here?).

Dialogue 1 — asking directions:

Tourist: 실례합니다. 박물관이 어디예요? (Sil-lye-ham-ni-da. Bang-mul-gwa-ni eo-di-ye-yo?) — Excuse me. Where is the museum?
Local: 가까워요. 직진하시고 신호등에서 왼쪽으로 도세요. (Ga-kka-wo-yo. Jik-jin-ha-shi-go sin-ho-deung-e-seo oen-jjo-geu-ro do-se-yo.) — It's near. Go straight and turn left at the traffic light.
Tourist: 걸어서 멀어요? (Geo-reo-seo meo-reo-yo?) — Is it far on foot?
Local: 아니요, 오 분쯤 걸려요. 은행하고 약국 사이에 있어요. (A-ni-yo, o bun-jjeum geol-lyeo-yo. Eun-haeng-ha-go yak-guk sa-i-e i-sseo-yo.) — No, about five minutes on foot. It's between the bank and the pharmacy.
Tourist: 감사합니다! (Gam-sa-ham-ni-da!) — Thank you!

Dialogue 2 — small talk at a café:

Hana: 어디에서 왔어요? (Eo-di-e-seo wa-sseo-yo?) — Where did you come from?
Jun: 부산에서 왔어요. 하나 씨는요? (Bu-sa-ne-seo wa-sseo-yo. Ha-na sshi-neun-yo?) — I came from Busan. And you, Hana?
Hana: 저는 서울 사람이에요. 여기 온 지 얼마나 됐어요? (Jeo-neun Seo-ul sa-ra-mi-e-yo. Yeo-gi on ji eol-ma-na dwae-sseo-yo?) — I'm from Seoul. How long have you been here?
Jun: 두 주 됐어요. 서울이 정말 좋아요. (Du ju dwae-sseo-yo. Seo-u-ri jeong-mal jo-a-yo.) — Two weeks. I really like Seoul.
Hana: 뭐가 제일 좋아요? (Mwo-ga je-il jo-a-yo?) — What do you like most?
Jun: 음식이요! 내일 공원에 갈 거예요. 같이 가실래요? (Eum-shi-gi-yo! Nae-il gong-wo-ne gal geo-ye-yo. Ga-chi ga-shil-lae-yo?) — The food! I'm going to the park tomorrow. Want to come along?
Hana: 좋아요! 몇 시에 만날까요? (Jo-a-yo! Myeot shi-e man-nal-kka-yo?) — Sounds good! What time shall we meet?
Jun: 열 시에 여기서 만나요. (Yeol shi-e yeo-gi-seo man-na-yo.) — Let's meet here at ten.

Practice: Write ten questions — two each for directions, shopping, time, personal info, and opinions — and say each aloud with rising intonation. Memorize the direction sequence (직진, 오른쪽, 왼쪽, 사거리) and give directions from your front door to the nearest shop, in Korean, out loud. Finally, role-play Dialogue 2 with changed details (your city, your time, your plan) until the question patterns feel automatic.
