This lesson gives you the past tense so you can say what you did, where you went, and what happened. Without a past tense you are stuck in the eternal present; with it, you can tell stories, explain your day, and answer the most common small-talk question in any language: 어제 뭐 했어요? (eo-je mwo hae-sseo-yo? — what did you do yesterday?). Korean past tense follows the same vowel rule you learned for the present.

Form the past by adding 았어요/었어요 to the verb stem: if the stem's last vowel is ㅏ or ㅗ, add 았어요; otherwise add 었어요; 하다 verbs become 했어요. 가다 → 갔어요 (ga-sseo-yo, went). 먹다 → 먹었어요 (meo-geo-sseo-yo, ate). 보다 → 봤어요 (bwa-sseo-yo, saw). 오다 → 왔어요 (wa-sseo-yo, came). 하다 → 했어요 (hae-sseo-yo, did). 사다 → 샀어요 (sa-sseo-yo, bought). 자다 → 잤어요 (ja-sseo-yo, slept). 읽다 → 읽었어요 (il-geo-sseo-yo, read). Feel the pattern — it mirrors the 아요/어요 rule exactly, so the present lesson does double duty. The formal past is 았습니다/었습니다: 갔습니다, 먹었습니다.

The negative past has two forms. Use 안 + past: 안 갔어요 (an ga-sseo-yo) — I didn't go. Or the past of 지 않다: 가지 않았어요 (ga-ji a-na-sseo-yo). Both are natural; 안 is shorter and more common in speech. For "couldn't," use 못 + past: 못 잤어요 (mot ja-sseo-yo) — I couldn't sleep.

Adjectives conjugate the same way: 예쁘다 → 예뻤어요 (ye-ppeo-sseo-yo, was pretty). 좋다 → 좋았어요 (jo-a-sseo-yo, was good). 크다 → 컸어요 (keo-sseo-yo, was big). Two stars of past-tense conversation: 좋았어요 (it was good) and 재미있었어요 (jae-mi-i-sseo-sseo-yo, it was fun/interesting) — you will use these constantly when talking about your weekend.

Time words anchor the past: 어제 (eo-je, yesterday), 그저께 (geu-jeo-kke, the day before yesterday), 지난주 (ji-nan-ju, last week), 지난달 (ji-nan-dal, last month), 작년 (jang-nyeon, last year), 아까 (a-kka, a little while ago). Combine them with the past tense: 어제 영화를 봤어요 (eo-je yeong-hwa-reul bwa-sseo-yo) — I watched a movie yesterday. 지난주에 서울에 갔어요 (ji-nan-ju-e Seo-u-re ga-sseo-yo) — I went to Seoul last week. To list several past actions, chain them with -고: 영화를 보고 쇼핑했어요 (yeong-hwa-reul bo-go syo-ping-hae-sseo-yo) — I watched a movie and went shopping.

Two beginner warnings. First, don't use the past tense for states still true now: if you still live in Busan, say 부산에 살아요, not 살았어요. The past means finished and over. Second, watch the vowel rule on irregular stems: 크다 (to be big) contracts to 컸어요, not 크었어요 — vowel-contracting verbs (크다, 트다) drop the stem vowel. When in doubt, memorize the common ones: 컸어요 (was big), 좋았어요 (was good), 봤어요 (saw).

Dialogue — talking about the weekend:

Dae: 주말에 뭐 했어요? (Ju-ma-re mwo hae-sseo-yo?) — What did you do on the weekend?
Rin: 토요일에 경복궁에 갔어요. (To-yo-i-re gyeong-bok-gung-e ga-sseo-yo.) — I went to Gyeongbokgung Palace on Saturday.
Dae: 어땠어요? 재미있었어요? (Eo-ttae-sseo-yo? Jae-mi-i-sseo-sseo-yo?) — How was it? Was it fun?
Rin: 네, 정말 재미있었어요! 사진도 많이 찍었어요. (Ne, jeong-mal jae-mi-i-sseo-sseo-yo! Sa-jin-do ma-ni jji-geo-sseo-yo.) — Yes, really fun! I took lots of photos too.
Dae: 좋았겠네요. 저는 집에서 영화를 봤어요. (Jo-a-kke-nne-yo. Jeo-neun ji-be-seo yeong-hwa-reul bwa-sseo-yo.) — Sounds great. I watched a movie at home.
Rin: 영화 재미있었어요? (Yeong-hwa jae-mi-i-sseo-sseo-yo?) — Was the movie fun?
Dae: 네, 정말 좋았어요. 일요일에는 장을 봤어요. (Ne, jeong-mal jo-a-sseo-yo. I-ryo-i-re-neun jang-eul bwa-sseo-yo.) — Yes, really good. On Sunday I did the grocery shopping.

Practice: Write a paragraph of 8–10 sentences about what you did yesterday, using at least five different past-tense verbs plus one adjective past. Read it aloud twice. Then convert these to the past: 마시다, 사다, 읽다, 예쁘다, 좋다. Finally, answer aloud in complete Korean sentences: 어제 뭐 먹었어요? 몇 시에 일어났어요? 지난 주말에 뭐 했어요? 한국에 가 본 적 있어요?
