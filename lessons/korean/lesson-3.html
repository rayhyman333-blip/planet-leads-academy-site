This lesson teaches the sentence patterns behind most Korean conversation: particles that mark each word's role, the present polite verb form, and subject-object-verb word order. Korean puts the verb last — always — and particles, not word order, tell you who did what to whom. Master this lesson and you can build thousands of correct sentences.

The basic order is subject-object-verb: 저는 밥을 먹어요 (jeo-neun ba-beul meo-geo-yo) — I eat rice. 저는 물을 마셔요 (jeo-neun mu-reul ma-syeo-yo) — I drink water. 저는 학교에 가요 (jeo-neun hak-gyo-e ga-yo) — I go to school. Time words go before the verb: 저는 내일 가요 (jeo-neun nae-il ga-yo) — I go tomorrow.

Particles are the grammar's engine. 은/는 (eun/neun) marks the topic — what the sentence is about: 저는 학생이에요 (jeo-neun hak-saeng-i-e-yo) — as for me, I am a student. Use 는 after vowels (저는), 은 after consonants (이름은 — i-reu-meun, as for the name). 이/가 (i/ga) marks the subject of a specific action or description: 비가 와요 (bi-ga wa-yo) — rain comes (it's raining). 누가 왔어요? (nu-ga wa-sseo-yo? — who came?). 을/를 (eul/reul) marks the object: 밥을 먹어요 (ba-beul meo-geo-yo), 를 after vowels (사과를 — sa-gwa-reul). 에 (e) marks destination or time: 학교에 가요 (to school), 세 시에 만나요 (se shi-e man-na-yo — meet at 3 o'clock). 에서 (e-seo) marks where an action happens or where you come from: 집에서 먹어요 (ji-be-seo meo-geo-yo — I eat at home), 미국에서 왔어요 (I came from America). 와/과 (wa/gwa) means "and/with": 친구와 갔어요 (chin-gu-wa ga-sseo-yo — I went with a friend). 도 (do) means "also": 저도 가요 (jeo-do ga-yo — I go too). 의 (ui) shows possession: 제 이름 (je i-reum — my name).

Present-tense verbs in the polite 요 form follow one rule. Take the verb stem (가다 → 가): if the stem's last vowel is ㅏ or ㅗ, add 아요; otherwise add 어요; 하다 verbs become 해요. 가다 (to go) → 가요. 먹다 (to eat) → 먹어요. 보다 (to see) → 봐요. 오다 (to come) → 와요. 하다 (to do) → 해요. 있다 (to be/exist) → 있어요. 없다 (not exist) → 없어요. Learn these eight deeply — they appear everywhere. 저는 영화를 봐요 (jeo-neun yeong-hwa-reul bwa-yo) — I watch movies. Negative with 안: 안 가요 (an ga-yo) — I don't go. Or with 지 않다: 가지 않아요 (ga-ji a-na-yo). "Cannot" uses 못: 못 가요 (mot ga-yo) — I can't go.

입니다/입니까 handles "is" sentences: 저는 학생입니다 (I am a student). 이것은 책입니다 (i-geo-seun chae-gim-ni-da — this is a book). Question: 학생입니까? (are you a student?). In the 요 form: 학생이에요 / 학생이에요? Adjectives work like verbs in Korean — they conjugate: 예쁘다 (to be pretty) → 예뻐요. 크다 (to be big) → 커요. 좋다 (to be good) → 좋아요. 저는 행복해요 (jeo-neun haeng-bo-kae-yo) — I am happy.

Two beginner mistakes to avoid. First, dropping particles because English doesn't need them — 밥 먹어요 is understandable but wrong; 밥을 먹어요 is correct. Second, putting the verb anywhere but last. English habits produce 저는 먹어요 밥을 — always rearrange to 저는 밥을 먹어요. The verb is the anchor at the end.

Dialogue — coworkers introducing themselves:

Sujin: 처음 뵙겠습니다. 저는 수진입니다. 회사원이에요. (Cheo-eum boep-ge-sseum-ni-da. Jeo-neun Su-jin-im-ni-da. Hoe-sa-wo-ni-e-yo.) — Nice to meet you. I am Sujin. I am an office worker.
Taehyun: 처음 뵙겠습니다. 저는 태현입니다. 학생이에요. (Cheo-eum boep-ge-sseum-ni-da. Jeo-neun Tae-hyeon-im-ni-da. Hak-saeng-i-e-yo.) — Nice to meet you. I am Taehyun. I am a student.
Sujin: 매일 지하철로 회사에 가요. (Mae-il ji-ha-cheol-lo hoe-sa-e ga-yo.) — I go to the office by subway every day.
Taehyun: 저는 걸어서 학교에 가요. (Jeo-neun geo-reo-seo hak-gyo-e ga-yo.) — I walk to school.
Sujin: 학교가 멀어요? (Hak-gyo-ga meo-reo-yo?) — Is the school far?
Taehyun: 아니요, 가까워요. 아주 편해요. (A-ni-yo, ga-kka-wo-yo. A-ju pyeo-nae-yo.) — No, it's close. Very convenient.

Practice: Write ten 입니다/이에요 sentences about yourself (name, job, origin). Write eight verb sentences using the eight verbs above, each with a particle (을/를, 에, or 에서). Say every sentence aloud. Finally, translate to Korean: "I drink coffee every day. I go to Seoul by subway. This book is interesting (재미있다)." Check: 저는 매일 커피를 마셔요. 지하철로 서울에 가요. 이 책은 재미있어요.
