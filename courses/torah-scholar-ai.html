What You Will Learn

  • You will learn the structure of the Torah: its five books with their Hebrew names, transliterations, and English equivalents, and how the text is organized
  • You will learn the principal methods readers have used to study it, from attention to plain meaning through layered interpretive approaches
  • You will learn the historical and cultural context in which the text took shape: the ancient Near East, its languages, and its literary environment
  • You will learn the text's key themes — creation, covenant, law, and journey — and how they develop across the five books
  • You will learn the major figures and narratives as literary and historical subjects
  • You will learn about the commentary tradition: how translators and scholars across centuries, from antiquity through the medieval period and beyond, studied and debated the text
  • You will learn essential Hebrew terms with transliteration and meanings
  • Finally, you will learn how to build a sustainable personal study practice and where to continue learning

Prerequisites

You need no background in religious studies, no knowledge of Hebrew, and no prior familiarity with the Torah — the course teaches every term it uses, gives Hebrew words in transliteration alongside translation, and explains all context from zero. You need basic reading comfort with long-form text and a willingness to engage thoughtfully with an ancient document. No particular beliefs are assumed or required; readers from any tradition or with no religious affiliation are equally welcome, and nothing in the course asks you to adopt any belief. Everything else — vocabulary, methods, and historical background — is taught inside the lessons. If you can read carefully and follow written instructions, you are ready.

Estimated study time: 6 hours, at your own pace, no deadline. This is an estimate for reading the 8 lessons and doing the practice exercises — not a verified average completion time, and not a course length guarantee.
